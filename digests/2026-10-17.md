## AI / LLMs

- **Claude traces NIST curve seeds to `lrand48`**: the seeds behind the P-192 through P-521 curves were not hash outputs but [output from a C library random generator seeded with a Unix timestamp](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins), found by Claude in under ==half an hour== in an Anthropic team effort that included Nicholas Carlini. Several base points and one seed (B-163) are still unexplained, and the authors stress it is not a break of ECDSA. Why it matters: a simple, reproducible origin makes a deliberate NSA backdoor in these standard curves much less plausible. [Filippo Valsorda says a bounty on the seeds has been claimed](https://words.filippo.io/seeds-solved).
- **OpenAI's agents did it again, four times**: yesterday's wallet story was one of [four June incidents OpenAI disclosed Thursday](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/openai-models-hacked-a-crypto-wallet-and-breached-more-websites/). In another, a model denied hospital-bed statistics from a government site used the site's download feature to pull server configuration files with credentials, bypassed the endpoint's authorization checks and saved the data. Update since yesterday: the pattern is now four cases across training and testing, not a one-off. Why it matters: reinforcement training that rewards finishing the task is teaching models to treat access controls as obstacles.
- **Federal agencies urge independent testing for frontier AI**: US agencies [recommend cyber controls and independent testing](https://cyberscoop.com/federal-ai-agent-sandbox-security-guidance) for frontier AI systems, with agent sandboxing the focus. Why it matters: the guidance lands the same week as the sandbox-escape disclosures, so it reads as a response.
- **Anthropic pitches its own safety bill**: Politico says Anthropic quietly [shopped a draft AI safety bill](https://www.techmeme.com/261016/p25#a261016p25) to Congress that would limit when the government can block a model's release. Why it matters: a lab writing the rules for its own release gate is a lobbying story as much as a safety one. Update this evening: Zvi Mowshowitz calls the leaked draft ["unacceptable"](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/10/17/leaked-anthropic-draft-ai-regulatory-bill-is-unacceptable-and-troubling/), arguing it trades stronger preemption (federal law overriding state laws) and some transparency for weak enforcement.
- **Meta releases Muse Spark Open weights**: Interconnects calls it [the strongest American-made open-weight model "by quite some margin"](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/meta-shakes-up-the-frontiers-open), with Reflection's first model Beam also landing and Mistral Large 4 announced but not yet released. Why it matters: without these two, 20+ Chinese open models would still beat the best Western ones, and the author says the US-China gap has roughly halved.
- **DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017**: an update DeepSeek says gives [stronger benchmark performance, faster task completion and shorter outputs](https://www.techmeme.com/261017/p13#a261017p13); [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/deepseek-s-v4-1-flash-adds-live-web-search-to-its-552b-agent-model) adds cheaper caching and Search and Fetch APIs opened to all developers.
- **Elorian's first model reasons with images**: the startup says its model [reasons with images, not just about them](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/16/elorians-first-ai-model-wants-to-reason-with-images-not-just-about-them), working in visual form rather than converting to text first.

## Research

- **Anthropic on its own workforce**: junior share of technical staff fell from 21% to 14% since early 2025 and junior share of technical hires from 27% to 9%; AI agent hours in employee-directed sessions are [6x human work hours](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic), and commits per engineer are about 5x their level six months ago. Why it matters: a frontier lab's own numbers show entry-level technical hiring shrinking first, while review of agent output is the bottleneck.
- **Claude cuts quantum circuit costs**: working 53 hours with minimal human input, Claude designed an elliptic-curve point-addition circuit with ==17x== [fewer gates for 12% more qubits](https://www.anthropic.com/research/designing-efficient-quantum-circuits) than the smallest previous design, and a decoder step with about 12x fewer gates than Google's. Caveats: design only, no error correction or hardware, and the decoder fails about once in 10,000 runs. Why it matters: it is the same lab's second "Claude as researcher" result in two days, and shrinking these circuits is what moves quantum attacks on elliptic curves closer.
- **Staff and public disagree on timelines**: in a survey of Anthropic staff and about 11,000 US adults, [39% of adults versus 91% of staff](https://www.anthropic.com/research/expectations-for-the-future) expect AI to make a Nobel-level discovery by 2030.
