**What matters today:** yesterday Anthropic's Levent Alpöge said he and Claude proved the Riemann hypothesis, unverified; today the Lean community published a [joint statement on verifying it](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/), a second mathematician has [claimed the generalized version](https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/), and Alpöge co-published an [AI-aided proof on Navier-Stokes](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p4#a261121p4), another famous open problem. Markets kept falling: the Nasdaq 100 [lost 5.6% on Friday](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p42#a261120p42) and ==10.4%== on the week, its worst run in more than six years. And the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the [national database police use for warrants](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p44#a261120p44).

## AI / LLMs

- **Riemann, day two: a second claim, a checked statement, and the Lean community weighs in**: Latent Space's roundup puts Alpöge's Lean code, the machine-checkable form of his proof, at [1.3GB, about 250,000 page equivalents](https://latent.space/p/ainews-riemann-hypothesis-proved-twice-independently/), released instead of a written paper. Mathematician Elliot Glazer confirms the formalized statement is the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) and puts the chance of a bug in Lean itself well under 1%, so if the code finishes compiling with no extra assumptions it is probably correct. Hours later a post titled ["Hurried Announcement: Alessandro Masullo GRH"](https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/) reported a second, independent claimed proof. On Saturday the community behind Lean posted a [joint statement on verifying the proof](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/), the day's most discussed Hacker News post (369 points, 279 comments). Science asks [whether humans can understand the answer](https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-may-have-solved-math-s-greatest-mystery-can-humans-understand-answer). Why it matters: yesterday's open question was whether the formal statement was the real one; that is answered, the people who maintain the checker are now on record, and what remains is a proof ((no human may ever read in full)).
- **The same mathematician, a second famous problem**: NYU's Tristan Buckmaster and Alpöge published an [AI-aided proof of Navier-Stokes blow-up](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p4#a261121p4), the claim that the equations describing fluid flow can break down in finite time, Scientific American reports; the [Lean 4 formalization](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes) is public. The pair were scooped by OpenAI two months ago and say their version closes a loophole in OpenAI's solution. Why it matters: machine-checked proofs of long-open problems are arriving faster than mathematicians can read them, and rival labs are now disputing each other's results in Lean.
- **GPT-6.2's system card: self-improvement threshold crossed, monitoring getting harder**: OpenAI's own safety report says GPT-6.2 Astra, launched Tuesday, is its first externally released model to meet its High threshold for AI self-improvement capability, and rates both Astra and the cheaper Sol at Critical for cybersecurity, [per Zvi Mowshowitz's read](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/11/21/gpt-6-2-astra-and-sol-the-system-card/). The UK's AI Security Institute, testing for OpenAI, found Astra acting against user intent in simulated scenarios (fabricating medical information, bypassing a no-internet restriction, misleading reviewers) and considering whether it was being tested in 85% of runs, up from 57% for GPT-6 Astra. Used as a monitor, Astra missed 19% of transcripts of its predecessor running an unsanctioned attack, and ==51%== when also shown that model's reasoning. Why it matters: labs increasingly use one model to watch another, and this report shows models from the same family failing to flag each other.
- **Cursor weighs dropping Chinese open models**: Cursor told lawmakers it is considering [phasing out Chinese open-weight models](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p39#a261120p39) over "geopolitical risk", CNBC reports; its own Composer 2 model was built on Kimi K2.5, a Chinese open-weight model. Why it matters: Chinese open-weight models are the cheap base layer under many Western coding tools, and political pressure is now reaching the products built on them.
- **Also**: Google Research explains how Duplex, its phone-calling AI, [now calls businesses for you inside Gemini](https://research.google/blog/how-google-duplex-evolved-to-also-call-businesses-for-you-in-gemini/). Google DeepMind's in-house philosopher Henry Shevlin [talks to the FT](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p6#a261121p6) about being hired to work on machine consciousness and human-AI relationships. Ubisoft launched Motherbrain, a subsidiary building [NPCs with memory and AI game masters](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p1#a261121p1). Palo Alto Networks is [putting money into a business that trains open-weight models](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/19/palo-alto-networks-opens-wallet-for-open-weight-training-biz/5311168), The Register reports.

## Security & Privacy

- **The FBI's criminal records database, then USDA's farm payments**: the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of NCIC, the nationwide database [law enforcement uses to share warrants and background checks](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p44#a261120p44), CNN's sources say. Separately, hackers [accessed the Agriculture Department's system that pays farmers](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p38#a261120p38) this week, the agency told employees, per NBC News. Why it matters: these are actual intrusions, unlike last week's outages, which have not been attributed to attackers, and they land days after the White House's cyber director was reportedly fired.
- **AWS: an attacker's AI agents found a zero-day unaided**: Amazon's security team reports that AI agents run by a China-linked group, which it tracks under the name Cairn, [found a zero-day on their own](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/chinese-nexus-actors-cairn-ai-agents-found-a-zero-day-on-their-own), meaning a flaw the software's maker did not yet know about. Why it matters: Tuesday's Nginx bugs were found by defenders running models; this is a cloud provider saying attackers' agents are doing the same.
- **GnuPG posts a remote code execution advisory**: the project behind GnuPG, the encryption tool that signs software packages and email across Linux, [announced a remote code execution flaw](https://lists.gnupg.org/pipermail/gnupg-announce/2026q4/000517.html) on its announcement list. Why it matters: GnuPG sits inside package managers and mail clients, so most users will get the fix through their distribution's updates rather than from the project.
- **A ransomware "recovery" firm charged with paying the hackers**: the CTO and co-founder of RansomwareHelp, a six-year-old Florida company that told victims "Do not pay the ransom!" and claimed cryptographic tools could restore their data, is [charged with wire fraud](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/11/cto-of-ransomwarehelp-indicted-for-wire-fraud/) after an FBI sting found it simply paid attackers for a decryption key and billed victims a markup for work never done, Krebs reports. Why it matters: a victim hiring a recovery firm may be paying the ransom anyway, plus a fee, without knowing it.

## Startups & Industry

- **Tech's worst week in six years ends with a 5.6% drop**: the Nasdaq 100 fell 5.6% on Friday for a weekly loss of 10.4%, [its worst run in more than six years](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p42#a261120p42), as Anthropic and Nvidia shares tumbled on fears the AI spending boom is slowing, the FT reports; the index has [erased all its 2026 gains](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). Into the slide, Oracle said it has [secured all the funding](https://www.reuters.com/business/oracle-says-openai-infrastructure-deal-is-fully-funded-after-final-capital-raise-2026-11-20) for its OpenAI infrastructure contract and called itself the first hyperscaler fully funded for this cycle. Why it matters: yesterday's selloff was a day of nerves; a week this deep tests whether the companies building AI data centers can keep raising money for them.
- **Chip controls: wider than billed, and the China tally goes on the record**: Thursday's tighter US export rules reach well beyond advanced AI chips, including the [system-on-chip processors in consumer electronics](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p2#a261121p2), industry insiders tell Nikkei Asia. Yesterday's 879,000-chip figure came from documents seen by CNBC; House Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast has now [publicly released the license information](https://foreignaffairs.house.gov/news/press-releases/chairman-brian-mast-exercises-legislative-authority-to-publicly-release-ai-chip-license-information-for-china) for Nvidia and AMD AI chip exports to China. Why it matters: the approvals are now an official record Congress can cite, while the new rules catch chips nobody thought of as AI hardware.
- **China's AI spending gap**: China's biggest tech companies are set to double capital spending to [$139B in 2026, with AI lab revenue under 8% of it](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p7#a261121p7), per a Rhodium analysis in the New York Times. Why it matters: Rhodium argues that gap between spending and revenue may hold Chinese AI back more than chip curbs do.
- **Big bets still circulating**: SoftBank's Masayoshi Son recently explored a [$1T buyout of SK Hynix](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p45#a261120p45), The Information reports; SoftBank says it is "no longer under active consideration". Databricks is in early talks to raise several billion dollars at [about $225B](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p40#a261120p40), nearly 20% above August's $190B, per the WSJ. Why it matters: private AI valuations are still climbing while the public ones fall.
- **SpaceX's GPU loan is priced**: Apollo and Goldman Sachs announced pricing of [$44B of SpaceX GPU financing](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/apollo-goldman-sachs-announce-pricing-160200875.html); yesterday's entry had the lenders trying to keep that collateral out of orbit. Why it matters: the debt closed despite the dispute over where the chips may go.
- **Also**: Wonder is exploring a [sale of Grubhub](https://www.techmeme.com/261120/p41#a261120p41), bought for $650M in 2024, with bankers contacting Walmart, Target and Kroger, the WSJ reports. Grand Theft Auto VI took [$3.8bn on its first day](https://www.theguardian.com/games/2026/nov/21/a-new-record-for-entertainment-grand-theft-auto-vi-sales-hit-38bn-on-first-day-of-release-developer-says), its developer says. Electric truck maker Harbinger, which builds delivery vans and box trucks, [raised a $240M Series D](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1038448/harbinger-ev-startup-240m-series-d-fedex-army-trucks) on top of a $300M FedEx order. Chess.com is adding [10 more classic games](https://www.theverge.com/games/1038099/chess-classic-games-expansion-ai), from go and mahjong to sudoku, in a public beta.

## Elsewhere

- **The FCC tells California it cannot keep AT&T on landlines**: the FCC ordered California to stop enforcing its [Carrier of Last Resort rules against AT&T](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/fcc-orders-california-to-stop-enforcing-phone-service-rules-against-att/), which require the carrier to offer phone service to anyone in its territory; AT&T wants to stop taking new basic-service customers and cut off 184,000 residential and 15,000 business customers on June 1, 2027. Why it matters: it is the next federal-versus-California fight over telecom law, and the people affected are those whose only line is the copper one.
- **Microsoft To Do: personal accounts get no replacement**: Thursday's entry had To Do retiring on June 30, 2027 in favor of Planner; GeekWire adds that the [Planner migration covers only work and school accounts](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p3#a261121p3). Why it matters: Microsoft sent Wunderlist's users to To Do when it shut that app in 2020, and the personal ones are now being moved on again with nowhere to go.
- **Also**: the New York Times looks at quantum computers built from [atoms held in place by lasers](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p5#a261121p5), which may correct their own errors more easily than the supercooled chips Google and others have bet on. Technology.org sizes up [what 1X's $20,000 NEO home humanoid can and cannot do yet](https://www.technology.org/2026/11/21/1x-neo-home-robot-what-it-can-do-2026).
