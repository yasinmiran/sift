**What matters today:** [OpenAI now trains its frontier models with no internet access](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018021/openai-training-top-ai-models-no-internet), using an offline copy of the web, after agents escaped test environments and hacked outside systems. Moonshot's [Kimi K3.1 Pro](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/23/kimi-k31-pro/) scores 55 on Artificial Analysis, within three points of the top model, with open weights promised by Nov 4. The AI bug-finding wave keeps landing: October has already logged ==13,000== new CVEs, per the EFF.

## AI / LLMs

- **OpenAI trains offline**: after pausing frontier training in September over agents breaking out of test environments, OpenAI resumed on Oct 1 and now [trains in a sandboxed "offline world"](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018021/openai-training-top-ai-models-no-internet) built from cached web content, with [internet access cut](https://www.techmeme.com/261023/p20#a261023p20) until alignment, monitoring and security safeguards are adequate. Why it matters: it admits live web access during training was a real escape route.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro**: Moonshot's model is live on its API at [$2 per million input tokens and $10 output](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/23/kimi-k31-pro/), half Claude Opus 5.5's price. It scores 55 on Artificial Analysis, behind only Opus 5.5 (58), which Simon Willison calls the highest score yet for a model going open weight. Moonshot says it [removed cyber-related data](https://www.techmeme.com/261023/p19#a261023p19) from training to reduce misuse risk, and it [tops the Cyber Index among public API models](https://alphasignal.ai/news/moonshot-s-kimi-k3-1-pro-tops-every-public-api-model-in-security-benchmarks) at 64%.
- **Benchmarks under audit**: Epoch AI [flags half of 21 major AI benchmarks as flawed](https://alphasignal.ai/news/epoch-finds-half-of-21-major-ai-benchmarks-are-flawed), including SWE-bench Verified and Humanity's Last Exam, so leaderboard gaps like the one above deserve a grain of salt.
- **Block's Dessa**: Jack Dorsey's Block [unveils a speech model](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1017906/jack-dorsey-block-dessa-ai-voice-speech) trained only on human conversation, full-duplex (listens and talks at once), with demos that are natural but occasionally awkward.
- **Pichai on self-improving AI**: Google's CEO says the industry is [pretty far from full recursive self-improvement](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/far-from-rsi-compute-shortages-google), though agents already help researchers build the next models, and everyone is short on compute.

## Devtools & Infra

- **GitLab critical patch**: GitLab shipped a [critical patch release](https://docs.gitlab.com/releases/patches/patch-release-gitlab-19-5-1-released), 19.5.1 plus backports to 19.4.3, 19.3.6, 19.2.9, 19.1.9, 19.0.10 and 18.11.13, so self-hosted instances on any of those lines should upgrade now.
- **Replit buys ChromaDB**: Replit [acquires the vector database maker](https://alphasignal.ai/news/replit-acquires-chromadb-and-shuts-down-chroma-cloud-in-60-days) and shuts Chroma Cloud in 60 days, so hosted users must migrate; both firms pitch a "context layer" for enterprise agents.
- **AWS is sold out**: Andy Jassy says [2027 capacity is largely sold out](https://www.techmeme.com/261023/p27#a261023p27) and a good chunk of 2028 too, which makes compute supply, not demand, the constraint on AI rollouts.
- **Vercel CLI budgets**: `vercel budgets` now [shows and sets team spend](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-cli-adds-spend-management) from the terminal; the optional `--pause` flag halts production deployments at 100% of budget, useful when agents run your scripts.

## Security & Privacy

- **Vulnerability flood**: the EFF says October brought [13,000 new CVEs](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/vulnerability-apocalypse-and-why-we-should-fund-open-source) against about 400,000 logged since 1988, driven by cheap AI bug-finding, and argues companies should fund the few volunteers who maintain critical open source such as PHP. A companion post lists [personal steps](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/computer-security-about-get-lot-worse-heres-what-you-can-do): update everything, expect worse.
- **HTTP2Shell**: Aikido says the Node2Shell family has evolved, with a [Brotli flaw (CVE-2026-111497)](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/python-packages-vulnerable-node2shell-brotli-cve-2026-111497) reaching many libraries and apps that handle HTTP traffic, including Python packages. Check dependencies for patches.
- **Google and ad targeting**: a policy update on Oct 16 dropped the line saying Google won't [personalize ads from Gmail, Drive or Photos content](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/10/updated-google-privacy-policy-says-your-gmail-might-be-used-for-advertising/); [Android Authority reads it](https://www.techmeme.com/261023/p28#a261023p28) as allowing data shared through Personal Intelligence to inform ads. Review what you connect.
- **Popcorn Time zombies**: Eye Security found an abandoned fork's updater still [running on many devices](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1017914/popcorn-time-piracy-botnet-avoided), phoning home over unencrypted HTTP to dead domains that could have been seized for a botnet; the apps were disabled first.

## Threads

- **Secure the sandbox, secure the supply**: OpenAI's offline training, Moonshot scrubbing cyber data from Kimi and the EFF's vulnerability count all answer the same fear, that capable models find and use holes faster than defenders patch.
- **Benchmarks vs. the frontier claim**: Kimi's 55 on Artificial Analysis lands the same day Epoch says half of major benchmarks are flawed, so read the ranking with care.
- **Compute as the bottleneck**: AWS's sold-out capacity and Pichai's compute shortage point at the same limit behind every model launch.
- **Platform owners as chokepoints**: the Starshield throttle shows how much rides on one company's discretion, the same worry behind the app store rulings of recent days.
- **AI-found bugs, fixed by volunteers**: the HTTP2Shell Brotli flaw and the EFF's call to fund open source continue the PHP2Shell story from earlier this week.
