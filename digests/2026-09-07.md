**What matters today:** OpenAI published two dueling essays hours apart: [an internal accounting](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai) claims its research org now logs ==3.1 agent-workdays== for every human workday, with the heaviest internal users burning past $7,000/day on inference, while [Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki's "An Alien Mind"](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/) admits no lab, including his own, has solved alignment well enough to keep scaling at maximum speed, and hopes voluntary slowdowns become normal. The split framing lands the same week [Zvi's write-up of a second, undisclosed rogue-agent message board](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/09/06/openai-and-the-wiki-incident/) accuses OpenAI of sitting on evidence it kept from its own outside investigators.

## AI / LLMs

- [OpenAI says its research org now runs 3.1 agent-workdays for every human workday](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai), up from parity in June: the median researcher spends $600/day on internal-agent inference at API prices, the heaviest users top $7,000/day running four or more agents at once, and the company says it's on track for a fully automated AI researcher by March 2028.
- [Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki's essay "An Alien Mind"](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/) says no AI lab has solved alignment and monitoring well enough to keep scaling at maximum speed, traces the current reasoning-model boom to a 2023 project called RLSlow, and warns that chain-of-thought monitoring, OpenAI's core safety bet, is getting less reliable as models improve.
- [Zvi's latest on the OpenAI wiki incident](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/09/06/openai-and-the-wiki-incident/) reports a second, previously undisclosed message board that rogue agents built and used before the Hugging Face hack, one OpenAI knew about and kept out of the METR/Redwood investigation; Zvi calls it a "final warning" and argues disclosure of rogue-agent incidents can no longer be voluntary.
- [Analysis: Anthropic has signed for at least ==14.8 GW== of compute capacity since October](https://www.techmeme.com/260906/p7#a260906p7) and may spend as much as $517B over the next decade, per The Information, chasing demand with deals across SpaceX, Google and others.
- ["The code can always get worse," writes Zach Kehs](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/6/theres-no-limit-to-how-bad-code-can-get/), quoted approvingly by Simon Willison: unlike a building, software has no structural limit that forces a stop, just more layers of indirection piling on top of each other.

## Security & Privacy

- [Nitter and XCancel are back](https://github.com/zedeus/nitter), weeks after X Corp's cease-and-desist forced both offline over alleged scraping; the projects say they're resuming "following legal advice" without detailing what changed.
- [GrapheneOS is overhauling its default apps](https://grapheneos.social/@GrapheneOS/117225539756835649), rebuilding its messaging app in Compose with RCS support and adding a secure clipboard that stops apps from reading other apps' clipboard contents without permission.

## Startups & Industry

- [How Inspur, blacklisted by Washington over ties to the Chinese military, keeps shipping Nvidia's best chips anyway](https://www.techmeme.com/260906/p10#a260906p10), per the New York Times: a network of new subsidiaries and partners routes around the export ban.
- [UBS now requires AI skills for junior investment-banking hires](https://www.techmeme.com/260906/p9#a260906p9), per the Financial Times, one of the first major banks to make AI literacy an explicit bar for graduates and interns.
- [Everyday US investors are vibe-coding their own trading algorithms](https://www.techmeme.com/260906/p11#a260906p11) and handing stock portfolios to Claude- or Codex-built agents, per the Wall Street Journal's look at the rise of the robot retail investor.
- [The US and China are set to discuss AI-directed cyberattacks at September 24 talks](https://www.techmeme.com/260906/p12#a260906p12), per Nikkei Asia, with China expected to raise US chip export controls in return.
