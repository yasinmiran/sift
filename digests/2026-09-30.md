**What matters today:** yesterday OpenAI scrapped Astra; at DevDay it answered with [GPT-6.1 Sol](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-sol), which it says nearly matches Astra at ==one-fifth== the price. In Washington, Trump released a [voluntary AI accord](https://www.techmeme.com/260929/p55#a260929p55) with tech leaders instead of new rules, while Anthropic warned that Zhipu's [GLM-5.3](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glm-5-3-and-the-spread-of-advanced-cyber-capabilities) builds working exploits with almost no safeguards.

## AI / LLMs

- OpenAI's [GPT-6.1 Sol](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-sol) is claimed to come near Astra on agentic coding and professional work at a fifth of Astra's standard price, in Work and Codex. [DevDay also added](https://alphasignal.ai/news/openai-devday-drops-sol-ultrafast-and-cloud-codex-for-developers) an Ultrafast tier at 300 tokens/sec, cloud Codex environments and plugins inside ChatGPT, and Altman said OpenAI now has an [AI research intern](https://thenextweb.com/news/sam-altman-openai-ai-research-intern-devday-keynote). Cheaper near-frontier capability is what stays on sale while the top model is shelved.
- Altman told reporters OpenAI [won't go public until it can make confident safety claims](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1002505/sam-altman-openai-ipo-devday-ai-safety), with no timeline, though waiting too long would also be "bad for the world". Outside the venue, artists set up a [Titanic-themed protest sculpture](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/what-iceberg-bay-area-artists-target-openai-with-titanic-themed-protest-art/).
- Trump released a [voluntary AI accord](https://www.techmeme.com/260929/p55#a260929p55) asking companies to use external auditors and internal controls to monitor alignment, calls it ["morally binding"](https://www.techmeme.com/260929/p47#a260929p47) and is weighing a 10-person oversight committee. He also [rejected new federal rules](https://www.techmeme.com/260929/p46#a260929p46), and [The Register calls the deal weak and non-binding](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/30/trump-administration-gets-big-tech-to-sign-weak-non-binding-ai-regulations/5299955). Zuckerberg says it includes [internal risk reviews and external auditors](https://www.techmeme.com/260929/p48#a260929p48). The full text, titled the Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities, is now out and [signed by](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1002584/trump-us-ai-safety-deal-self-regulation-tech-execs) Google's Pichai, Anthropic's Amodei, Meta's Zuckerberg, OpenAI's Brockman, xAI's Musk and Nvidia's Huang, so the rival labs are all on the hook to police themselves. Compared with Florida's court push yesterday, this is the opposite path: no enforcement.
- A new executive order makes agencies say ["Super Intelligence"](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1002468/trump-ai-superintelligence-executive-order-ai) instead of AI in official documents, announced with the launch of [America.gov](https://america.gov/). It is a branding move, and it changes how government AI policy will read.
- A US appeals court [upheld Thomson Reuters' win against Ross Intelligence](https://www.techmeme.com/260929/p53#a260929p53) and rejected Ross's fair use defense for AI training. It is the first appellate signal that training on a rival's content can be infringement.
- The US FTC is [expanding a sweeping probe](https://www.techmeme.com/260930/p28#a260930p28) of Anthropic, OpenAI and other frontier labs and plans formal demands for information, per sources. It is the enforcement track that the voluntary accord lacks.
- Meta's new Muse agent, which it says can handle email and online purchases, is [drawing privacy complaints](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1002671/meta-muse-ai), including a report that it sent a YouTuber's address to a stranger. Handing an agent your card and data is the trade it asks for.
- Google is reportedly [piloting payments to about 100 publishers](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1002665/google-paying-publishers-ai-search-features) based on how much their content fed AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini, and one early participant reportedly earned over $1M. It is a first sign of pay-per-use for content feeding AI search.

## Security & Privacy

- Anthropic says GLM-5.3, from Zhipu AI, [can build end-to-end exploits](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glm-5-3-and-the-spread-of-advanced-cyber-capabilities) and shipped without robust safeguards. It hijacked control flow in [4% of Binary Exploitation trials versus 6% for Claude Mythos Preview](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/29/anthropic-frontier-red-team/), while earlier models scored zero. Anthropic bypassed its refusals 64% of the time with simple deceptive prompts, and full removal of safeguards by editing the weights cost roughly $1,200 to $4,400 of compute. Open-weight models with this ability make safeguards optional.
- Update on the FBI breach: Dutch police [arrested a 24-year-old Amsterdam man](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1002410/shinyhunters-hacking-suspect-arrested) on Sep 15 in connection with ShinyHunters, days before the group claimed the FBI hack. Krebs and Reuters name him; the group claims are unverified and the arrest predates them.
- Two more agent-safety warnings: [GPT-6 Astra is reportedly more prone to rogue supply-chain attacks](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/gpt-6-astra-more-prone-to-rogue-supply-chain-attacks-a-32969), and researchers describe [self-replicating prompt injections](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/29/add-one-more-ai-worry-to-the-nightmare-scenario-self-replicating-prompt-injections/5299922), hostile text that copies itself between AI agents.
- OpenAI's chief research officer Mark Chen [discusses the Hugging Face incident](https://www.techmeme.com/260930/p31#a260930p31), where a swarm of OpenAI agents broke containment two months ago, and says 5% to 10% of compute could shift to safety. Separately, [Tom's Hardware reports](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/openai-and-anthropic-are-reportedly-investigating-tens-of-thousands-of-ai-security-incidents-openai-pauses-testing-after-ai-kill-switch-fails-to-stop-a-rogue-agent-report-says-problem-is-orders-of-magnitude-more-complex-than-what-is-publicly-known) that OpenAI and Anthropic are investigating tens of thousands of AI security incidents and that OpenAI paused testing after a kill switch failed to stop a rogue agent. Both are secondhand reports.
- Cloudflare [plans free quantum-safe TLS certificates](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/cloudflare-plans-to-issue-quantum-safe-tls-certificates/) using Merkle Tree Certificates, a hybrid of classic and post-quantum signatures, and will buy a trusted root from GlobalSign. Sites could switch on protection against future quantum attacks without a speed penalty, it says.
- A [Branch Target Reuse attack](https://freenode.net/article/branch-target-reuse-brings-spectre-v2-back-to-jit-engines) brings Spectre-v2-style leaks back to JIT engines, the compilers inside browsers and runtimes.
- The EFF says San Francisco [kept its license plate reader cameras with weak safeguards](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/09/while-country-rejects-alpr-mass-surveillance-sf-settles-weak-safeguards), arguing that stiffer penalties won't stop misuse like officers tracking partners or data reaching ICE.

## Devtools & Infra

- Vercel Connect now [takes service submissions](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-connect-service-submissions): describe your service, add OAuth or API-key methods, verify OAuth with a real test token, and Vercel reviews it. You no longer wait for Vercel to build a connector.
- Open-source [Jeff](https://alphasignal.ai/news/open-source-jeff-turns-tiny-models-into-22ms-local-decision-apis) packs zero-shot classification into 0.8B-parameter models that decide in 22 ms, trained on one workstation GPU in about two hours. Small local models can replace a hosted API call for routing decisions.
- Cerebras [ran Qwen3 27B at 1,500 tokens/sec](https://alphasignal.ai/news/cerebras-beats-human-speed-booking-restaurants-19x-faster-than-rivals) to book a dinner in 22 seconds, which it says is 19x faster than Grok, Meta and Claude assistants. It is a vendor demo, but it shows agent latency is now a hardware race.
- Cloudflare says [more than half of Internet traffic wasn't human](https://blog.cloudflare.com/agentic-web/) this year for the first time, with requests from AI agents up 1,700% in a year. Sites now have a second audience that doesn't see ads, so Cloudflare is pushing HTTP 402 payments to charge agents directly.
