**What matters today:** Apple closed its fall event with the foldable it spent years denying interest in: the [iPhone Duo](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/09/apples-long-rumored-foldable-becomes-reality-with-the-2000-iphone-duo/) ships October 23 with a 5.4-inch outer screen, 7.6-inch inner screen and Touch ID, starting at ==$1,999== for 256GB. AI safety governance kept converging from multiple directions: [Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon's resignation warning](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/anthropic-researcher-quits-with-a-warning-self-improving-ai-could-kill-us-all/) turned into a call for [international coordination to limit recursive self-improvement](https://www.techmeme.com/260910/p5#a260910p5), and [Paul Christiano said publicly the industry isn't on track](https://www.techmeme.com/260909/p56#a260909p56) to bring loss-of-control risk down to an acceptable level. Meanwhile the capability race didn't pause for any of it: [OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra-next-generation-work) and [DeepSeek answered hours later with V4.1-Flash](https://www.techmeme.com/260910/p12#a260910p12), the same day [OpenAI's Navier-Stokes math claim kept drawing scrutiny](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/992953/openai-math-millennium-prize-navier-stokes) from the academics it may have scooped.

## AI / LLMs

- [Jacob Coxon quit Anthropic](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/anthropic-researcher-quits-with-a-warning-self-improving-ai-could-kill-us-all/) two months before his equity would have vested to warn publicly that frontier labs "earnestly believe" self-improving superintelligence "could kill us all by the end of the decade"; in a [follow-up interview](https://www.techmeme.com/260910/p5#a260910p5) he called for industry-wide, international coordination to limit recursive self-improvement, and [Interconnects framed the reaction](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/one-resignation-turned-the-embers) as one resignation turning long-simmering AI-safety unease into a wildfire.
- [Paul Christiano, the researcher who pioneered RLHF, joined OpenAI's nonprofit board](https://www.techmeme.com/260909/p56#a260909p56) and its Safety and Security Committee, saying the industry currently isn't on track to bring loss-of-control risk down to an "acceptable" level, the same week [California signed two AI safety-evaluation bills](https://www.techmeme.com/260909/p51#a260909p51) that Anthropic and OpenAI themselves backed.
- [OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra-next-generation-work), its new flagship for business work, pitched on computer and browser use, coding and cybersecurity judgment; API pricing lands at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output, with a Fast mode at 2x the speed for 2x the price, rolling out to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise plans plus Azure and AWS Bedrock.
- [DeepSeek debuted V4.1-Flash](https://www.techmeme.com/260910/p12#a260910p12), the smallest model in a new asymmetric Causal Encoder-Decoder family: a 552B-parameter mixture-of-experts model that activates only 8B parameters per input token and 16B per output token, natively handles vision, and [beats DeepSeek's own flagship V4-Pro on cost and speed at roughly a quarter of the memory footprint](https://alphasignal.ai/news/deepseek-s-v4-1-flash-beats-its-own-flagship-at-a-quarter-of-the-memory-cost); V4-Pro API traffic starts routing to Flash on September 14.
- [OpenAI's Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize claim keeps drawing scrutiny](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/992953/openai-math-millennium-prize-navier-stokes): after NYU's Tristan Buckmaster accused the company of racing to beat his and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge's unpublished work to publication, a [new interview has Buckmaster describing being pulled into the OpenAI-Anthropic rivalry](https://www.techmeme.com/260910/p25#a260910p25) while OpenAI says it has separately made progress on another Millennium Prize problem.
- [Cognition ran up to 18 concurrent Devin agent sessions over three weeks](https://alphasignal.ai/news/cognition-s-devin-breaks-rsa-260-record-in-three-weeks-with-ai-agents) to rewrite a GPU-based factoring pipeline and crack RSA-260, an 862-bit semiprime unbroken since 1991, for about $400,000 in compute, ==10x cheaper== than the prior public record.

## Devtools & Infra

- [Kepler Computing emerged from stealth with $468M](https://www.techmeme.com/260909/p55#a260909p55), claiming its 3D chip stacking and a proprietary material can raise HBM and SRAM density without EUV lithography, chasing the same memory shortage now driving up prices across the industry.
- [Positron raised $875M at a $5B valuation](https://www.techmeme.com/260910/p21#a260910p21) for its Asimov inference chip, which sidesteps the HBM shortage entirely: a "memory-first" design pairing its compute architecture with up to 2.3TB of commodity LPDDR5X memory per chip, taping out on TSMC N3P by year end with production in the second half of 2027.
- [Cloudflare turned on post-quantum DNSSEC validation on 1.1.1.1](https://blog.cloudflare.com/post-quantum-dnssec-1111/) using NIST's ML-DSA-44 algorithm, whose 2,420-byte signatures are nearly 38x larger than today's typical DNSSEC signatures; the company is also building in downgrade protection so a validator that supports the new algorithm can't be tricked into accepting an old, eventually-breakable one.

## Security & Privacy

- [Proofpoint found at least four hacking groups, some tied to the Chinese government, using a near-identical exploit kit it calls BlueMoon](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/09/4-groups-caught-using-the-same-chrome-and-windows-exploit-kit/), chaining two Chromium bugs with a Windows kernel flaw spanning Windows 10 (2018) through Windows 11; all three were patched only in the past 24 hours, and Proofpoint suspects AI-assisted bug hunting sped up how fast the kit spread.
- [Bruce Schneier says a mere rumor of an exploit is now enough for an AI agent to find it](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/09/ais-compress-exploit-timeline.html), fast enough that he could have started exploiting a vulnerability well before its public patch just from knowing roughly what it was about; Simon Willison's comment on the post argues the finding is incompatible with how open-source projects currently run embargoed disclosures.
- [Read the Docs published a postmortem on a nearly ten-day DDoS attack](https://about.readthedocs.com/blog/2026/09/2026-ddos-attack/) that peaked at 5.5 million requests per minute, about 100x normal traffic, deliberately targeting URLs that bypass caching and shifting request patterns and IP pools every time the site tightened rate limits.
- [New Mexico, the only US state taking its Cambridge Analytica-era case against Meta to trial](https://www.techmeme.com/260909/p57#a260909p57), opened by accusing the company of failing to protect Facebook user data and enabling targeted ads meant to suppress Democratic turnout.
