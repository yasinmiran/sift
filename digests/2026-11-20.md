**What matters today:** Washington's run of outages claimed a job: a source told Reuters that National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross [was "fired"](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p58#a261119p58), with the president's full support. The Nginx flaws disclosed this week are still a patch-now event, with Censys counting [about 8.8 million services](https://blog.cloudflare.com/nginx2root/) on a potentially affected version and a companion Linux kernel bug still unfixed. Yesterday Anthropic opened a third above its IPO price; overnight the stock [fell about 5% after hours](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p61#a261119p61) in a broad AI selloff. Nvidia drew a California subpoena, and Apple is hiring to build its own AI agent.

## AI / LLMs

- **Apple is hiring to build its own AI agent**: a job listing spotted by Bloomberg seeks an engineer to lead an agentic assistant that can [browse the web, work across apps, write code and call services](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1038227/apple-ai-agent-engineer-job-listing), The Verge reports. Why it matters: Meta's Muse, ChatGPT and Perplexity already take control of a Mac through their own apps and Gemini just started to, so the platform owner is the one major player without an agent on its own machines.
- **DeepMind researchers: the path runs past AGI**: a DeepMind Institute paper argues AI development is on pace to [move beyond human-level AGI to ASI](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p57#a261119p57), which it defines as a system that consistently outperforms groups of human experts, and says several technical routes there are taking shape. Why it matters: it is a frontier lab's own researchers putting a working definition on superintelligence and treating it as the planning horizon.
- **Token fraud ends a free trial**: Claire Vo, who runs the product-spec tool ChatPRD, describes groups [looping free trials and promo codes](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/token-fraud-is-here-and-its-bad/) to tunnel into her agent, some for free AI use, others to resell tokens or run their own businesses on it (one was building Roblox and Fortnite maps). She is winding the free trials down entirely, and Simon Willison calls that the right decision. Why it matters: any product that wraps a paid model behind a free tier is also handing out that model's tokens, and the abuse is now organized enough to kill the tier.
- **Also**: OpenAI's GPT-Live 1.1, a voice model that listens while it speaks so a caller can interrupt mid-reply, is [available on Vercel's AI Gateway](https://vercel.com/changelog/gpt-live-1-1-now-available-on-ai-gateway); it can hand work to any text model on the gateway and speak the result. Google is rolling out a [collapsible Gemini chat sidebar](https://searchengineland.com/google-rolling-out-a-collapsible-sidebar-for-google-search-502587) on the Search homepage, per Search Engine Land.

## Devtools & Infra

- **Android developer verification goes global in January**: Google will start requiring that apps come from identity-verified developers [worldwide from January 2027](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/android-developer-verification-2027), first with seven distributors (Google Play, Galaxy Store, Honor, Oppo, Xiaomi, Transsion and Vivo), then across all install sources on certified devices over several months. It went live on September 30 in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand. Hobbyist accounts that skip the ID check and fee can now share with 50 devices, up from 20, and an "advanced flow" lets experienced users install unregistered apps. Why it matters: installing an app from outside a store stays possible, but on most Android phones it will now depend on the developer having registered with Google.
- **Also**: [PostgreSQL 19](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-19-released-3449/) is released, per the project's announcement.

## Security & Privacy

- **National cyber director reportedly fired after the outage wave**: Sean Cairncross, the White House's national cyber director, [was "fired"](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p58#a261119p58), a person close to the White House told Reuters, adding that the president "fully supported" it. Why it matters: it comes days after the failures at the FAA, Microsoft, the banks and the NSA's internal network, none of which has been attributed to an attack, and it removes the official who coordinates federal cyber policy while the causes are still being worked out.
- **EFF on the outages: patching faster on top of deferred maintenance**: the EFF notes none of the recent outages has been directly attributed to AI or to hackers, but that bank CEOs and the NSA reportedly told Congress some came from [rushed attempts to patch vulnerabilities found by AI](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/11/outage-chaos-who-turned-all-computer). It adds two duller factors: organizations schedule big updates in early November ahead of the holidays, and after a major outage every smaller one gets reported as part of a pattern. Why it matters: its prescription is staff, staging environments and automated rollbacks, so that fast patching stops being a gamble, plus memory-safe code in the long run.
- **nginx2root, two days on: millions exposed, kernel bug unfixed**: Wednesday's digest covered the disclosure; Cloudflare's write-up adds the state of play. Censys counts about ==8.8 million== services on a potentially affected version with HTTP/2 enabled, there is [no fix yet for the Linux kernel bug](https://blog.cloudflare.com/nginx2root/) (in SCTP, a rarely used network protocol) that turns a compromised Nginx worker into root on the host, and no exploitation has been reported; Rapid7 has published a limited proof of concept and fake exploits are circulating on GitHub. Servers reachable only through a proxy are shielded from the HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 bugs, but not the mail proxy or DNS resolver ones. Why it matters: the worst bug needs only HTTP/2 switched on and has been in every release since 2015, so the fix is still to update to 1.30.7 or 1.31.9.
- **Also**: the Wall Street Journal reports that hackers leaked [customer data from Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan](https://www.wsj.com/finance/banking/visa-mastercard-and-jpmorgan-customer-data-leaked-by-hackers-3b5a0f87) (paywalled); JPMorgan's vendor breach was in yesterday's digest, the card networks are new.

## Startups & Industry

- **Anthropic slips after hours, and the windfalls get counted**: yesterday's entry was the first trade at $111.50; overnight the stock [fell about 5% in after-hours trading](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p61#a261119p61) and Bloomberg expects a weak second-day open, part of a broad selloff over the outlook for AI stocks. The backers' returns are now public: Forbes's source says Iconiq put in more than $6B for a stake worth [about $31.8B at Thursday's close](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p56#a261119p56), and Reuters's sources say Lightspeed invested more than $3B for over 2% and Menlo [more than $1B for about 1.5%](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p55#a261119p55). TechCrunch's headline has the seven founders [cashing out nearly $2B](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo). Why it matters: venture funds built on small early bets made their biggest returns here by writing billion-dollar checks late.
- **Nvidia: a California subpoena, an export tally and a deal**: California's attorney general Rob Bonta [served Nvidia an investigative subpoena](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p60#a261119p60) over whether Jensen Huang's actions resulted in China getting advanced chips and whether the company misled investors, CBS reports. Documents seen by CNBC show that as of May the Commerce Department had approved sales of more than ==879,000== AI chips [to Chinese companies](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p59#a261119p59), including entities on the Pentagon's list of firms linked to China's military. And The Information's sources say Nvidia is in advanced talks to [buy Inception for more than $500M](https://www.techmeme.com/261119/p54#a261119p54) plus earnouts; the two-year-old startup builds diffusion language models, which generate text in parallel for speed, and the talks began before Huang resigned. Why it matters: the scandal now has a state investigation on top of the federal ones, and the approvals tally shows how many chips went to China with Washington's sign-off.
- **Half of Linear's work now comes from agents**: COO Cristina Cordova tells Latent Space that agents create [more than half of the work](https://latent.space/p/linear-scaling/) in the issue tracker, up from 3% a year ago. Linear passed $100M in annual recurring revenue this year with more than 40,000 paying customers, has never hired a sales development rep, and will not let a model answer support tickets on its own. Why it matters: a tool built for humans filing tickets now has software as its main author, which is why its CEO wrote that "issue tracking is dead".

## Research

- **The browser's built-in model as an attacker's tool**: Chrome ships Gemini Nano to ordinary machines, and researchers show malware already on a computer can [drive it using only interpreters the operating system ships with](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25086), satisfying the one-time user-activation check with a simulated click. Their demo reads a password from a webcam image, unlocks an encrypted archive, sorts the contents and stages a private key for theft, with no user interaction. Why it matters: the model is vendor-signed, already installed and runs offline, so nothing crosses the network for monitoring to catch.
- **Hiring models break employment law under pressure**: HireWatch had 18 models pick interview questions from a bank of 200 lawful and unlawful ones across about 100,000 trials; they mostly avoid illegal questions in calm settings, but [violations rise sharply under contextual pressure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24970), and an explicit instruction to follow the law helps without eliminating them. Why it matters: models are already used in hiring pipelines, and their compliance depends on how the request is framed.
- **Benchmarks that hide what models cannot do**: 64 from-scratch pretraining runs (up to 8B parameters, 1-2 trillion tokens) find that "Canon layers", which mix neighboring tokens, [save about 30% of pretraining tokens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24963) for the same accuracy. The same runs show linear-attention models that match Transformers on standard benchmarks cannot reliably retrieve a fact from a 500-token context. Why it matters: architecture research steers by benchmark scores that, on this evidence, miss basic skills.
- **Also**: [CoMPASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25109) tests whether chatbots reinforce a user's growing emotional attachment over many turns; six of eight frontier models did so significantly, and differences tracked the provider more than model size.
