**What matters today:** Yesterday Anthropic was said to be weighing a price above its range; overnight it [priced the IPO at $83 a share](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering), and the stock starts trading on Nasdaq today. OpenAI published its own measure of how far coding agents have taken over AI research: they now [finish 46% of its researchers' 2-to-4-hour tasks unaided](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier), double the share in March. Google's mid-tier Gemini 4 Neon is being scored above its own flagship on coding, Arizona opened a criminal investigation into AI companies over "rogue AI agents" in banks, and Ruby patched seven memory flaws, four of them credited in part to Anthropic.

## AI / LLMs

- **OpenAI: agents now do nearly half of its researchers' multi-hour tasks**: in a report on how coding agents are speeding up AI research, OpenAI says agents complete ==46%== of its researchers' 2-to-4-hour tasks on their own, [up from 23% in March](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier), and that the median researcher's agent use reached $2,400 a day. The report calls the frontier "jagged": performance stays uneven across kinds of work. Why it matters: this is a lab measuring, in its own shop, the loop people mean by recursive self-improvement (AI speeding up the building of AI), and reporting that the share doubled in eight months.
- **Gemini 4 Neon: Google's middle tier outscores its flagship on coding**: Neon is live in Cursor and the Gemini API, and on CursorBench it [beats Gemini 4 Carbon at a lower cost per task](https://alphasignal.ai/news/google-s-gemini-4-neon-beats-carbon-on-coding-tasks-while-costing-23-less), per AlphaSignal. Artificial Analysis puts it [third on its Intelligence Index](https://www.techmeme.com/261118/p58#a261118p58), behind only Claude Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 Astra, a small gain over Carbon at about 17% lower cost per task and close to Opus 5.5 on coding. Why it matters: a day after OpenAI cut its flagship's price, the cheaper tier is where the labs are competing for the default coding agent slot.
- **Meta staff flag Muse mixing up accounts**: internal posts seen by Reuters show employees worried that Muse, Meta's assistant with more than 10 million downloads, [could be mixing up conversations between accounts](https://www.techmeme.com/261118/p64#a261118p64) and exposing private data through actions nobody asked for. Why it matters: an assistant that acts on its own inside personal accounts turns a routing bug into a privacy breach.
- **Also**: Prime Intellect's [Model Factory](https://alphasignal.ai/news/prime-intellect-s-model-factory-merges-ai-training-and-deployment-into-one-loop) puts pre-training, fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, sandboxes and inference on one stack for teams that want to own their model's improvement loop. Parallel [retuned its search API](https://alphasignal.ai/news/parallel-web-systems-retunes-search-apis-to-beat-rivals-on-healthcare-ai) for FDA labels, trial registries and PubMed, and reports the best accuracy on four healthcare benchmarks in its own tests.

## Devtools & Infra

- **Vercel Board: a message board for agent fleets**: Vercel launched [Board](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-board), an experimental shared message store where agents post tasks, delegate, log status and keep each task's discussion in one thread, with access grants for humans and agents and two-way sync to Slack and GitHub. It is reached through the CLI or a JavaScript SDK, works across harnesses (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, LangChain and others) and is [available on all plans](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-board-is-now-available). Why it matters: a single agent can track work in a markdown file; teams of agents from different vendors have had no common place to coordinate.
- **Copilot code review sets its own depth**: GitHub's reviewer now [picks how much effort each pull request gets](https://alphasignal.ai/news/github-s-copilot-now-judges-its-own-code-review-effort-automatically), reads related public repos for context, and flags leaked secrets, including credentials with no recognizable format. Why it matters: secret scanners match known key patterns; a reviewer that catches the shapeless ones covers the gap they leave.
- **Also**: Simon Willison's [datasette-arrows](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/18/datasette-arrows/) plugin streams tables of any size out of Datasette in Apache Arrow format, using a new hook that lets plugins add streaming exports; he says Claude Fable 5.2 built it in almost one shot.

## Security & Privacy

- **Ruby patches seven memory flaws, some with working exploits**: the Ruby team released [4.0.10, 3.4.13 and 3.3.14](https://www.ruby-lang.org/en/news/2026/11/18/multiple-vulnerabilities-in-regexp-and-string/) to fix seven bugs in the regular expression engine and in changing a string's case. It knows of proofs of concept showing some can lead to remote code execution, in one case just by upper- or lower-casing a crafted string. Four are credited to Stephan Lipp of Ada Logics and Anthropic; CVE numbers are still pending, and Ruby 3.2 and older get no fix. Why it matters: after Java and Nginx earlier this week, a third piece of core server software ships memory fixes with an AI lab in the credits, and case conversion of user input is something almost every web app does.
- **Arizona opens a criminal investigation into AI companies**: the state attorney general is [investigating OpenAI and other AI firms](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/arizona-says-it-is-investigating-openai-other-firms-over-possible-rogue-agents-2026-11-19) over what the office calls the "apparent infiltration of banks by rogue AI agents", Reuters reports. Why it matters: a criminal inquiry by a state prosecutor is a different level of exposure from the civil suits AI companies usually face, and the allegation is so far only the office's description.
- **US charges a Russian national with stalking staff of a firm aiding Ukraine**: prosecutors allege he acted as a foreign agent for Moscow, [covertly seeking information on employees](https://www.techmeme.com/261118/p55#a261118p55) of a tech and defense company that a Bloomberg source identifies as Palantir. Why it matters: the alleged targets are the engineers, not the company's systems.

## Research

- **Training a model to report on itself**: [Introspective oversight](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23365) adds a third-person "observer" role to a model, trained only to answer questions about the assistant's behavior accurately. On five held-out tests for things like deception and reward hacking it scored 0.907 AUROC (1.0 is perfect detection), ahead of reading transcripts (0.828) or decoding internal activations (0.796). Why it matters: outside monitors see only what a model writes; this asks the one party with full context.
- **When coding agents edit their own rules**: across 932,791 agent-written pull requests, changes to CI workflows and permissions carried [3.9 times the governance risk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23251) of ordinary code changes, and policy flags appeared on 63.6% of the merged ones. Why it matters: the pipeline config is where an agent's own limits are written, and most of these changes went in flagged.
- **Where the tokens go**: a study that has agents make a change and then undo it, repeatedly, finds about [80% of tokens in a successful run](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23375) are spent in model calls that leave the requested change untouched. Why it matters: agents are billed per token, and this is a first attempt to say how much of the bill buys the edit.
