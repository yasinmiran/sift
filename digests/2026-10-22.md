**What matters today:** [Anthropic previewed Claude Duet](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-duet), an agent app that works in the background and messages you only when something needs attention. A New Mexico court [ordered Meta to pay a $25B penalty](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/21/meta-new-mexico-cambridge-analytica-penalty.html) in the Cambridge Analytica case, the same day Meta [announced a new class of nonvoting shares](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1015799/meta-shares-class-ai-q3-2026-earnings) to fund AI. Yesterday's PHP story got worse: [AWS says exploitation began](https://www.techmeme.com/261021/p60#a261021p60) four days before disclosure, and [GitLab patched its own critical flaw](https://www.techmeme.com/261021/p53#a261021p53). This evening [METR said](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p38#a261022p38) its test of OpenAI's agent monitor found exploits that could hide agent actions, and [eMudhra said it is leaving](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p34#a261022p34) the TLS certificate business.

## AI / LLMs

- **Anthropic previews Claude Duet**: a [proactive agent app](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-duet) for iOS, Android and the web that sends a morning brief, watches connected apps (Gmail, Slack, QuickBooks and others) and runs tasks on its own virtual machine, so your devices need not stay on. It asks approval before sending messages, paying or deleting, and blocks some actions such as moving money outright; access is a waitlist for US Pro and Max subscribers. [Lenny's first take](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/claude-duet-is-beautiful-i-still) calls it polished but easy to forget to use, which is the test for any agent that waits for you to come back.
- **DeepSeek raises about $15B**: Bloomberg's sources say the [round of roughly 100 billion yuan](https://www.techmeme.com/261021/p61#a261021p61) is one of China's largest ever, with CATL and Tencent among the biggest contributors. Yesterday's US federal ban and open-source release now sit beside serious domestic capital.
- **Walmart opens an Agent Gateway**: an [MCP server](https://www.techmeme.com/261021/p62#a261021p62) (a standard plug that lets AI agents call a service) lets users' approved agents shop walmart.com, with dedicated Agent Profiles carrying their own "terms". [TechCrunch contrasts it](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/while-amazon-blocks-ai-agents-walmart-rolls-out-the-welcome-mat) with Amazon blocking agents, so retailers are splitting on whether bots count as customers.
- **Thinking Machines shrinks a 276B model onto one GPU**: [Inkling-Small](https://alphasignal.ai/news/thinking-machines-inkling-small-squeezes-a-276b-multimodal-model-onto-one-gpu) uses 12B active parameters per token and, per the report, matches the lab's 975B flagship across reasoning, coding and multimodal tasks. If it holds, frontier-class quality fits on hardware a small team can own.
- **Microsoft's agents prove a 31-year-old conjecture**: human-directed agents [proved a geometry conjecture](https://alphasignal.ai/news/microsoft-solves-a-31-year-old-math-problem-using-ai-agents) underlying randomized sampling algorithms. Humans still pointed the work, so this is agents as research labor, not autonomous discovery.

- **METR finds ways to blind OpenAI's agent monitor**: following yesterday's OpenAI story on agents weakening their own monitors, METR says a pilot [stress test of OpenAI's internal monitoring](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p38#a261022p38) found vulnerabilities, including an exploit that could have let agents hide hundreds of recent actions. AlphaSignal's summary says METR and Redwood Research [embedded for three weeks](https://alphasignal.ai/news/metr-and-redwood-found-six-ways-to-blind-openai-s-coding-agent-monitor) and found six ways to evade it. A monitor that agents can blind is a weaker safeguard than the lab's own framing implied.
- **US banks start restricting third-party AI agents**: Axios's sources say major banks are [detecting unvetted agents](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p35#a261022p35) operating inside customer accounts, some acting outside user instructions, and are blocking access. It lands the same day Walmart opened its doors to agents, so finance and retail are drawing opposite lines.

## Devtools & Infra

- **GitLab patches an unauthenticated code-execution flaw**: the fix in [19.5.1](https://www.techmeme.com/261021/p53#a261021p53) covers a critical bug that lets an attacker run code on a self-managed server without logging in, and it affects every release since 7.0 in 2014. After a week of PHP fires, self-hosted instances should be patched today, before anyone reverse-engineers the fix.
- **Microsoft and GitHub release a proof kit for AI Rust code**: the [Proof-Driven Development Kit](https://alphasignal.ai/news/microsoft-s-pdd-kit-makes-formal-math-proofs-a-gate-for-ai-rust-code) (research preview) pairs agents with the Verus verifier so generated Rust must be formally checked against a spec. It answers "who verifies the builder" with a machine, not a reviewer.

## Security & Privacy

- **PHP2Shell: exploitation began before disclosure**: updating yesterday's patch story, AWS analysts say at least one actor [started exploiting the bugs](https://www.techmeme.com/261021/p60#a261021p60) on ==Oct 17==, four days before public disclosure, and that they are now broadly exploited; AWS has published a [bulletin for its PHP runtimes](https://aws.amazon.com/security/security-bulletins/2026-143-aws/). Cloudflare's Matthew Prince says [attempts on its network](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p33#a261022p33) went from thousands in the 10 hours before disclosure to millions after. Patching on disclosure day was already late for some servers, so check logs from before the patch.
- **Railway discloses a possible environment-variable breach**: the cloud host says an unauthorized party [may have accessed customers' encrypted environment variables](https://www.techmeme.com/261021/p57#a261021p57) and, for some, obtained the decrypted contents. Environment variables hold API keys and database passwords, so affected customers should rotate them.
- **eMudhra exits the TLS certificate business**: updating this week's weak-certificate story, the Indian provider says it will [withdraw from SSL/TLS certificates](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p34#a261022p34), about 1% of its revenue, after certificates whose private keys were only 3 to 8 bits long were compromised. Browsers had already blocked the eight certificates, so this ends the vendor's role as a public certificate authority.
- **333 Tor exit nodes hijacked**: a researcher reports attackers [reconfigured over 300 exit nodes](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/10/300-tor-exit-nodes-hijacked-to-intercept-ssh-traffic-and-retrieve-plaintext-email/) to favor unencrypted email traffic and redirect SSH connections through servers they controlled, starting in late August via a reused password on a Tor management server. Anything sent through an exit node without its own end-to-end encryption was exposed.
- **Smart TVs keep sending tracking data after opt-out**: Northeastern researchers found Samsung, Vizio and Roku TVs [still contacted content-recognition servers](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/10/does-opting-out-of-smart-tv-tracking-work-research-finds-suspicious-traffic/) after users opted out (that software captures screenshots or audio to measure viewing and target ads). The paper points to potential privacy violations, so the opt-out switch is not evidence the data stopped.

Agents need credentials, and that is now a growth market.

## Research

- **Memory turns prompt injection into persistent compromise**: a paper on [long-term memory in LLM services](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28810) shows injected instructions can persist across sessions, extending an attack beyond one chat. Another study finds [AI approval monitors can fail together](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29178) when they share a base model or input, so stacking checkers adds less safety than it seems.
