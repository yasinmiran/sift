**What matters today:** [Anthropic donated the Agent Skills standard](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p53#a261022p53) to the Agentic AI Foundation, saying 70+ platforms already use it, the second agent standard it has handed to a neutral home after MCP. [OpenAI is nearing a round](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p52#a261022p52) of up to $30B at a $1.25T pre-money valuation, [down from the $1.4T](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p55#a261022p55) it pitched earlier this month. The PHP story gets a human footnote: the [Hacker News poster who leaked the embargo apologised](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50193416), and the volunteer behind the patches [spoke to The Atlantic](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p58#a261022p58).

## AI / LLMs

- **Anthropic gives Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundation**: Skills are the open format that gives agents packaged know-how and workflows, and Anthropic says [70+ platforms have integrated it](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p53#a261022p53), after it donated MCP to the same foundation in 2025. Neutral governance is what lets rivals adopt a format without depending on its author.
- **Claude now runs directly on Google Cloud**: [Anthropic offers Claude natively there](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-brings-claude-directly-to-google-cloud-with-same-day-feature-access) with same-day feature access, Google billing, IAM and commitment drawdown, no Vertex AI needed. Enterprises can spend existing Google Cloud commitments on Claude without a middle layer.
- **Claude helps prove open-source libraries cannot crash**: Anthropic and Theorem used Claude to produce [machine-checked proofs](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-s-claude-proves-239-open-source-libraries-crash-free-exposing-fuzzing) that open-source libraries are crash-free at the assembly level, surfacing long-latent bugs that fuzzing (throwing random inputs at code) missed. Anthropic's own [write-up on formally verifying real-world software](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification) drew 204 points on Hacker News. Proof, unlike testing, covers every input.
- **Artificial Analysis puts Claude Opus 5.5 first in a tougher coding-agent index**: the leaderboard now uses [Cognition's FrontierCode benchmark](https://alphasignal.ai/news/artificial-analysis-ranks-claude-opus-5-5-first-in-tougher-coding-agent-index) and stricter Q&A grading, which reshuffled the top of the chart. One index's ranking, not a verdict.
- **Salesforce plans fees for outside AI agents**: customers [brace for charges](https://zmedia.co/news/wire/cmvjv98d900edugumfgilx4zz-salesforce-customers-brace-for-new-fees-to-use-claude-in-its) when agents such as Claude work inside Salesforce apps. Where yesterday's Walmart opened its doors, a software vendor is putting a meter on them.
- **Canva launches Craft, its first image model**: trained on [human-designed templates](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p51#a261022p51) and open to all users, with plans to support third-party chatbots. [The Verge notes](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1017151/canva-craft-in-house-ai-model) it plans a design before generating it. Training on designer work, not scraped images, is the pitch to its users.

## Devtools & Infra

- **AWS Continuum for code vulnerabilities goes generally available**: it [finds vulnerabilities, checks which are actually exploitable](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-continuum-for-code-vulnerabilities-is-now-generally-available/) in your AWS environment, ranks them by business impact and proposes a fix as a patch or pull request. Every fix starts with a human approval step. It answers a week of PHP patching with triage instead of more alerts.
- **Vercel wants static credentials gone**: its post [argues for short-lived OIDC tokens](https://vercel.com/blog/death-to-long-lived-static-credentials) (a signed proof of identity that expires) instead of reusable API keys, and says only ==8.4%== of 1,000+ SaaS vendors it surveyed supported secure authentication. A stolen key keeps working until someone rotates it; a stolen token dies on its own.
- **Netflix gives sensitive tasks their own VM on Titus**: [single-tenant Titus](https://netflixtechblog.com/single-tenant-titus-vm-level-isolation-on-a-multi-tenant-container-platform-6d8b1909d163?source=rss----2615bd06b42e---4) runs a task in a dedicated virtual machine inside the container platform, for real-time systems where a shared Linux kernel is too noisy. Teams keep the same API while the platform team owns the host.
- **Arc browser shuts down on December 22**: its makers say they will [sunset Arc](https://mailchi.mp/diabrowser/arc-sunset). Anyone using it as a daily driver has two months to move.

## Security & Privacy

- **PHP RCE: the embargo leak and the patcher**: updating this week's story, the [Hacker News poster apologised](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50193416) for flagging the coming fixes without knowing about an embargo, and says their contact had been told there was none. The Atlantic interviewed [Ondřej Surý](https://www.techmeme.com/261022/p58#a261022p58), the volunteer who maintains PHP packages and automates their patches. Disclosure timing was already tight, and a public thread shows how easily it slips.
- **Meta turns Muse sharing back on**: after researchers found shared conversation links [guessable through public DNS](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1017066/meta-muse-sharing-back-on-artifiacts), including a loan application and an internal revenue review, Meta paused sharing and has now restored it. Check what you have already shared.
- **FCC blocks gadgets over a "foreign surveillance chip"**: using the power it gave itself on October 13, the agency [blocked two products](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1017016/fcc-block-foreign-surveillance-chips-hivision-huawei-loophole) it says contain HiVision's Hi3516CV610, a chip that turns a camera sensor into a security camera or dashcam. It did not name the products, so buyers cannot yet check their own.
- **Canada's C-22 surveillance bill reaches the Senate**: the EFF says the Lawful Access Act, [already through the House](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/canadians-are-running-out-time-oppose-dangerous-c-22-surveillance-bill), would force digital services to give the government new powers, and points to a petition. The Senate is the last stop.

## Research

- **Agent handshakes cost real tokens**: a benchmark of [agent protocol handshakes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30151) finds a legacy MCP handshake takes three round trips and 230 ms at 50 ms latency against 61 ms for A2A, and that a 200-capability counterpart eats 11% of a 200k-token context before the agent starts. Another paper [compares MCP and CLI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30157) and recommends a hybrid. Protocol choice is a latency and context-budget decision, not just a standards one.

## Threads

- **Standards leaving their authors**: Anthropic donating Agent Skills, the agent handshake cost paper and the MCP versus CLI comparison all ask who sets the rules agents run on, and at what cost.
- **Proof over testing**: Claude's crash-freedom proofs, Anthropic's verification write-up and AWS Continuum's exploitability checks each try to replace "probably fine" with evidence.
- **Credentials as the weak point**: Vercel's push against static keys, the eMudhra OTP bypass and the Muse links found through public DNS show secrets and identities leaking through plumbing nobody was watching.
