## AI / LLMs

- **Riemann, day three: the verification files are public**: [verification files for the Lean proof](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) of the Riemann hypothesis are posted on an MIT mathematics department page and reached the Hacker News front page (106 points). TechCrunch's recap frames the week as an AI claiming the 167-year-old problem and [a human claiming it too](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too). Why it matters: yesterday's statement from the Lean community was about how the proof should be checked; published files are what independent checking needs.
- **Google's search page loses its search button**: Google's latest redesign of the search page [drops the search button and adds an AI sidebar](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/googles-latest-search-page-redesign-drops-the-search-button-adds-ai-sidebar), Ars Technica reports. Why it matters: the most visited page on the web is being rearranged around the AI assistant, with the classic search controls giving up the space.
- **Also**: Nikolas Goebel writes about using fast "System One" models as [informal proof checkers](https://www.nikolasgoebel.com/2026/11/22/informal-verification.html), a timely subject this week. One developer explains [why they changed their opinion on MCP](https://frantic.im/mcp), the standard way agents connect to outside tools. Igor's Techno Club has a [field guide to running Qwen3.8 locally](https://igorstechnoclub.com/field-guide-running-qwen38-locally). On LessWrong: an essay on [why models believe in skepticism](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/r2aAGgfFcFfRikEdb/why-models-believe-in-skepticism), and a post revisiting Scott Alexander's ["Whispering Earring"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/y6WgbNRWsQdnoutqa/piercing-my-ear-for-the-whispering-earring), his parable of an adviser that is always right, read as a warning about AI.

## Devtools & Infra

- **mod_wsgi's author builds its successor in Rust, with AI help**: Graham Dumpleton started mod_wsgi, the Apache module that has served Python web apps for 20 years, and [Muru is his new server](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/21/muru/): written in Rust, AI-assisted, running both WSGI and ASGI apps (Python's older synchronous and newer async web interfaces) in development and production. Simon Willison notes Dumpleton has published an unusually detailed account as he builds, a run of posts over the last three weeks. Why it matters: it is a worked, public example of a veteran maintainer using AI to build infrastructure in a language outside his project's history, with the design reasoning written down.
- **Also**: gVisor, Google's sandboxing layer for containers, now presents itself as ["The Sandbox Kernel for Agents"](https://gvisor.dev). [fast-anything](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/fast-anything) profiles a codebase and ports its hot spots to Rust. RightNow AI describes GPU kernels for Nvidia's Blackwell chips that return [the same bits at 1 row or 64](https://www.rightnowai.co/blog/bit-identical-fp4-moe-kernels-on-blackwell).

## Security & Privacy

- **Optimizely fallout: "you can't outsource accountability"**: earlier this week Optimizely confirmed the compromise that pushed malicious JavaScript to its customers' sites. Troy Hunt's weekly update looks at the notices that followed: affected companies describe ["an incident being at a third party"](https://www.troyhunt.com/weekly-update-531/), and Norwegian Airlines, he says, has not named the third party at all. Why it matters: Hunt's point is that the choice to disclose, and the responsibility, stay with the company people gave their data to, whoever's script was at fault.

## Elsewhere

- **Amazon prints its lobbying on the box**: Amazon is putting labels on New York shipments claiming the City Council [could add up to $5 to any Amazon order](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039564/amazon-nyc-council-packages-shipping-label), The Verge reports, after showing similar notices in its app and on its website earlier this week. The target is the Delivery Protection Act, which would require last-mile delivery firms operating warehouses to be licensed by the city and to employ their workers directly, effectively banning the contractor arrangements Amazon uses for many deliveries. Why it matters: the $5 is Amazon's claim, not a fee in the bill, and it now reaches customers on the package itself.
- **After the Huang scandal, a dating panic**: matchmakers tell Wired that more tech workers [fear romantic espionage](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p11#a261121p11) since the Jensen Huang scandal, and an AI companion app is pitching "zero espionage" executive girlfriends. Why it matters: a national security story has become a personal anxiety among newly wealthy tech workers, and a product pitch.
