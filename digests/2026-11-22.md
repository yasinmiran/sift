**What matters today:** the Nvidia chip scandal grew on three fronts over the weekend. The Wall Street Journal's sources put [a man Singapore charged with fraud in 2025](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p1#a261122p1) at the center of the alleged smuggling route to China, the New York Times describes [a Chinese spy agency campaign](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p5#a261122p5) to get AI chips for the country's companies, and Reuters reports the SEC's probe is [looking at Nvidia's accounting](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-secs-nvidia-probe-is-examining-potential-accounting-issues-sources-say-2026-11-20). AI hardware is getting dearer for buyers who thought the price was settled: server makers are [demanding more months after contracts were signed](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p2#a261122p2). And yesterday's Lean community statement on the Riemann proof turns out to carry a verdict: [full confidence that it is correct](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p3#a261122p3).

## AI / LLMs

- **Riemann, day three: the checker's maintainers say the proof is correct**: yesterday's entry had the Lean and Mathlib communities publishing a joint statement on verifying Levent Alpöge's proof; the line Techmeme leads with today is its conclusion, "we have ((full confidence))" that [the proof of the Riemann hypothesis is correct](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p3#a261122p3), endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative, the two organizations that steward the proof checker and its math library. Separately, [verification files for the Lean proof](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) are posted on an MIT mathematics department page and reached the Hacker News front page. TechCrunch's recap frames the week as an AI claiming the 167-year-old problem and [a human claiming it too](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too). Why it matters: the people best placed to say whether the machine check holds have now said it does, which moves the open question from "is it right" to "who can understand it".
- **Tesla ships FSD v15 with a generated view of the road**: Tesla began rolling out FSD v15, the new version of its driver-assistance software, to some vehicles on Saturday; Gizmodo singles out its [photorealistic rendering of the car's surroundings](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p4#a261122p4), which Elon Musk says is AI video generated in real time with no game engine. Electrek says the release [targets the system's biggest weaknesses](https://electrek.co/2026/11/21/tesla-fsd-v15-rollout-25x-safety-reality-view), from signs to potholes. Why it matters: the display is the visible change, but reading signs and road damage is where the driving itself has to improve.
- **Google's search page loses its search button**: Google's latest redesign of the search page [drops the search button and adds an AI sidebar](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/googles-latest-search-page-redesign-drops-the-search-button-adds-ai-sidebar), Ars Technica reports. Why it matters: the most visited page on the web is being rearranged around the AI assistant, with the classic search controls giving up the space.
- **A game studio pulls AI music after a review bombing**: The Binding of Isaac: Repentance Plus was review-bombed on Steam after a Reddit user found audio files in a Grand Theft Auto parody section carrying metadata from Suno, the AI music generator; creator Edmund McMillen apologized, said he "had no idea", and [the music has been removed](https://www.theverge.com/games/1039770/binding-of-isaac-ai-music), The Verge reports. Valve requires developers to disclose generative AI use on Steam. Why it matters: players found the AI content from file metadata before the developer disclosed it, and the penalty arrived as store reviews.
- **Also**: The New Stack put [GPT-6.2 Astra against Claude Fable 5.2](https://thenewstack.io/gpt-6-2-astra-vs-claude-fable-5-2) and says Astra, whose launch drew mixed reviews, tested well. The New York Times asks Jony Ive and others about Silicon Valley's fixation on ["taste" as a defense against AI slop](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p7#a261122p7), and whether AI can learn it. Nikolas Goebel writes about using fast "System One" models as [informal proof checkers](https://www.nikolasgoebel.com/2026/11/22/informal-verification.html). Karthik Tadepalli explains why he does [not expect superintelligence in 2027](https://blog.karthiktadepalli.com/p/asi-2027). One developer explains [why they changed their opinion on MCP](https://frantic.im/mcp), the standard way agents connect to outside tools, and Igor's Techno Club has a [field guide to running Qwen3.8 locally](https://igorstechnoclub.com/field-guide-running-qwen38-locally).

## Devtools & Infra

- **mod_wsgi's author builds its successor in Rust, with AI help**: Graham Dumpleton started mod_wsgi, the Apache module that has served Python web apps for 20 years, and [Muru is his new server](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/21/muru/): written in Rust, AI-assisted, running both WSGI and ASGI apps (Python's older synchronous and newer async web interfaces) in development and production. Simon Willison notes Dumpleton has published an unusually detailed account as he builds, a run of posts over the last three weeks. Why it matters: it is a worked, public example of a veteran maintainer using AI to build infrastructure in a language outside his project's history, with the design reasoning written down.
- **Gadgets become something you can reprogram by asking**: The Verge's David Pierce argues that modding and jailbreaking hardware, long a hobby for a few, is now easy because people can [use language models to hack their own gadgets](https://www.theverge.com/column/1039139/vibe-coding-gadgets-tv-camera). Why it matters: device makers have relied on the difficulty of modding to keep products locked, and that difficulty is going away.
- **Also**: gVisor, Google's sandboxing layer for containers, now presents itself as ["The Sandbox Kernel for Agents"](https://gvisor.dev). [fast-anything](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/fast-anything) profiles a codebase and ports only its hot spots to Rust. [carquet](https://github.com/Vitruves/carquet) is a C library for Parquet, the columnar data file format, tuned with SIMD instructions. Nicolas Frankel writes [on reviewing AI-generated code](https://blog.frankel.ch/reviewing-ai-generated-code), and Anil Madhavapeddy asks whether software was [always too complex for humans](https://anil.recoil.org/notes/software-too-complex-for-humans).

## Security & Privacy

- **AI speeds up the homework for attacks on industrial equipment**: experts tell the Financial Times that AI has made [planning cyberattacks on industrial equipment faster](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p6#a261122p6) by helping attackers read manuals and research past accidents more quickly. Why it matters: the slow part of hitting physical systems was learning how each machine works and fails, and that is reading work models do well.
- **Optimizely fallout: "you can't outsource accountability"**: earlier this week Optimizely confirmed the compromise that pushed malicious JavaScript to its customers' sites. Troy Hunt's weekly update looks at the notices that followed: affected companies describe ["an incident being at a third party"](https://www.troyhunt.com/weekly-update-531/), and Norwegian Airlines, he says, has not named the third party at all. Why it matters: Hunt's point is that the choice to disclose, and the responsibility, stay with the company people gave their data to, whoever's script was at fault.
- **Also**: 808bits works through how close a new [38-step SHA-256 collision](https://808bits.com/articles/sha-256-38-step-collision-bitcoin) gets to breaking Bitcoin; SHA-256, the hash function Bitcoin depends on, runs 64 steps. Alexandre Dulaunoy traces a line [from SATAN to agentic attacks](https://www.foo.be/2026/11/From_SATAN_to_Agentic_Attacks), arguing each generation fears the tool rather than the infrastructure it exposes.

## Startups & Industry

- **The Nvidia chip scandal: a middleman, a spy agency and the accountants**: Li Ming, an associate of Megaspeed executive Alice Huang whom Singapore charged with fraud in 2025, was [central to smuggling restricted AI chips](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p1#a261122p1) from Southeast Asia to China, the Wall Street Journal's sources say. An FT investigation ties Huang to gaming firms, alleged Prince Group figures and [hundreds of shell companies](https://www.ft.com/content/f3ad8a81-3890-4a8a-8853-ff0a94b08ba9) (paywalled) in a network moving Nvidia chips to China. Current and former officials describe to the New York Times a campaign by the MSS, China's civilian spy agency, to [help Chinese companies acquire advanced AI chips](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p5#a261122p5). And Reuters' sources say Nvidia's [accounting is a focus of the SEC investigation](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-secs-nvidia-probe-is-examining-potential-accounting-issues-sources-say-2026-11-20), which Wednesday's entry had examining whether disclosures misled investors. Why it matters: the reporting now describes an organized acquisition effort with state backing, and the regulator's questions have reached Nvidia's books.
- **AI server makers reopen signed deals**: Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro are [demanding substantially higher prices](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p2#a261122p2) from buyers months after contracts were signed, the Financial Times reports, with increases of up to ==45%== and the added cost landing on the trading firms in the middle. Fortune adds the power side: data center electricity demand is [growing 19% a year against 2% for the grid](https://fortune.com/2026/11/22/ai-data-centers-power-grid-capacity). Why it matters: a signed contract no longer fixes what AI capacity costs, the same week investors started doubting the spending.
- **How Anthropic ran its IPO banks**: fearing leaks, Anthropic limited some of the banks on its record offering to [four-person teams](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p8#a261122p8), shared files on a platform that logged every view, and sought six-month bans on advising rivals, Reuters' sources say. A separate report says the listing made [more than 5,000 employees millionaires](https://www.financialexpress.com/market/global-markets/anthropic-ipo-creates-over-5000-employee-millionaires-overnight/4389252). Why it matters: an issuer this large could dictate terms to Wall Street, including who its bankers may work for next.
- **Also**: Google Quantum AI says it demonstrated contextuality, a property of quantum mechanics with no classical equivalent, on [its Willow chip at 105 qubits](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/quantum-computing/putting-one-of-quantum-mechanics-deepest-features-to-test-at-scale). Data Center Dynamics argues the next phase of the buildout [runs on optical links](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/opinions/why-the-next-phase-of-the-data-center-buildout-runs-on-light).

## Elsewhere

- **Protesters enter Meta's Delhi offices**: members of India's opposition Congress party protested at Meta's offices in Delhi on Friday and [appear to have entered the building](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039732/protesters-storm-meta-offices-in-india), The Verge reports from video shared by CNN. The party's youth wing wants leaders' accounts restored and accuses Meta of censoring speech that Prime Minister Narendra Modi's BJP disagrees with; two accounts were restored Friday, and more protests are threatened. Why it matters: a moderation dispute with an opposition party has moved from complaints to people inside the office.
- **Amazon prints its lobbying on the box**: Amazon is putting labels on New York shipments claiming the City Council [could add up to $5 to any Amazon order](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039564/amazon-nyc-council-packages-shipping-label), The Verge reports, after showing similar notices in its app and on its website earlier this week. The target is the Delivery Protection Act, which would require last-mile delivery firms operating warehouses to be licensed by the city and to employ their workers directly, effectively banning the contractor arrangements Amazon uses for many deliveries. Why it matters: the $5 is Amazon's claim, not a fee in the bill, and it now reaches customers on the package itself.
- **Weight-loss drugs as land policy**: researchers writing in Ars Technica modeled wide use of GLP-1 medicines such as Ozempic among adults with obesity and estimate lower calorie demand could [free up to 32 million hectares of farmland](https://arstechnica.com/health/2026/11/glp-1-drugs-could-have-a-surprisingly-large-environmental-payoff/), an area slightly larger than Italy, and prevent up to 6.6 million deaths a year by 2050. Why it matters: it is a simulation, but it treats a drug class as a force on the global food system, not only on patients.
- **After the Huang scandal, a dating panic**: matchmakers tell Wired that more tech workers [fear romantic espionage](https://www.techmeme.com/261121/p11#a261121p11) since the Jensen Huang scandal, and an AI companion app is pitching "zero espionage" executive girlfriends. Why it matters: a national security story has become a personal anxiety among newly wealthy tech workers, and a product pitch.
