**What matters today:** two stories ran in parallel. Microsoft 365 went down worldwide at midday Thursday and was [only gradually recovering](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1032862/microsoft-outlook-down-outage) almost two days later, with millions of users cut off from Outlook. And yesterday Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO; today the aftermath spread in three directions: the FT's sources say the board has [approached Arm CEO Rene Haas](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p17#a261113p17) as a possible successor, the NYT's say the [$500B lending consortium](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p20#a261113p20) Huang assembled is in flux, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said three departments wanted Megaspeed blacklisted for months while Commerce held the pen. Elsewhere, OpenAI gave its agents a wallet, and Google's Project Zero set a schedule to cut its bug disclosure deadline to 30 days.

## AI / LLMs

- **Agents get a wallet**: OpenAI announced [ChatGPT Wallet](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet), which lets agents in ChatGPT Work, Codex and Dots complete purchases with the user's approval and existing card protections; CNBC says it is open to US Pro subscribers over 18. The same day Brave's Search API began [accepting payments from agents](https://brave.com/blog/search-api-agentic-payments/) over x402, a protocol for machine-to-machine payment. Why it matters: paying was the step where an agent still had to hand back to a human, and two companies loosened that in one day.
- **Gemini 4 Hydrogen, the new default**: Google's third Gemini 4 model [launched yesterday](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1032505/google-search-ai-mode-gemini-4-hydrogen) across Search, the Gemini app, AI Studio and the API; it is now the default in AI Mode and the free-tier model. Simon Willison [finds the pricing confusing](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/12/llm-gemini/): $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 output, only 25% under Carbon and Argon until their prices double on December 1, and ==15 times== the $0.10/$0.50 of Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna. AINews relays [Artificial Analysis's independent tests](https://latent.space/p/ainews-jensen-huang-resigns-after-33-years-leading-nvidia/): a low 8% hallucination rate on its knowledge benchmark, but 54% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, behind Claude Sonnet 5.5 at 64%. Why it matters: this is the Gemini most people will actually meet, and it is priced well above rivals' small models.
- **Chinese models, American chips**: Will DePue's analysis at Unemployment Labs says six open-weight models from DeepSeek, Moonshot, MiniMax and Ant Group [carry fingerprints of Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs](https://unemploymentlabs.com/checkpoint-forensics), which cannot legally be sold to China; it is one group's forensic reading, not independently confirmed, and its scale is debated. Today a Reuters review of Chinese contracts and disclosures added a second line of evidence: [more than $3.2B in compute projects](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p7#a261113p7) built or planned around restricted Nvidia GPUs. Why it matters: the export debate now has evidence from the models and from the paperwork, where it used to rest on anonymous sources.
- **Anthropic's Friday**: Bloomberg's sources say the IPO is [closed to institutional investors in mainland China and Hong Kong](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p18#a261113p18), as SpaceX's was, partly because of US arms export rules. The Verge's hands-on with Duet, its consumer agent, meanwhile calls it ["a little neurotic and surprisingly helpful"](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1032518/claude-duet-ai-agent-hands-on). Why it matters: export rules now decide who may buy shares in an AI lab, as well as who may buy its chips.
- **Labs cut side projects**: OpenAI [disbanded its standalone robotics team](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p19#a261113p19), moving staff into research and applied units on the argument that models like GPT-6 Astra can be applied to robots directly, Fortune reports. The Information's sources say SpaceX's AI group [laid off about 60 Grok Imagine staff](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p14#a261113p14), leaving a handful on the image and video tool while it prioritizes coding tools. Why it matters: both are betting that general models and coding products deserve the headcount that specialist teams used to get.

## Devtools & Infra

- **Microsoft 365, down for two days**: the outage began midday Thursday and hit Microsoft's email, cloud and AI services worldwide. Microsoft [blames failed server health checks](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/microsoft-blames-failed-server-health-checks-not-hackers-for-global-outage/), not an attack, and extended its email timeout to 48 hours so delayed mail is not lost; one expert told Ars it may prove worse than the Amazon or CrowdStrike outages. By Friday afternoon The Verge reported service returning after almost two days, with [mail restored for some users](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1032862/microsoft-outlook-down-outage) and delivery still delayed. Why it matters: a check meant to detect unhealthy servers took down the mail system much of the working world runs on, and recovery is being counted in days.
- **Spending caps and sealed payloads**: Cloudflare put [Spend Management](https://blog.cloudflare.com/spend-management/) into beta for pay-as-you-go customers: set a limit on Workers or Durable Objects and Cloudflare pauses the product when it is reached, where before it could only send alerts. Vercel's [Workflow SDK 5](https://vercel.com/blog/workflow-sdk-5) encrypts every step input and output with a per-run AES-256-GCM key before it leaves your code, so the backend stores only ciphertext. Also out: [llama.cpp 1.0.0](https://huggingface.co/blog/ggml-org/llama-cpp-v1), the first 1.0 of the engine under much of local AI. Why it matters: a hard cap is the feature developers asked serverless platforms for after surprise bills, and Cloudflare now enforces one.
- **Who checks the agent's work**: GitHub's [guide to agent skills](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/build-once-run-on-any-agent-a-practical-guide-to-agent-skills/) (a skill is a versioned directory around a SKILL.md file describing one repeatable task) runs a separate verifier over AI-written changes, and Stack Overflow's blog argues the same point under the title ["Your coding agent shouldn't grade its own homework"](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/11/13/your-coding-agent-shouldn-t-grade-its-own-homework/). ArtCraft, which has shipped [17 AI-built open source clones](https://latent.space/p/artcraft/) of popular apps, shows the gap: its founder put PhotoCraft just past 60% parity with Photoshop, while the project's own page says 76% for feature breadth and 46% for professional readiness. Why it matters: agents can now build whole projects, so verification is becoming its own step with its own tooling.
- **Hardware around the chips**: Bloomberg's sources say Samsung is in talks to [buy Modine](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p4#a261113p4), a US maker of data center cooling systems valued at $5.6B, and South Korea's DeepX taped out [DX-M2](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p8#a261113p8), an edge chip meant to run 100B-parameter models on robots and drones in under 5 watts, with mass production targeted for fall 2027. Why it matters: money is moving to the parts around the GPU (cooling, on-device silicon) while Nvidia sorts out who runs it.

## Security & Privacy

- **Project Zero shortens the clock**: Google's bug-hunting team dropped its "90 + 30" policy (90 days to fix, 30 more for patches to roll out) for [a flat deadline](https://projectzero.google/2026/11/disclosure-policy.html): details go public 90 days after the report, falling to 60 days on February 2, 2027 and to ==30 days== on January 3, 2028, with grace periods removed. Its reasoning: LLMs let attackers find the same bugs faster and reverse a patch into an exploit almost immediately, and the team now finds several vulnerabilities a week in targets that once yielded a few a year. Why it matters: Project Zero's deadlines set the norm other researchers follow, so vendors have about a year to learn to patch three times faster.
- **Another Windows zero-day, published out of spite**: Nightmare Eclipse, a researcher in a public feud with Microsoft, disclosed [SignDrop](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/windows-0-day-dropped-by-disgruntled-researcher-hands-hackers-system-rights/), a flaw in the Common Log File System that lets an ordinary local user gain full System rights on some Windows versions by making Windows Defender open a crafted log file. Why it matters: it was published as a zero-day, with no fix ready, and it turns the built-in antivirus into the route to full control of a machine.
- **North Korea's haul, partly clawed back**: the Justice Department has court authorization to [seize or restrain $1.2B](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p61#a261112p61) of the $2.7B that Tether and Circle froze after the $6.3B Binance hack of October 29. The FBI separately says North Korean hackers stole [more than $400M in 18 months](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p63#a261112p63) through FaceStealer, a scheme built on fake video calls and AI deepfakes. Why it matters: stablecoin issuers can freeze funds in a way bitcoin cannot, and that made them the one recovery route in the largest crypto theft on record.
- **A 22-year-old Postgres bug, taken to code execution**: researcher Mehmet Ince published how he turned a type-confusion flaw (the database treating one kind of data as another) into [remote code execution, CVE-2026-6460](https://mehmetince.net/part-3-6-how-i-ended-up-turning-a-22-year-old-postgresql-type-confusion-primitive-into-rce-cve-2026-6460/). Why it matters: Postgres sits under a large share of production software, so check your version against the CVE.
- **OpenAI sells defense**: OpenAI's [new security products](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/12/openai-launches-security-products-tells-orgs-theyre-doomed-if-they-dont-use-them/5309500) are Daybreak Red Team, which tests web apps for flaws, and Daybreak Defense Factory, which automates finding and fixing them. Why it matters: an AI lab is now selling defense against the attacks AI enables, in a week with no shortage of them.

Why it matters: one company's filing contains the whole loop of the AI buildout, with Nvidia financing a customer that buys Nvidia chips to serve Anthropic.
- **Mirendil raises $1B**: the lab founded by former Anthropic researchers to build self-improving AI closed a ==$1B Series A== [led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p74#a261112p74), six months after a $200M seed. Why it matters: a billion-dollar first priced round says investors will fund AI that does AI research at frontier-lab scale before there is a product.

Kalshi, warned of possible CFTC charges earlier this week, is in talks to raise at a [$65B valuation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/kalshi-in-talks-for-new-funding-round-at-65-billion-valuation) (paywalled), and Chinese AI server maker xFusion won approval for a [$1.2B Shenzhen IPO](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p6#a261113p6). Why it matters: network gear demand and private valuations are holding up through Nvidia's worst week.

## Research

- **Agents that do too much, or too little**: [SideTaskBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14548) pairs 25 Python repairs with unrequested side tasks planted in third-party references; two models delivered the unrequested work about 40% of the time while still fixing the bug, so a passing repair hides the failure. A second paper shows the opposite attack: fake risk warnings planted in low-trust content [make agents refuse legitimate tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14864), and a caution-first prompt raised Gemini's wrongful refusals from 9.8% to 86.3%. Why it matters: both come from agents trusting text they were only meant to read, and telling an agent to be careful made the second attack worse.
- **Arena rankings can be gamed**: a paper on [leaderboard hacking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14769) finds vote-based model rankings open to strategic voting, prompting and model nomination, each worth up to 15 to 20 places and up to 45 combined. It lands as Kaggle launches [Arenas](https://alphasignal.ai/news/kaggle-arenas-puts-ai-models-to-a-blind-public-vote), a blind public vote across charts, websites, video and ASCII art. Why it matters: arena scores are quoted in launch announcements, and the method is easier to manipulate than its popularity suggests.

## Elsewhere

- **AI advice for every US air traffic center**: the FAA is expanding SMART, an $875M system that suggests reroutes to controllers, [from Washington, DC to all 22 centers](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/faa-expands-ai-advice-to-manage-air-traffic-congestion-across-entire-us-airspace/), crediting it with rerouting 180 flights since September 21. Airlines back the rollout; the controllers' union says it was not involved in designing or testing it. Why it matters: a nationwide rollout rests on 180 flights of evidence, and the people who must act on the advice had no hand in building it.
- **Meta and the teen-harm cases**: Bloomberg's sources say confidential settlements with young plaintiffs have [mostly ranged from $2M to $2.5M](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p73#a261112p73). Court filings also show Meta staff discussed a 2023 "Whisper strategy" to turn academics against Jonathan Haidt, and The Verge finds the reveal has [annoyed even his critics](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1032645/jonathan-haidt-critics-response-meta). Why it matters: a known price per case lets everyone estimate what the remaining suits could cost, and the filings keep adding to the bill.
- **Friday the 13th, as scheduled**: a 1960 paper in Science by physicist Heinz von Foerster extrapolated population growth to a breaking point on November 13, 2026; Ars explains [why the math never held](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/11/its-the-end-of-the-world-as-we-know-it-per-a-physicists-1960-prediction/) (it assumed past growth rates would continue unchanged). The real event of the day is SpaceX's attempt at the [first full recovery of an orbital rocket](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/rocket-report-starship-set-for-historic-test-flight-a-century-of-electron/): a Starship launch Friday and an upper stage landing attempt 22.5 hours later. Why it matters: recovering both stages for reuse is the step Starship's economics depend on.
