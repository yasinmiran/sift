**What matters today:** yesterday Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO; overnight the story became Washington's. Senate Banking chair Tim Scott [asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p70#a261112p70) at separate December hearings, and the New York Times' sources say President Trump [urged Huang to keep fighting for his job](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p66#a261112p66) two days before he quit. A forensic analysis claims six open models from Chinese labs [carry the fingerprints of export-controlled Blackwell GPUs](https://unemploymentlabs.com/checkpoint-forensics), which would put evidence behind the question Congress is asking. Away from Nvidia, Google made [Gemini 4 Hydrogen](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1032505/google-search-ai-mode-gemini-4-hydrogen) the default model in Search's AI Mode, and a lab of former Anthropic researchers raised a $1B Series A.

## AI / LLMs

- **Gemini 4 Hydrogen, the new default**: Google's third Gemini 4 model [launched yesterday](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1032505/google-search-ai-mode-gemini-4-hydrogen) in Search, the Gemini app, AI Studio, the API and Gemini for business; it is now the default in AI Mode and handles the hardest questions in AI Overviews. Google pitches it at multi-part and oddly specific questions, and as the fast execution model that pairs with its larger siblings in coding work. Simon Willison [finds the pricing confusing](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/12/llm-gemini/): Hydrogen costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 output, only 25% under Carbon and Argon until their prices double on December 1, and ==15 times== the $0.10/$0.50 of Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna. Why it matters: Hydrogen is also the new free-tier model, so this is the Gemini most people will actually meet, and it is priced well above rivals' small models.
- **Chinese models, American chips**: an analysis by Will DePue at Unemployment Labs says six open-weight models released since April by DeepSeek, Moonshot, MiniMax and Ant Group [carry fingerprints of Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs](https://unemploymentlabs.com/checkpoint-forensics), which cannot legally be sold to China. The claim is one group's forensic reading of published model files and has not been independently confirmed. Why it matters: if it holds, the export debate gets its first evidence drawn from the models themselves instead of from shipping records and sources.
- **Muse makes phone calls**: Meta's consumer agent can now [place calls to US businesses](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p1#a261113p1) on a user's behalf, for paid US subscribers, after a test that began in September; Gemini and the startup Instinct offer something similar. Why it matters: phone calls reach the businesses that have no API and no booking page, so agents get there without anyone's permission.
- **Open weights, one day's batch**: Edge0 released [Edge0-145B](https://edge0.ai/blog/introducing-edge0-145b-frontier-ai-capabilities-on-your-own-computer), an open 145B-parameter model it bills as frontier capability on your own computer (the company's claim). Salesforce's [SOV-Transcribe-1](https://alphasignal.ai/news/salesforce-s-sov-transcribe-1-beats-every-open-weight-speech-model-at-2-3-error) tops the open speech leaderboard at a 2.3% word error rate, MiniMax's 33B [H3-Image](https://alphasignal.ai/news/minimax-drops-minimax-h3-image-a-33b-open-model-generating-16mp-photos-in-6) generates 16-megapixel images in 6 sampling steps, and [STDec](https://alphasignal.ai/news/stdec-makes-diffusion-llms-14x-faster-without-touching-model-weights) is a decoder that speeds up diffusion language models up to 14x with no retraining. Why it matters: speech, image and local chat each got a strong free option in a single day, which keeps pressure on paid APIs.

## Devtools & Infra

- **GitHub's October, counted**: the monthly [availability report](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-availability-report-october-2026/) says 140 of 151 production services and 32 of 50 MySQL primaries now run in Azure Central US, with compute there more than doubled. It also owns the failures: write outages on October 7 traced to an unsafe interaction between database orchestration and production routing, and an October 29 Azure network disruption that hit all three Central US availability zones and at least 34,128 signed-in users. The second region, East US 3, is a week behind plan. Why it matters: GitHub is mid-migration to Azure, and the report shows the move adding capacity and new failure modes at the same time.
- **Skills as portable agent instructions**: GitHub's [guide to agent skills](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/build-once-run-on-any-agent-a-practical-guide-to-agent-skills/) describes a skill as a versioned directory around a SKILL.md file that says when and how to do one repeatable task, kept separate from project config and from the credentials the host must supply. Its example runs a separate verifier over AI-written changes, on the rule that an agent checking its own work is not verification. In the same vein, Zed [rebuilt its Claude agent integration](https://alphasignal.ai/news/zed-rebuilds-claude-agent-integration-so-background-ai-work-stays-visible) on version 2 of the Agent Client Protocol so background agent work stays visible in the editor. Why it matters: instructions and editor plumbing are both settling into formats that work across agents, which lowers the cost of switching between them.
- **ArtCraft wants to rebuild everything**: at the AI Engineer Code Summit, founder Brandon Thomas said the company has shipped [17 open source clones of popular apps](https://latent.space/p/artcraft/), built by AI in Rust, led by the Photoshop alternative PhotoCraft, with about 1.7 million downloads a day and some 4,000 contributors. He put PhotoCraft just past 60% parity with Photoshop; the project's own GitHub page says about 76% for feature breadth and 46% for professional readiness. Why it matters: it is the largest live test of whether agents can reproduce mature commercial software, and the parity numbers show how far "a good approximation in 24 hours" is from finished.
- **Inference hardware**: Cerebras reported a [record $210M quarter](https://alphasignal.ai/news/cerebras-hits-record-210m-as-cloud-inference-revenue-surges-272), with two new hyperscaler customers and 700 megawatts of capacity secured, and vLLM, the open source model server, [retuned DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash](https://alphasignal.ai/news/vllm-rebuilds-deepseek-v4-1-flash-serving-to-run-58x-faster-on-rubin) for Nvidia's Vera Rubin rack, reporting 1.7M tokens per second per GPU. Why it matters: serving speed is where chip rivals and Nvidia's next generation are competing, whoever ends up running Nvidia.

## Security & Privacy

- **North Korea's haul, partly clawed back**: the Justice Department says it has court authorization to [seize or restrain $1.2B](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p61#a261112p61) of the $2.7B that Tether and Circle froze after the $6.3B Binance hack of October 29. Separately, the FBI says North Korean hackers stole [more than $400M in 18 months](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p63#a261112p63) through a scheme it calls FaceStealer, built on fake video calls and AI deepfakes. Why it matters: stablecoin issuers can freeze funds in a way bitcoin cannot, and that made them the one recovery route in the largest crypto theft on record.
- **A 22-year-old Postgres bug, taken to code execution**: researcher Mehmet Ince published how he turned a type-confusion flaw (the database treating one kind of data as another) that has sat in PostgreSQL for 22 years into [remote code execution, CVE-2026-6460](https://mehmetince.net/part-3-6-how-i-ended-up-turning-a-22-year-old-postgresql-type-confusion-primitive-into-rce-cve-2026-6460/). Why it matters: Postgres sits under a large share of production software, so check your version against the CVE.
- **Also**: OpenAI [launched security products](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/12/openai-launches-security-products-tells-orgs-theyre-doomed-if-they-dont-use-them/5309500) with a pitch The Register sums up as use them or be doomed, and Humble Bundle's redesign [removed the settings for two-factor verification](https://www.theverge.com/games/1032659/humble-bundle-redesign-two-factor-verification-security-settings), though The Verge's reporter was still asked for an emailed code at login. Why it matters: an AI lab is now selling defense against the attacks AI enables, and a storefront redesign shows how easily account protections get dropped by accident.

## Startups & Industry

- **Nvidia: Washington takes over**: yesterday Huang resigned and agreed to testify to Congress; today a Senate committee set out its own request. Banking chair Tim Scott [asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p70#a261112p70) to appear at separate December hearings, and Lutnick has agreed. Democrats [opened their own investigation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/democrats-launch-nvidia-investigation-write-scathing-letter-on-huangs-scheme-with-trump) with a letter alleging a "scheme" between Huang and Trump. The New York Times' sources say Trump [called Huang on Tuesday](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p66#a261112p66) urging him to fight for his job, with aides saying he saw Nvidia as essentially too big to fail, and that the closeness of Trump and Lutnick to Huang made officials [wary of reining in chip sales](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p67#a261112p67). CNN adds a caution: US officials [disagree on whether Alice Huang spied](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p72#a261112p72), and some at ODNI and the CIA say the intelligence so far does not prove it. Why it matters: the inquiry is widening from one executive's conduct to how the administration handled chip exports, while the espionage claim at the center remains unproven.
- **Nvidia: inside the company**: Wired reports employees are [petitioning to bring Huang back](https://www.wired.com/story/nvidia-employees-petition-for-jensen-huangs-return/). Bloomberg's sources describe [a penitent Huang](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p68#a261112p68) who says he no longer cares what happens to him. Bloomberg also reports that before his exit, Nvidia was under pressure to raise the 25% backstop it offered on a planned [$500B AI financing vehicle](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p69#a261112p69) with asset managers, a guarantee covering part of the chips' future resale value. Why it matters: the petition shows how much of Nvidia's identity was one person, and the financing vehicle is exactly the kind of deal a cautious interim CEO now has to decide on.
- **Mirendil raises $1B**: the lab founded by former Anthropic researchers to build self-improving AI closed a ==$1B Series A== [led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p74#a261112p74), six months after a $200M seed; the company frames the goal as [opening up frontier AI research](https://mirendil.com/news/series-a). Why it matters: a billion-dollar first priced round says investors will fund AI that does AI research at frontier-lab scale before there is a product.
- **Deals and earnings**: Cisco's quarterly revenue [rose 24% to $18.5B](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p64#a261112p64), beating estimates, and it raised its full-year forecast. ByteDance plans to [buy back up to $6B of shares](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p71#a261112p71) at about a $400B valuation, 20% above its 2025 buyback. Kalshi, warned of possible CFTC charges earlier this week, is in talks to raise at a [$65B valuation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/kalshi-in-talks-for-new-funding-round-at-65-billion-valuation) (paywalled). Nscale's updated IPO prospectus shows [a widening loss](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/nscale-updates-ipo-prospectus-that-shows-widening-loss.html), and Electrek relays a report that SpaceX could [announce a $1.5 trillion Tesla merger](https://electrek.co/2026/11/12/spacex-tesla-merger-announcement-december-report) in December, well ahead of the first-half 2027 timing reported in October. Why it matters: network gear demand and private valuations are holding up through Nvidia's week, even as an AI cloud heads to market with growing losses.

## Elsewhere

- **AI advice for every US air traffic center**: the FAA is expanding SMART, an $875M system that suggests reroutes to controllers, [from Washington, DC to all 22 centers](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/faa-expands-ai-advice-to-manage-air-traffic-congestion-across-entire-us-airspace/). The agency credits it with rerouting 180 flights since September 21, saving about 10 minutes and almost 80 gallons of fuel each. Airlines back the rollout; the controllers' union says it was not involved in designing or testing it. Why it matters: a nationwide rollout rests on 180 flights of evidence, and the people who must act on the advice had no hand in building it.
- **What teen addiction suits pay**: Bloomberg's sources say confidential settlements between social media companies and young plaintiffs have [mostly ranged from $2M to $2.5M](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p73#a261112p73), and Meta agreed to pay two teens a combined $7M in the past two weeks. Why it matters: a known price per case lets everyone estimate what the remaining personal injury suits could cost.
- **A social network that checks your pulse**: Whoop opened [Whoop Humans](https://www.theverge.com/gadgets/1032630/whoop-humans-social-network), a feed inside its app where wearers share metrics, activities and photo posts, and where it says every account belongs to a real person with a "verified heartbeat". Why it matters: proof of being human is turning into a product feature, here tied to a paid wearable.

- **Evidence for the export question**: the Blackwell fingerprint analysis lands the same day CNN reports the spying claim is unproven, so the technical allegation is getting firmer as the personal one gets weaker; vLLM tuning a DeepSeek model for Nvidia's next rack shows how entangled the two ecosystems already are.
