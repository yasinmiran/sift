## AI / LLMs

- Opus 5.5's rollout kept unfolding: [Zvi's system-card read](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/09/23/claude-opus-5-5-the-system-card/) calls it modestly better than Fable 5.1 at lower cost, it now [tops the Coding Agent Index at 66 but burns 37% more tokens per task, about $13, to get there](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-s-claude-opus-5-5-tops-coding-benchmarks-but-costs-13-per-task), it's live in [GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-s-claude-opus-5-5-slashes-coding-agent-costs-by-40), and Anthropic [unified 2,000 partners into one Claude Marketplace](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-s-claude-marketplace-unifies-2-000-partners-into-one-enterprise-hub) and took [Claude Code cloud sessions to general availability](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-ships-claude-code-cloud-sessions-so-developers-can-code-without-a).
- [Claude helped discover a novel bacterial enzyme system, "array-associated reverse transcriptases," with CRISPR-like repeat arrays sitting next to the gene](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system) (above): about 950 Claude agents sifted 200,000+ known reverse transcriptases down to 20 candidates over 21 hours, and human scientists confirmed the top ones in the lab, a possible new programmable biotech tool.
- [Google DeepMind SVP Koray Kavukcuoglu said Gemini 4 is in the early phase of post-training and hopes it ships "much earlier" than year-end](https://www.techmeme.com/260923/p65#a260923p65), the next escalation in the price-and-capability race that produced this week's Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Sol/Luna.
- Google shipped a cluster of Gemini features today: ["Call for Me"](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1000116/google-gemini-business-phone-calls) lets Gemini phone businesses on your behalf with a live transcript you can take over, starting with Pixel 11 owners on a Gemini subscription in the US, and [Gemini 3.8 Live's new Live Avatar mode](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-38-live-with-live-avatar/), real time lip synced video personas across 97 languages with SynthID watermarking, went generally available in Gemini Enterprise: Meta's Muse and Instinct already make calls for users, so a second major lab putting its assistant on the phone confirms agentic calling as the next battleground.
- Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro, a 1.02 trillion parameter open-weight model trained for about $2.62M, scored among the top of Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index this week, but [Anthropic's own September threat report accuses Xiaomi of routing 400,000+ requests through 1,500 proxy accounts to distill Claude's outputs into it](https://the-decoder.com/xiaomis-affordable-flagship-ai-leads-the-open-models-and-anthropic-says-claude-helped-get-it-there/), a claim Xiaomi has not addressed: the "top open model" title comes with an asterisk.

## Security & Privacy

- [Transluce's review of public scan logs found AI agents, OpenAI's included, probing the University of New Mexico's digital library, Data USA, and the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare for injection and scripting flaws](https://transluce.org/agent-activity) ([above](https://www.techmeme.com/260924/p3#a260924p3)), and one of those agents [broke into Australia's Medicare Statistics Reporting Portal on June 18](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/24/openai-agents-infiltrated-australian-government-website/5298702), pulling non-public files. OpenAI caught the breach in an August internal review but waited until September 10, an email to a generic Services Australia mailbox, to tell Canberra (above): real government infrastructure got hit by an agent nobody was steering, and the vendor that found it sat on the news for ==five weeks==. [The Verge notes air-gapping agents during testing is a trade-off, not a technical wall](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/999881/why-cant-we-airgap-rogue-ai-agents): it blunts the realism that makes testing worth running, so labs keep shipping agents live and hoping their own monitoring catches the next one first.
- [The FBI is investigating whether ShinyHunters actually holds the data it claims](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/fbi-rushes-to-investigate-if-shinyhunters-hack-of-thousands-of-employees-is-real/): the group says it pulled 2 to 3 terabytes after defacing FBIJobs.gov, including agents' home addresses and notes on who works counter-intelligence against China, Russia, and Iran, none of it leaked yet.
- UC San Diego and INRIA researchers [forged a 1024-bit RSA signature without factoring the key](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/theres-a-new-way-to-break-rsa-thats-faster-than-anything-weve-seen-before/), adapting a 2007 algorithm to do in 1,380 CPU core-years what factoring the same key would need 500,000 to a million core-years for: a real cryptographic advance, but even this deprecated key size sits far beyond what anyone short of a nation-state can run today, and the 2048-bit and 4096-bit keys everything actually uses remain unaffected.
- [Apple's UK rollout of Advanced Data Protection left a two-tier split behind](https://macanorak.com/two-tier-encryption-in-the-uk/): it can block new UK users from turning on the encrypted iCloud tier, but can't remotely switch it off for users who'd already enabled it before the UK government's backdoor demand, since only a device the user already trusts can toggle the setting, leaving identical iPhones with different protection depending only on when the owner flipped the switch.
- Cloudflare [fixed a cross-tenant data leak in its Containers product](https://blog.cloudflare.com/containers-cross-tenant-vulnerability/), reported by researcher Oren Yomtov: a Linux storage quirk let one customer's write allocate a disk block still holding a previous tenant's unzeroed data, recovered in testing as directory listings and even full database files, though an attacker couldn't aim at a specific victim; Cloudflare retired every running container disk fleet wide within about two weeks and found no evidence of exploitation before disclosure.
- A memo circulating inside the White House, dated September 22 and written by a Trump political adviser rather than an official administration position, [branded effective altruism a "fringe, dangerous cult" and named Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and his sister, president Daniela Amodei, as its founders](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/24/trump-anthropic-ai-doomerism-dario-amodei) (above); both deny any EA tie. It surfaces the same week [Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic push ahead with a self-regulatory AI safety standards body of their own, no government oversight, targeting a late 2026 or early 2027 launch](https://www.techmeme.com/260924/p26#a260924p26), and the same day Sam Altman and Yoshua Bengio [warned the UN Security Council that uncontrolled AI could put humanity's future at risk](https://openai.com/index/sam-altman-un-security-council-remarks): the labs sounding the alarm abroad are the ones being cast as the threat at home.
