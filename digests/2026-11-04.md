**What matters today:** Election night put the AI buildout on the ballot: voters rejected data centers in [local measures](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1026365/data-centers-midterms-results), and CoreWeave's bonds [fell](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p39#a261104p39) after Democrats swept Congress. Binance published its postmortem of the [$6.3B theft](https://www.theverge.com/news/1025576/binance-cyrpto-theft-explained): a tampered installer from a Google ad in June, then a fake hiring process in September. OpenAI is [buying Pierre](https://openai.com/index/openai-to-acquire-pierre) to deepen Codex, and Canva is [cutting 22% of staff](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025971/canva-changes-layoffs).

## AI / LLMs

- **OpenAI buys Pierre**: OpenAI will [acquire Pierre Computer Company](https://openai.com/index/openai-to-acquire-pierre) to expand Codex's tools for building, understanding and reviewing code. An [exclusive report](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/exclusive-openai-targets-microsofts-github-112906674.html) frames the deal as aimed at Microsoft's GitHub, so code review is the next contested layer.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro goes open**: Moonshot published the [Kimi K3.1 Pro weights](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro), and [AlphaSignal relays](https://alphasignal.ai/news/moonshot-ai-opens-kimi-k3-1-pro-weights-to-beat-claude-on-coding-agents) the claim that it beats Claude on coding agents. That is Moonshot's own benchmark framing; the weights are what you can test yourself.
- **Google reverses course on schools**: After backlash, Google says schools can enable [Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search individually](https://www.techmeme.com/261103/p44#a261103p44), per CNBC. Yesterday's rule tied Search to the AI features; the new one removes the all-or-nothing choice, though the early 2027 restrictions tooling is still ahead.
- **Claude Code's product lead leaves**: Cat Wu, who led product for Claude Code and Cowork, has [left Anthropic](https://www.techmeme.com/261103/p43#a261103p43), per Business Insider; Thariq Shihipar, also of Claude Code, announced his exit last month. Two departures from one product team a day after the IPO filing's risk language is worth watching.
- **Revolut cuts coding-AI cost 90%**: Revolut says it cut AI coding inference costs [about 90% in under a year](https://www.techmeme.com/261103/p36#a261103p36) by making self-hosted GLM models its default over commercial tools like Claude Code, per The Register. Those are the company's own figures; the saving shows how fast "good enough and owned" competes with the frontier price list.
- **Uber's agent traffic is tiny**: Uber says AI agents arranged only ==4== [rides in Q3](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1026306/uber-ai-agents-q3-earnings-disintermediation), and about 500 Eats orders in Halloween week out of 320 million trips and deliveries. The "agents will disintermediate apps" thesis has almost no volume behind it yet.
- **Leaderboard refresh**: Per [AlphaSignal's summary](https://alphasignal.ai/news/artificial-analysis-rebuilds-its-ai-capability-indexes-crowning-claude-opus-5-5), Artificial Analysis rebuilt its six domain Capability Indexes to track hallucination control and agentic tool use, with Claude Opus 5.5 on top. Index methodology changes reshuffle rankings, so read the headline as one benchmark's view.

## Devtools & Infra

- **Cloudflare watches personal agents**: Cloudflare launched [analytics and finer access policies](https://blog.cloudflare.com/personal-agents-visibility-control/) for personal agents that browse sites on a person's behalf. Site owners can allow helpful agents instead of choosing between blocking everything and seeing nothing.
- **Russia moves data centers underground**: After Ukrainian drone strikes hit facilities run by Yandex, Sberbank, VKontakte and the finance ministry, Russian firms are [looking to hide data centers underground](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/russia-looks-to-hide-data-centers-underground-as-ukrainian-drones-strike-back/), per Ars Technica. Rostelecom has discussed mines with Norilsk, and the Kremlin now requires owners to reserve capacity for emergency state needs. Cloud infrastructure has become a military target on both sides.
- **Jet engines for power**: Data centers are [turning to old jet engines](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/data-centers-are-turning-to-old-jet-engines-as-the-wait-for-power-grows) as grid hookups lag, per TechCrunch. Power, not chips, is setting the build schedule.

## Security & Privacy

- **Binance's postmortem**: Binance says the intrusion started in June when one developer [downloaded a tampered installer](https://www.theverge.com/news/1025576/binance-cyrpto-theft-explained) spread by a malicious Google ad, exposing details of its engineers. In September another engineer was courted through multiple rounds of fake interviews and handed a fake assignment to download; Binance also says an internal AI tool played a role. [Ars Technica adds](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/11/binance-says-hackers-who-stole-billions-in-crypto-spent-months-planning-heist/) that $6.34B was targeted, $2.85B has been frozen or intercepted, and the FBI and TRM attribute it to North Korean actors.
- **PHP bug was known for two years**: A [post-mortem on the php2shell exploit chain](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cyber-security/php-vulnerability-post-mortem-reveals-bug-was-known-two-years-ago-small-team-of-maintainers-knew-of-the-issue-before-it-was-made-public-as-new-php2shell-chained-exploit-ravages-servers) says a small team of maintainers knew of the flaw before it became public, while the chained exploit hits servers. Thinly staffed core projects are now a front line.
- **Blood fridges hacked in France**: The French Blood Establishment says [external providers' cold storage units were hacked](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p31#a261104p31) to raise temperatures, part of a wave of refrigeration cyberattacks, per CNBC. Attackers are going after physical systems that spoil what they hold.
- **CrowdStrike's AI kill switch**: CrowdStrike introduced an [AI Kill Switch](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/crowdstrike-introduces-ai-kill-switch) for halting AI agents that misbehave. It follows yesterday's Fiddler and Dreadnode purchases, so the vendor is now selling the control layer it just bought.
- **Postal Service watched protests**: Records released in EFF's FOIA suit show the USPS internet Covert Operations Program (iCOP) [monitored social media posts about protests](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/11/postal-service-surveilled-protest-activities-online-little-rules-oversight) in 2020 and 2021, across the political spectrum, with little documented oversight. One bulletin warned of possible violence while admitting it had no intelligence of specific threats.

## Startups & Industry

- **Sequoia reshapes**: Sequoia raised about $10B in new funds and is [shutting its Arc accelerator and Scouts program](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p44#a261104p44), merging early-stage and growth teams, per TechCrunch. It is also [closing its London office](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p30#a261104p30) after six years, so the firm is pulling back to the Bay Area.
- **Canva restructures**: Canva says restructuring affects ==1,271== people, or [22% of staff](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025971/canva-changes-layoffs), as it merges product, growth and platform teams; the CEO calls it a "refounding" toward small teams, and some roles will relocate to hubs. The Age ties it to slowing growth.
- **Revolut at $115B**: A secondary share sale led by Glade Brook and Coatue [values Revolut at $115B](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p33#a261104p33), up from $75B a year ago, as it plans a US bank launch, per TechCrunch.
- **Record AI-hardware quarters**: AMD reported record [$13.4B revenue, $8.5B of it data center](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1026148/amd-earnings-q3-2026-data-center-pc), and guided to $17B next quarter and 80%+ growth in 2027. Super Micro [reported revenue up 298% to $19.95B](https://www.techmeme.com/261103/p35#a261103p35) against $16.39B expected. Server demand is still outrunning forecasts.
- **Debt keeps funding the buildout**: Meta raised [$15B in a dollar bond sale](https://www.techmeme.com/261103/p47#a261103p47) with up to $58B in orders, per Bloomberg. TeraWulf secured a [Google credit backstop](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p36#a261104p36) for its Kentucky data center project with Anthropic, due online in the second half of 2027, per Reuters.
- **Qualcomm and SpaceX**: Qualcomm signed a multiyear deal to supply hardware and network software for [tens of thousands of cell sites](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p29#a261104p29) in a US-wide SpaceX 5G network planned for 2027, per Bloomberg.
- **Traversal raises $120M**: The SRE agent startup raised [$120M at a $1B valuation](https://www.techmeme.com/261103/p39#a261103p39) led by Kleiner Perkins, Redpoint and Khosla, per Forbes, for AI that troubleshoots outages at firms like American Express and Pepsi.
- **Nvidia's soft power**: The Information reports Nvidia [shapes customers and US chip policy](https://www.techmeme.com/261103/p42#a261103p42) informally; sources say OpenAI took no public stance on China chip exports to avoid angering it. Pairs with yesterday's China 49% cap report.

## Policy

- **Election reprices AI politics**: Polling by NBC found [76% of Election Day voters opposed data centers](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1026365/data-centers-midterms-results) in their communities, dozens of localities passed restrictions, and winners like Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa back moratoriums. Crypto and AI super PACs spent [$250M and lost 9 of 12](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p35#a261104p35) competitive races called so far, per Molly White. Transformer argues the sweep [raises the odds of frontier AI legislation](https://www.techmeme.com/261104/p40#a261104p40) in early 2027, including mandatory testing and developer liability.
- **California keeps its net neutrality law**: California will accept [$1.42B in broadband funding](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/california-fights-preposterous-trump-admin-demand-to-scrap-state-broadband-laws/) but rejects the federal condition that it stop enforcing net neutrality and rate rules, per Ars Technica. Legal fights with Washington and ISPs are likely, and the grants could be delayed.

## Threads

- **Voters meet the buildout**: The midterm data center results, CoreWeave's bond drop, the Transformer legislation forecast and the Nikkei debt story all say financing and politics now gate AI infrastructure.
- **Attack chains start with a person**: Binance's tampered installer and fake interview, and the PHP maintainers who knew of a bug for two years, show the weak point is a human or small-team step, not the protocol.
- **Debt funds the buildout**: Meta's bond, TeraWulf's Google backstop, and AMD and Super Micro's record quarters are one story: spending flows from lenders to chips.
- **Power and war as limits**: Russia's underground data centers and jet engines for US data centers show physical constraints, not software, gating compute.
- **Coding agents get contested**: OpenAI's Pierre deal, Kimi K3.1 Pro's open weights and Revolut's GLM switch all pressure the commercial coding-tool price list, while Uber's four rides show consumer agents still lack volume.
- **Backlash moves policy**: Google's schools reversal, the election result and California's refusal on net neutrality all follow public pushback.
