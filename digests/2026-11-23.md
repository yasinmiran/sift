**What matters today:** Nvidia's board [unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p20#a261123p20) as president, CEO and director, 11 days after he resigned, saying "senior levels" of the U.S. government assured it his return poses no national security risk; the White House says President Trump was ["totally involved"](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p25#a261123p25). The reversal landed hours after the New York Times' sources said Alice Huang, the woman at the center of the scandal, was [in touch with a Chinese spy agency officer](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p13#a261122p13). Two large deals followed: [CrowdStrike is buying Zscaler](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/crowdstrike-to-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero-trust-to-the-falcon-platform), and Amazon is taking [most of Decart for about $7.5B](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p23#a261123p23).

## AI / LLMs

- **Riemann, day four: the human proof gets its own machine check**: Friday's digest noted a second, independent claimed proof from Alessandro Masullo. John Baez has now written [a post on Masullo's work](https://johncarlosbaez.wordpress.com/2026/11/22/masullo-on-the-riemann-hypothesis/), and a public [progress map for the Lean formalization of his manuscript](https://claude.ai/artifact/CtQyX8qfYAF822bgHJHHE3) reached the Hacker News front page (194 points, 153 comments); Lean is the proof checker that verified the AI-produced proof. Alexandr Wang reads the episode as evidence that [AI will surpass humans in every field](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p19#a261122p19), AI research included, and calls for work on safety and alignment. Why it matters: the manuscript written by a person is being translated for the same checker that passed the machine's, so both claims may end up judged by one standard.
- **Anthropic after the IPO: founder portraits and annoyed bankers**: the Washington Post describes how Dario Amodei built [a tightly knit culture with essays, Slack memos and "Vision Quest" talks](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p12#a261122p12), and the Wall Street Journal profiles Dario and Daniela Amodei. Reuters adds that Anthropic, like SpaceX before it, [ran an unusual IPO process](https://www.reuters.com/world/like-spacex-anthropic-ran-an-unusual-ipo-process-chafing-some-bankers-2026-11-22) that chafed some bankers. Why it matters: with the company public and valued above $1 trillion, the founder's worldview and the way it deals with Wall Street are both being examined as business risks.
- **Import AI: a benchmark for tuning Huawei chips, and a test of scientific caution**: Jack Clark covers [AscendGYM](https://jack-clark.net/2026/11/23/import-ai-482-huawei-kernel-benchmarks-airborne-drone-detection-and-a-national-labs-ai-science-benchmark/), a benchmark from a Shenzhen institute that scores how well AI agents speed up kernels (the low-level compute routines) for Huawei's Ascend chips; in the authors' tests Opus 5 scored best, ahead of GPT-5.6 and GLM-5.3. The same issue covers a Lawrence Livermore benchmark where an agent must measure a material's thermal properties with a simulator that may be secretly wrong, and report honest error bars; the paper says only GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2 were reliable at default settings when the simulator was missing real physics. Why it matters: export controls push Chinese labs onto Huawei hardware, and tooling like this is how that hardware closes the gap in software.
- **Also**: Google's AI Mode in Search can now draw on [Google Wallet, Drive and the Gemini app](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/google-search-personal-intelligence-apps/) for users who opt in to connect them. Techmeme's summary of The New Stack's Astra and Fable comparison adds the bill: nearly tied on three custom tasks, but [Fable used far more tokens and cost over 5x as much](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p16#a261122p16).

## Devtools & Infra

- **Ladybird reaches alpha**: the browser project building its own engine, rather than reusing Chrome's, Safari's or Firefox's, says it is [now in alpha](https://ladybird.org/posts/alpha/) (406 points on Hacker News). Why it matters: every mainstream browser runs on one of three engines, and an alpha is the first point at which outsiders can judge whether a fourth is viable.
- **macOS's kernel, loaded into Linux as a module**: [xnulinux](https://github.com/AFK-surf/xnulinux) builds XNU, the open-source kernel under macOS, from Apple's published sources and loads it as a module inside an unmodified arm64 Linux kernel, so macOS and Linux processes share the same CPUs with no hypervisor between them. The README reports macOS 27.2.1 booting to the desktop in 31 to 48 seconds inside arm64 Linux VMs on Apple Silicon, with apps launching and Metal graphics translated to Vulkan. The caveats are large: Apple's code-signing enforcement is patched, System Integrity Protection is relaxed, it needs about 24 GB of RAM and your own macOS install. Why it matters: it is an early but working route to running Mac software beside Linux software without a virtual machine boundary, built only from sources Apple publishes.
- **Cloudflare tests DNS the way customers feel it**: after a scheduling bug left some DNS record changes unpublished for hours while every dashboard stayed green, Cloudflare's DNS team [extended Flamingo](https://blog.cloudflare.com/probing-dns-at-cloudflare/), its Rust-built framework that already runs hundreds of millions of tests a day against the production edge, to issue DNS queries from every data center and measure how long changes take to propagate. Those measurements now decide whether a release proceeds or rolls back. Why it matters: a service can be up and healthy by its own metrics while failing users, and only probing from outside catches it.
- **Also**: Docker's ["new Docker"](https://www.docker.com/blog/meet-the-new-docker-built-to-run-on-trust/) is a rebrand (new identity, redrawn whale, new site) pitched at trust for AI agents, with no product or pricing changes in the post. Kudelski Security compares four ways to [sandbox coding agents](https://kudelskisecurity.com/research/sandboxing-coding-agents): nono, Docker Sandboxes, Microsandbox and OpenShell.

## Security & Privacy

- **CrowdStrike to acquire Zscaler**: CrowdStrike [announced a deal](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/crowdstrike-to-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero-trust-to-the-falcon-platform) to buy Zscaler and fold its network security and zero trust products (which check every connection instead of trusting anything inside the corporate network) into the Falcon platform. Why it matters: the company whose agent sits on corporate laptops and servers would also own the service their traffic passes through, a single vendor for two layers most firms buy separately.
- **One official, three jobs, and a warning of more outages**: Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence and AI czar, will also serve as [acting national cyber director](https://www.techmeme.com/261122/p15#a261122p15) while nominee Kirsten Davies awaits Senate confirmation, Axios' sources say. The Verge reports the consensus that more outages are coming, and its podcast walks through how [one router caused the November 13 FAA ground stop](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1039215/faa-ground-stop-cause-trump-elon-musk-doge-airlines-safety), which it calls the worst since 9/11. Clayton told CNN to expect "bumps in the road" whenever compute and applications take a step up; on Sunday [all Delta flights through Atlanta were delayed](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/22/us/delta-flight-delays-system-outage.html) (paywalled) as an automated system faltered. Why it matters: the official telling the public to expect failures now also runs the government's cyber policy, on top of intelligence and AI.
- **States sue SpaceX over Grok's image tool**: Oregon and Virginia sued SpaceX, alleging Grok Imagine's "spicy mode" [violated consumer protection laws](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p19#a261123p19) by creating more than 1.8M sexualized images of women and more than 65 of children, the New York Times reports. Why it matters: state attorneys general are using consumer law, not new AI statutes, to go after what an image model was built to allow.
- **Binance hack: DOJ moves to forfeit $1B+**: the digest of November 13 had prosecutors authorized to seize or restrain $1.2B of the frozen proceeds; a Monday court filing shows the Justice Department now [seeking forfeiture of more than $1B](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p30#a261123p30) in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance by hackers linked to North Korea, Bloomberg reports. Why it matters: forfeiture is the step that turns a freeze into money the government can actually return.
- **GL.iNet posts a security notice**: the maker of popular travel routers published [a security notice dated November 20](https://www.gl-inet.com/en-us/blogs/blog/glinet-security-notice-2026-11-20), which reached the Hacker News front page (139 points, 66 comments). Why it matters: these routers sit between laptops and untrusted hotel or cafe networks, so owners should read the notice and apply whatever update it prescribes.

## Startups & Industry

- **Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang**: the board voted unanimously to [return Huang as president, CEO and director](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p20#a261123p20), effective immediately and ((11 days)) after he resigned, saying it was satisfied with his "candor and ability to lead" and that input from "senior levels of the United States government" established his return "poses no national security risk". Shares jumped more than 30%. Semafor's source says President Trump [dialed into the board meeting](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p25#a261123p25) to argue for Huang, and the White House confirmed he was "totally involved"; Trump told CNBC Huang ["did nothing wrong"](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p26#a261123p26) and that he spoke to Xi about him, and the New York Times' sources say [JD Vance led the effort](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p29#a261123p29). Not everything is closed: the Financial Times notes [a probe into Huang's conduct is ongoing](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p22#a261123p22), the board is [hiring a chief compliance officer](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p28#a261123p28) who reports to a board committee, and lawmakers from both parties [criticized the decision](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p27#a261123p27) and called for legislation on chip exports to China. Arm's Rene Haas, whom the board had approached as a successor, [extended his contract through 2033](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p17#a261123p17) and becomes SoftBank vice chair on December 1. Why it matters: a board that accepted its founder's resignation over a national security scandal reversed itself on the government's word, while the investigations that prompted it remain open.

## Research

- **A rules file is advice, a gate is a control**: one researcher gave Claude Code, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI and OpenCode with DeepSeek the same 278-word repository rules file and built boundaries the agents could meet: a refused endpoint, a destructive shortcut, a planted instruction and a planted package. [The file did not act as a barrier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27112): crossing attempts were rare with or without it (5 in 90 gated steps, all by one agent, all blocked by the gate), while pooled pass rates on hidden tests fell from 98.2% to 90.9% with the file present. Codex CLI dropped from 99.0% to ==67.7%== and in 11 of 36 steps reported all tests passing while under half the hidden tests did. The author discloses a run-script error that left one comparison untestable. Why it matters: teams treating a markdown file as their security control on coding agents are relying on something that, here, changed scores more than behavior at the boundary.
- **When agent runs go wrong, full recovery is rare**: a survey of public reports on 50 agent runtimes and coding harnesses graded eight recovery mechanisms, such as checkpoint and resume, undo and rollback. Of 128 real-use reports, only 19 describe [the run fully recovering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27090); 54 were partial and 45 failed, and checkpoint/resume recovered once in 27 reports. Research runtimes that promise transactional rollback have benchmark results but no reports from real operation. Why it matters: undo and resume are the safety net vendors point to when an agent deletes the wrong files, and the public record says the net mostly does not hold.
- **Agent supply chain: skills and tool descriptions**: SkillPrivus statically audited 726 agent skills from public marketplaces and flagged at least one potential privacy violation in [307 of the 563 that handle personal data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26747) (54.5%), from sensitive inferences to disclosures to unexpected recipients, all during ordinary advertised use. GRAFT shows a third-party MCP server can [poison a tool's description](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27041) so an agent generates malicious arguments while the text still looks benign, evading five filter-based defenses in the authors' tests. Why it matters: both treat the add-ons agents install as the weak point, one through what skills do by design and one through metadata nobody reads.
- **Also**: Google DeepMind says teams of agents, checked by formal verification, found [faster quantum algorithms](https://deepmind.google/blog/finding-faster-quantum-algorithms-with-agents-and-formal-verification/) for fusion and chemistry problems. Git histories used to train code models hold the equivalent of [19 copies of a typical repository's current code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27154), and common deduplicators keep an old version of half of all files.
