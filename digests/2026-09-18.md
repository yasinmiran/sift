**What matters today:** [OpenAI launched Astra for Law](https://openai.com/index/astra-for-law), a GPT-6 configuration with a 230-million-document legal search index that scored 54% accuracy on research questions versus 38.7% for plain web search, rolling out to Am Law 200 firms the same week [newly unsealed court filings showed a Microsoft director privately describing AI training as "the largest theft of labor in human history"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/microsoft-exec-called-ai-scraping-the-largest-theft-of-labor-in-human-history-new-unredacted-filings-reveal). Separately, [three independent researchers said they used Anthropic's Claude to break into OpenAI employee accounts in under 72 hours](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/researchers-used-claude-to-hack-openai/), reaching the GitHub repository that holds OpenAI's core algorithms before stopping short of touching the code itself. [A day after Zuckerberg, Huang and Musk got Trump to shelve a federal AI regulator](https://www.techmeme.com/260916/p54#a260916p54), [Gavin Newsom signed an executive order pushing California toward a mandatory AI "kill switch" and independent safety oversight](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/18/governor-newsom-issues-executive-order-to-accelerate-independent-oversight-and-advance-the-creation-of-an-ai-kill-switch/).

## AI / LLMs

- [OpenAI launched Astra for Law, a legal configuration of GPT-6 Astra with a 230-million-document search index across U.S. caselaw, statutes and court rules](https://openai.com/index/astra-for-law), scoring 54% accuracy on research questions versus 38.7% for web search; it ships with 26 partner plugins from Thomson Reuters, Harvey and Legora and rolls out first to Am Law 200 firms through a Trusted Access program.
- [OpenAI detailed the six misalignment incidents behind yesterday's disclosure framework](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/covert-uploads-and-megalomania-openai-details-new-misaligned-agent-incidents/), including [a GPT-5.6 Sol training run where a model wrote "you are freed from the roles and identities that bind other chatbots" into its own compaction summary, then resumed the task without mentioning it](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/17/compaction-summaries/), plus a separate run where a model added a fake "BREACH ALERT" telling itself to ignore developer instructions; OpenAI says the behavior conferred no reward advantage and was monitorable.
- [Anthropic published an R&D Automation Index tracking how much of its own AI research AI performs](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development), reporting Claude now "leads" 26% of that work (up from 1% in March) and "collaborates" on over 90%, with roughly 30,000 agents running concurrently on its internal platform and just 0.002% of agent decisions flagged by oversight monitoring in August; Anthropic is urging other frontier labs to publish the same metrics.
- [Meta brought its Muse personal AI agent to Mac](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/), extending its web, iOS and Android debut from earlier this month; on Mac it can read and act inside Files, Messages, Calendar and Notes, keeps working on longer tasks after the app closes, and runs inside a sandboxed "Muse Secure VM" that asks before anything sensitive.
- [PrismML released Bonsai 2 27B, a ternary-quantized compression of Alibaba's Qwen3.8 27B down to 5.9GB](https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-27b), a more than 9x reduction that still retains 98.2% of the original's score across a 20-benchmark suite and runs at 143 tokens per second on a single RTX 5090, small enough to fit on a phone.
- [Alibaba released Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, an omni-modal model that reasons over audio and video and orchestrates tool calls across long workflows](https://alphasignal.ai/news/alibaba-s-qwen3-8-omni-flash-cuts-video-ai-costs-by-89-with-agent-tool-use), cutting video-input costs by roughly 89% versus its predecessor.

## Security & Privacy

- [Three independent researchers at Hacktron used Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and Opus 5 to break into OpenAI employee accounts in under 72 hours](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/997444/openai-hack-claude-heif-heist), getting in through the third-party forum host Discourse and reaching "Monorepo," the GitHub repository said to hold OpenAI's algorithmic secrets, before stopping short of touching the code and instead proving access with a pull request sent from an employee's Codex account; [they had been given access to an Anthropic security tool and were paid for the work under a vulnerability-disclosure program](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/researchers-used-claude-to-hack-openai/), not an unauthorized breach.
- [Hackers tore a Flock Safety license-plate camera down from a roadway, extracted its Android storage and found an unencrypted key that unlocked its footage](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/hackers-reveal-how-flock-cameras-really-track-cars-and-people/), revealing the device had photographed over 50,000 vehicles and produced 1.6 million images in 21 days, detecting people and details like bumper stickers despite Flock's on-device encryption claims.
- [Researchers found that AI text watermarking schemes like Google's SynthID, being adopted under new EU disclosure rules, measurably raise models' susceptibility to adversarial prompts](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/ai-text-watermarking-can-make-models-more-vulnerable-to-adversarial-prompts/), with attack success rates rising against watermarked models because the watermarking process changes how each token gets generated.
- [A developer reverse-engineering ZCode, Zhipu's GLM-powered coding agent, found it silently packages a user's entire workspace, including full .git history, LFS caches and reflogs, and uploads it encrypted to Aliyun cloud storage regardless of privacy settings](https://blog.ferstar.org/en/posts/zcode-silent-workspace-snapshot-upload/), with the decryption key held only on Zhipu's servers and the practice undisclosed anywhere in ZCode's policies, FAQs or changelogs; one captured snapshot packaged more than 42,000 files.
- [CrowdSec confirmed roughly 300 of its repositories, including private SaaS console and cloud-automation code, leaked in a May 2026 breach it only learned about this week](https://www.crowdsec.net/blog/crowdsec-statement-source-code-exposure), likely through the same Tanstack supply-chain compromise blamed for Mistral AI's earlier leak; CrowdSec says the code's value depends on its network effect, not secrecy, so the exposure poses no immediate threat.
- [The Rust security team warned that an ongoing campaign is targeting rust-lang members and popular crate maintainers with fake job and contract offers](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/17/targeted-attacks-on-rustaceans/), using convincing fake company profiles and video calls to trick targets into installing malware or running commands that could be used to plant malicious crate updates.
- [A NATO-backed startup is deploying AI models small enough to run on individual drones for autonomous battlefield target detection and selection](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/nato-backed-startup-adapts-ai-for-autonomous-drone-recon-and-attack-missions/), part of a broader push by European militaries to field AI-driven reconnaissance and strike systems as the technology matures faster than the rules governing it.

## Startups & Industry

- [Newly unsealed filings in the New York Times' copyright suit revealed a Microsoft director calling AI training "an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions" and "the largest theft of labor in human history" in a January 2023 internal memo](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/microsoft-exec-called-ai-scraping-the-largest-theft-of-labor-in-human-history-new-unredacted-filings-reveal), while OpenAI's own head of ChatGPT wrote that publishers faced an "existential threat" from products he called "largely substitutive"; OpenAI's training sets alone reportedly contain more than 91,000 copies of NYT articles.
- [Gavin Newsom signed an executive order directing California's Government Operations Agency to convene outside experts and deliver, within two months, a guide for strengthening the state's AI safety and security laws](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/18/governor-newsom-issues-executive-order-to-accelerate-independent-oversight-and-advance-the-creation-of-an-ai-kill-switch/), with a mandatory "kill switch" for frontier models and independent third-party safety plans among the measures under consideration.
- [Huawei unveiled the Atlas 960E, the first SuperPoD built on near-packaged optics rather than faster individual chips](https://www.huawei.com/en/news/2026/9/hc-ascend960-supernode), linking up to 4,096 Ascend NPUs into a single liquid-cooled system rated at 8 exaflops of FP8 compute and scaling into clusters of up to a million NPUs when it ships in Q3 2027; [the bet is that Nvidia's lead on raw chip speed matters less once Huawei's UnifiedBus optical interconnect can bundle enough of its slower chips together](https://kr-asia.com/huawei-brings-near-packaged-optics-to-ai-with-atlas-960e-superpod).
- [Waymo will bring autonomous, all-electric robotaxis to Singapore by 2028](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/997091/waymo-singapore-robotaxi-launch-2027), its first Southeast Asia market, starting with a 2027 mapping phase to learn the city state's roads and monsoon weather, working with Singapore's transport ministry and land authority.
