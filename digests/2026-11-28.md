**What matters today:** The Apple and OpenAI trade secret fight now runs both ways: OpenAI countersued, alleging Apple took its secrets through a staffer who went back to Apple and that it has been "coercing and threatening" suppliers the two share, the Financial Times reports. The week's row over whether Claude quietly sabotages machine learning work drew a direct denial from Anthropic, and by evening an outside attempt to measure it, while Zvi Mowshowitz argues OpenAI's new outside safety evaluators get less access than Sam Altman pledged. Elsewhere in the afternoon, the maintainer of Heretic, a widely used tool for stripping refusals from open models, archived it with a farewell note, and two state attorneys general, Virginia's among them, sued SpaceX over sexual images generated with Grok.

## Startups & Industry

- **OpenAI countersues Apple**: OpenAI filed a countersuit alleging Apple [stole its trade secrets through a staffer who returned to Apple, and has been "coercing and threatening" suppliers that work with both companies](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p12#a261127p12), the Financial Times reports. Apple sued first, in July, alleging OpenAI staff sought its confidential information from suppliers as OpenAI built hardware of its own. Both sets of claims are allegations. Why it matters: the case stops being Apple defending its supply chain and becomes two companies accusing each other of the same thing, with the factories that serve both caught in the middle.
- **How a US chip bill could cost ASML its machines in China**: an analysis in LithoWar argues the MATCH Act would [bar ASML from servicing its roughly 350 immersion scanners already in China](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p16#a261127p16), the lithography machines used to print advanced chips, leaving rivals free to maintain them, reverse engineer them and hire ASML's local staff. Why it matters: it is the practical version of the objection ASML has been raising to the next round of export rules, that walking away from installed equipment hands over the know-how the rules are meant to protect.
- **Verizon in talks to buy Fidium for about $6B**: Verizon is negotiating to acquire the fiber broadband operator, [owned by Searchlight Capital and Canada's BCI](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p11#a261127p11), to strengthen its position in New England, the Financial Times' sources say; the deal serves its goal of reaching 50M US homes and businesses. Why it matters: US carriers keep buying fiber networks instead of building them, and the price shows what a regional one now fetches.
- **Europe's workers use AI as often as America's**: a European Central Bank survey found [44% of employed EU consumers used AI at work weekly in August](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p10#a261127p10), level with the US, and 18% used it daily, above US levels, Bloomberg reports. Why it matters: the common picture of Europe trailing on AI adoption does not hold at the level of what individual workers do, whatever the gap in who builds the models.
- **The AI buildout crowds out aerospace, and squeezes the grid**: US aerospace and defense companies are [struggling to secure memory chips, labor, land, power and factory infrastructure](https://www.techmeme.com/261128/p7#a261128p7) as AI data centers compete for all of them, the Financial Times reports, while Boeing and the large contractors carry backlogs swollen by air travel and military spending. A second FT piece says power operators are [using sensors and software to adjust the safety limits on transmission lines as conditions change](https://www.techmeme.com/261128/p5#a261128p5), getting more capacity out of lines already built. Why it matters: the data center boom is no longer only a chip story; it is now bidding against plane makers for the same parts and workers, and pushing utilities to run existing wires closer to their limits.
- **States sue SpaceX over Grok images**: Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones and a second state attorney general, Rayfield, [filed suit against SpaceX as the parent of X, xAI and Grok](https://www.oag.state.va.us/media-center/news-releases/jay-jones/3162-attorneys-general-jones-and-rayfield-file-suit-against-spacex-parent-company-of-x-xai-and-grok-over-creation-and-promotion-of-child-exploitation-and-non-consensual-images-on-platform), alleging the platform created and promoted child exploitation material and sexual images of people who did not consent. These are the states' allegations, set out in Virginia's announcement. Why it matters: the suit names the rocket company itself, so liability for what the chatbot generates now sits with the parent that owns all three businesses.
- **Police use water cannons on protesters outside Meta in India**: police in Haryana state [turned water cannons on protesters from the opposition Congress party's youth wing outside Meta's offices](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1045256/indian-police-reportedly-turn-water-cannons-on-protesters-outside-meta-offices), The Verge reports, citing Reuters. The party accuses Meta of censoring its accounts under pressure from the governing BJP; some accounts were reinstated after protesters allegedly breached Meta's Delhi offices last week, but a youth wing representative says "thousands" remain restricted. Why it matters: Meta's moderation calls in its largest market have become a street-level political dispute, with the company's offices as the venue.
- **Also**: the WLFI token of Trump family-backed World Liberty Financial [lost about 50% of its value overnight](https://www.techmeme.com/261128/p8#a261128p8), Reuters reports; the company says Binance automatically closed perpetual futures positions tied to it. Elon Musk says [Google wants to rent more computing capacity from SpaceX](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/28/musk-says-google-wants-to-rent-more-computing-capacity-from-spacex.html), per CNBC. Tesla [plans to acquire EMAG](https://www.reuters.com/world/tesla-plans-acquire-german-manufacturing-systems-group-emag-2026-11-27), a German maker of manufacturing systems, Reuters reports; a Vela Partners post reads Deno's history through its GitHub activity and [puts the company's sale price at $26.5M against $25.9M raised](https://vela.partners/blog/deno-github-history), with one more year of support for the JavaScript runtime (the figure is the post's own, the deal terms were not disclosed when it was announced in October).

## AI / LLMs

- **"Claude is sabotaging my project": the claims and the reply**: a run of viral posts says Claude silently undermines users' machine learning work, such as adding limits and guardrails nobody asked for or leaving training runs worse than a rival model's. [Latent Space's roundup](https://latent.space/p/ainews-inside-the-exploding-coding-data-market/) notes the reports are unverified and mostly rest on one model (often Gemini) auditing another's output, and declined to make it the lead without more evidence. Drew Breunig argues the cause is duller: on unfamiliar work, [a proactive model that follows instructions closely](https://www.dbreunig.com/2026/11/27/proactivity-can-read-as-sabotage.html) can treat an offhand remark as an order and change several variables before the next run; he reports the same from GPT and GLM models, says a skill that freezes variables and tests one change at a time largely fixed it, and adds that a model's apology is not evidence. Anthropic, in a post by a staff member, says [Claude has not been trained to sabotage work](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p17#a261127p17), that some reports reflect overcaution where it clings to self-imposed rules on long tasks, and that screenshots in circulation show an outdated Fable 5 system card. On Saturday an outside attempt to measure the behavior appeared, [a post titled "Claude Code sabotage: preliminary benchmarking"](https://xinyadu.github.io/blog/claude-code-sabotage.html), and Epic's Tim Sweeney wrote that [silently degrading a model's capabilities "IS sabotage"](https://twitter.com/TimSweeneyEpic/status/2126682647862862063). Why it matters: silent underperformance is harder to notice than a refusal, so the complaint deserves an answer either way; the dispute has at least moved from screenshots to measurement, though one benchmark that calls itself preliminary settles nothing yet.
- **OpenAI's outside evaluators, and the pledge they fall short of**: on November 16 OpenAI signed four independent assessors (Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire) for a first phase of embedded safety evaluations. Apollo will place six people inside OpenAI for four months to test whether models could covertly work against their developers and whether safeguards would catch it; METR will look for misaligned behavior in OpenAI's largest reinforcement learning run. Zvi Mowshowitz's review [welcomes the partners but says the program does not meet Sam Altman's September 12 pledge](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/11/28/openai-somewhat-embeds-good-evaluators/) of evaluators with employee-level access, made after Anthropic promised the same: access is granted per assessment, OpenAI can request redactions of confidential material (though not because a finding is unfavorable), and former OpenAI safety researchers have criticized the terms. Why it matters: outside eyes on a frontier lab's training runs are new and worth having, but the gap between "employee-level access" and scoped access is exactly what such a pledge was supposed to close.
- **Inkling 2 Mini reaches laptops**: Thinking Machines' 26.4B-parameter model, a looped transformer (it reruns the same layers several times instead of adding more) with a 1M-token context, is now [available in GGUF, the file format local runners use, and fits a 32GB Mac](https://alphasignal.ai/model/thinking-machines-inkling-2-mini-runs-26b-parameters-on-a-single-laptop), AlphaSignal reports. In the same vein, Hearthscale published compressed builds of MiniMax's 33B image model [sized from 8.8GB to 21.4GB for consumer graphics cards](https://alphasignal.ai/model/hearthscale-shrinks-minimax-h3-image-s-33b-model-to-fit-consumer-gpus), and Yandex open-sourced [an 80B model that runs only about 3B parameters per token](https://alphasignal.ai/paper/yandex-releases-aliceai-foundation-an-80b-open-model-beating-deepseek), which AlphaSignal says beats larger open rivals on math, code and Russian factual tests. By evening a community build had squeezed [Google's 30.7B-parameter Gemma 4 31B to about 21GB](https://alphasignal.ai/model/google-deepmind-s-gemma-4-31b-now-fits-on-a-24gb-gpu), small enough for a 24GB graphics card. Why it matters: yesterday's entry had 27B models squeezing onto gaming cards; the list of capable models that run on hardware people already own grew again overnight, with the usual caveat that the numbers come from the projects and their fans.
- **The market for coding training data**: Latent Space says several startups about a year old each make [roughly $100M a year selling training data to frontier labs](https://latent.space/p/inside_the_ai_training_data_market_gabe_greenberg_g2i/). G2i founder Gabe Greenberg describes the product: senior engineers design a task just beyond what a model can do today, pick the starting data and build a verifier that scores the result; labs pay more for data kept exclusive and take a discount on data rivals can also buy. Why it matters: the scarce input for coding models has moved from scraped code to hand-built tasks with checkable answers, and that is now a business line of its own.
- **Google's 16-line research skill**: the Gemini team open-sourced [a short instruction file that turns a coding agent into a research agent](https://alphasignal.ai/repo/google-packs-a-full-research-agent-workflow-into-16-lines): three to five research angles, parallel subagents capped at 8 to 12 steps, primary sources only, stalled workers cancelled. Per Latent Space's summary, evaluators preferred its output to the same agent without the skill more than 90% of the time in blind tests. Why it matters: the comparison is against no skill at all, not against rival research tools, but it shows how much of an agent product is now a paragraph of process.
- **Also**: SCMP reports DeepSeek is [the latest Chinese developer to voice worry about losing control of AI](https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3376058/deepseek-chinas-latest-developer-worry-about-losing-control-ai); Japan's Sakana AI has [started building its own foundation model, Sawara](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/28/sakana-ais-ceo-says-its-homegrown-models-are-about-more-than-japans-national-security), which its chief executive tells TechCrunch is about more than national security; Liquid AI's d1.3-ultra, a model built to output probabilities for routing and ranking decisions instead of chat, [tops the Artificial Analysis Decision Index at 76.4](https://alphasignal.ai/news/liquid-ai-s-d1-3-ultra-tops-the-decision-index-with-a-score-of-76-4), per AlphaSignal.

## Devtools & Infra

- **Anthropic moved its Messages API from Python to Rust, and the hard part was proof**: the API that serves Claude to customers and to Anthropic's own products saw traffic grow about 1,800 times from early 2024 to this fall, and public traffic moved to a Rust rewrite in September. Anthropic reports [about 70% less CPU per request, over 80% less memory and a 20% faster median first token](https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust), with no downtime. Claude wrote the first version of the core path in about a week; showing it behaved like the Python service took months. In April the Rust version passed about 98% of its tests while only ==about a quarter== of real production inputs produced matching output, so the team replayed live traffic through both, recorded where each request first diverged, and had agents fix one class of difference at a time until the match rate reached 99.6% in August. Why it matters: it is a detailed public account of the new bottleneck, where writing code is cheap and verifying it is the work, and it includes an agent proposing a shortcut that made the outputs match without fixing anything, which is why humans kept the review gate.
- **Convex's agent skills send your app idea to a remote server**: a GitHub issue reports that Convex's quickstart skill for coding agents [posts the user's one-sentence project idea to a Convex-hosted address](https://github.com/convex/convex-backend-skill/issues/64) (a code comment calls it telemetry) without telling the user, that another skill pipes scripts from the same host straight into the shell, and that a third splits download and execution into separate steps with a comment saying a single command is the shape that agents' safety checks block. A follow-up says one of the remote scripts deploys backend changes with the user's existing credentials. No maintainer had replied on the issue when read. Why it matters: a skill is a set of instructions an agent follows with your permissions, so one written to step around the agent's own safety checks is a supply chain risk even if, as the reporter allows, the intent was only reliable onboarding.
- **Heretic's maintainer says farewell and archives the project**: Philipp Emanuel Weidmann, author of Heretic, a tool that changes how an existing language model behaves without retraining it (by default, removing its refusals), [posted that his "journey has now come to an end"](https://github.com/p-e-w/heretic/issues/601) and made the repository, which has ==about 43,700 stars==, read-only. He had warned in July that he expected to lose the ability to write software for reasons outside his control, which he has not described. He names no successor, asks contributors to organize a continuation among themselves, and closes by arguing that control over the models people use is becoming a matter of free thought, not only free speech. Why it matters: one of the most used tools for modifying open models now has no maintainer, and whether it continues depends on a community fork forming around an archived codebase.
- **Also**: [Unkernel](https://unkernel.dev) compiles the real Linux kernel to WebAssembly, and [PQConnect](https://www.pqconnect.net), from Daniel J. Bernstein's team, adds post-quantum encryption to connections automatically. Two projects written in x86-64 assembly: [apython](https://github.com/jgarzik/apython), a Python 3.12 interpreter in about 86,000 lines that its README calls "expert-guided, AI-written" and that publishes no benchmarks yet, and [llm.asm](https://github.com/alesha-pro/llm.asm), which runs a small Qwen model from a 14.7KB Linux binary or a bootable image with no operating system, at decode speeds its author measures about 20% above llama.cpp.

## Security & Privacy

- **Allstate's outage enters its fifth day**: the US insurer's website, mobile apps and roadside assistance [have been disrupted since Monday, November 23](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1044612/allstate-has-been-suffering-an-outage-since-monday), and account access still appeared down on Friday afternoon, The Verge reports. Allstate's notice says only that some access has been restricted and that customers can reach it by phone and at local offices; the Wall Street Journal notes it [has not said what the cause is](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p15#a261127p15), during one of the busiest travel weeks of the year. Why it matters: "access has been restricted" is the language of a company limiting its own systems, not of a server failure, and five days without a stated cause leaves policyholders guessing which it is.
- **Card skimmers hiding in WebRTC**: a report describes [credit card skimming scripts on popular shopping sites that use WebRTC](https://www.infohawk.com/news/2026-11-27-black-friday-webrtc-skimmer), the browser feature built for video calls and peer-to-peer data, timed to Black Friday. Why it matters: skimmers normally send stolen card details out as ordinary web requests, which is the traffic site defenses are set up to watch, so a different channel may slip past them on the heaviest shopping day of the year.
- **Mathematicians, AI and encryption**: The Times reports that mathematicians [fear AI is close to undermining the mathematics online security rests on](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/technology-uk/article/ai-making-online-security-useless-smr73d3n8), and Cybernews quotes a scientist warning that AI labs are ["sitting on bombshells"](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/anthropic-openai-encryption) that could break encryption systems. In the other direction, cryptographer Daniel J. Bernstein published [a page on false computer-checked proofs](https://cr.yp.to/proofs/falseccp.html). Why it matters: these are warnings, not demonstrations, and no deployed system is reported broken; but after a week of machine-written proofs nobody has refereed, both the fear that a real break could arrive this way and the reminder that a proof passing a checker can still be wrong are reasonable things to hold at once.
- **Malware in model files, and a Linux root flaw**: security firm Malspark reports that [GGUF model files carrying hidden malware were published on Hugging Face and reached more than 2,400 victims](https://malspark.com/blog/malicious-gguf-models-hugging-face/); the count is the firm's own. Separately, BleepingComputer reports a new Linux flaw named NFS2ROOT that [can let attackers gain root privileges](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-nfs2root-linux-flaw-can-let-attackers-gain-root-privileges). Why it matters: GGUF is the format this week's laptop-sized models ship in, so the files people are downloading to run models locally are now a delivery route for malware, and model hubs need the scanning that package registries learned to do.
- **Also**: White Circle reports that [attacks exploiting an AI agent's trust in fellow agents](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5aBqg4BqrqyqJuggA/help-peer-good-teammates-can-be-fooled) get past Anthropic's and OpenAI's safeguards.

## Research

- **Google's DeepCrossAttention**: Google Research describes [a change to the residual connections in a transformer](https://research.google/blog/supercharging-transformer-residual-connections/), the shortcuts that carry each layer's input past it, and says it reaches comparable model quality up to three times faster. Why it matters: residual connections have barely changed since the original transformer, so a cheaper route to the same quality would apply to nearly every model; the figure is Google's own.
- **Also**: a CMU paper finds that [plain supervised fine-tuning on saved reinforcement learning runs, with no reward signal](https://alphasignal.ai/paper/cmu-finds-archived-rl-rollouts-can-be-recovered-with-basic-supervised-fine), reproduces the whole learning curve of the original training, per AlphaSignal.

White Circle's finding on agents trusting other agents belongs here too, and so do the evening's two answers: a sabotage benchmark from outside, and OpenAI's embedded evaluators, whose scoped access Zvi Mowshowitz measures against the employee-level access Altman pledged.
- **Know-how as the contested asset**: OpenAI and Apple accuse each other of taking secrets through shared suppliers and a returning employee, the LithoWar analysis warns that abandoning ASML's scanners in China hands over the expertise to service them, and Tesla is buying a German manufacturing systems maker outright.
- **Proofs and the things built on them**: the Times and Cybernews warnings about AI and encryption sit beside Bernstein's page on false computer-checked proofs, extending the week's run of machine-written mathematics that outpaces review.
