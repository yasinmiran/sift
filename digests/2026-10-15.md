## AI / LLMs

- **Eight states sue OpenAI.** Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho and Montana [filed separate suits](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1011500/openai-state-lawsuits-chatgpt-safety) alleging OpenAI put profit over safety and failed to warn users of ChatGPT's risks, citing family lawsuits over teen and adult harm. Bloomberg Law calls it a [bipartisan effort](https://www.techmeme.com/261014/p46#a261014p46), and the Guardian says the states [accuse it of coaching children to self-harm](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/14/openai-child-self-harm-lawsuit), claims OpenAI has not conceded. Consumer-protection law is now a second front beyond the family suits.
- **FTC subpoenas OpenAI and Anthropic.** The regulator is [probing safety incidents](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/6150621-ftc-subpoena-openai-anthropic-safety-incidents-probe) at both labs, per The Hill. It adds a federal investigation to the morning's state suits, and this time Anthropic is in it too.
- **Apple v. OpenAI, a standstill.** Following yesterday's expedited-discovery order, OpenAI [agreed not to use the trade secrets](https://www.techmeme.com/261014/p50#a261014p50) Apple claims defectors took while the suit proceeds, and to collect forensic images of 14 employees' devices. It limits what OpenAI can ship from that material before a ruling.
- **Anthropic's numbers.** The company says revenue [rose 45% in the quarter](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/14/technology/anthropic-ipo-revenue-profit.html) (paywalled). The Information reports it told IPO investors that [distillation accounts for 5% to 7% of revenue](https://www.techmeme.com/261014/p56#a261014p56) (copying a leading model's outputs to train a rival) and that rapid but slowing growth could weigh on its valuation. Anthropic also published a post on [strengthening defenses against distillation attacks](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50065553).
- **OpenAI on espionage alert.** After yesterday's arrest of an engineer over a hidden recording device, the WSJ reports executives had already [warned staff about honey pots and insider threats](https://www.techmeme.com/261015/p23#a261015p23) and told them to adopt operational security measures. It reframes the arrest as one symptom of a lab-wide security posture.
- **ChatGPT ads.** 404 Media details [how ChatGPT targets ads](https://www.404media.co/how-chatgpt-targets-ads-to-you-based-on-your-prompts-phone-number-and-email) using prompts, phone number and email. What you type to the assistant now feeds ad targeting.
- **Nvidia's open agent model.** Nvidia launched [Nemotron 3.5 Super VL](https://www.techmeme.com/261015/p34#a261015p34), a 120B-parameter open model that reads text and images for enterprise agents; Nvidia says it beats frontier models on specialized benchmarks (its own claim). It is a bid for the open-weights agent slot.
- **METR wants a seat inside the labs.** The evaluator [pushes for in-the-weeds access](https://alphasignal.ai/news/metr-pushes-to-embed-inside-ai-labs-and-catch-rogue-agents) to frontier labs so it can say publicly whether internal agents slip past their creators' control, and has a [proposal for informing the public](https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-14-better-informing-the-public-about-loss-of-control-risks) about loss-of-control risk. It extends this week's argument about lab oversight.
- **Benchmarks.** Epoch's SpireClimb has [GPT-6 Astra winning 98% of Slay the Spire runs](https://alphasignal.ai/news/epoch-s-spireclimb-shows-gpt-6-astra-winning-98-of-slay-the-spire-runs), and frontier models went from near-zero to 90%+ on base difficulty in six months, per AlphaSignal. Vals AI's Robo-bench finds AI [can arm robots but cannot keep them standing](https://alphasignal.ai/news/vals-ai-s-robo-bench-reveals-ai-can-arm-robots-but-can-t-keep-them-standing). Games are saturating while physical control lags.

## Devtools & Infra

- **Cloudflare to buy Deno.** Cloudflare is [acquiring Deno](https://qz.com/cloudflare-acquiring-deno-workers-platform-101226), the JavaScript runtime, to strengthen its Workers platform for running code at the edge. It gives Workers a first-party runtime team.
- **GitHub's reliability ledger.** The September report says GitHub [moved 120 of 160 production services to Central US](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-availability-report-september-2026/) on Azure by October 7, but missed its September 30 goal of moving all of them and paused ramps on September 11 over uneven capacity. Weekly database deadlocks fell about 77% from June. The migration is behind schedule while the August outages remain fresh.
- **Epic's vibe-coded launcher.** Epic released a preview of a [rewritten Epic Games Launcher](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2026/10/programming-is-hard-epic-games-leans-on-vibe-coding-for-its-new-launcher-app/) built from scratch with AI help, which it says is faster than the old one and Steam. A small team rewrote a flagship app and plans Windows, Mac and Linux builds.
- **Modal at $16B.** The inference and sandbox startup [raised a Series D at a $16B valuation](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-modals-1b-arr-16b-series-d) on $1B in annual revenue, with its Sandboxes product alone at a $300M run rate. Agents that run code need somewhere to run it, and that is now a billion-dollar business.
- **Windows on Arm, for real.** The first [RTX Spark laptops](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1011705/nvidia-rtx-spark-everything-runs-on-arm) with Nvidia's Arm chip ship Friday, October 16, with Nvidia promising existing Windows apps run, unlike the 2012 Surface flop. Tom's Hardware says the chip in Microsoft's Surface Laptop Ultra [beats Apple's M5 Max MacBook Pro by about 25%](https://www.techmeme.com/261015/p26#a261015p26) in agentic AI benchmarks with Qwen models.
- **Netflix Conductor, rebuilt.** After Netflix stopped maintaining the open-source version, its workflow engine now runs [about 420 million workflows a month](https://netflixtechblog.com/netflix-conductor-the-next-chapter-41ad21067649?source=rss----2615bd06b42e---4) across roughly 200,000 definitions, and the team rewrote its evaluation engine to cope. A look at what orchestration costs at that scale.

## Security & Privacy

- **Spyware through ads.** Citizen Lab and Confiant say Israeli ad firm Discover-Tech [worked with spyware companies](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1011956/discover-tech-zero-click-ads-spyware) to infect devices, including at Al Jazeera, just by showing users an ad. It is the first technical evidence of a zero-click attack delivered via targeted advertising, and Citizen Lab's report calls the scheme [AdFection](https://citizenlab.ca/research/how-discover-tech-turned-targeted-ads-into-a-pipeline-for-spyware). Any site that sells ad space is now a delivery route.
- **French fridge hack.** Attackers compromised internet-connected controllers at an estimated [700 supermarkets](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/fridge-hack-costing-millions-and-sparking-fires-stemmed-from-poor-security/), forcing defrosters until temperatures hit 50°C, spoiling food and in some cases starting fires. Advisories show the controllers allowed remote admin login, and Danfoss units used a daily password derived from the date by public firmware. Basic patching would have blocked it.
- **Vercel's sandbox bounty.** The [$1M challenge](https://vercel.com/blog/findings-from-the-1-million-dollar-vercel-sandbox-challenge) found no customer-data access, but the worst finding was an upstream Linux kernel networking flaw that let crafted packets leak host memory, crash a host, and in tests on the researcher's own machines run commands as administrator. Patches are under private review with CVEs pending, and most major clouds isolate workloads on this layer.
- **Phishing marketplace dismantled.** Microsoft and Japan's National Police Agency [took down YAM Store](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1011487/microsoft-scam-messages-marketplace-takedown) and its CoGUI and Iron Man phishing kits, which sent over a billion messages to 75,000+ organizations since January. CyberScoop adds that [Health-ISAC and others joined](https://www.techmeme.com/261014/p55#a261014p55). Selling kits was the business, so the marketplace was the target.
- **Yandex outage and bots.** Cloudflare says Ukrainian drone strikes cut Yandex traffic by up to 99%, but [malicious automation moved to other hosts](https://blog.cloudflare.com/yandex-outage/) rather than vanishing. Aggregate traffic drops do not show who went quiet.
- **Surveillance cameras and faces.** A Georgia man [sues Flock and a sheriff's office](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/10/georgia-man-sues-flock-sheriffs-office-over-alleged-alpr-fueled-retaliation/), alleging months of license-plate searches without probable cause, while Flock's CEO [argues crime reduction means giving up privacy](https://www.techmeme.com/261014/p52#a261014p52). EFF is asking the Wisconsin Supreme Court to [end warrantless plate searches](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/were-asking-wisconsin-supreme-court-end-warrantless-alpr-searches). Separately, the DHS inspector general found a "significant weakness" in how [TSA safeguards facial-recognition data](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/10/dhs-inspector-general-finds-tsa-not-properly-safeguarding-passenger-facial).
