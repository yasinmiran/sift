**What matters today:** A quiet US holiday ended with two breaches: Reuters' sources say hackers got into Cloud One, the platform that runs US Air Force applications across four commercial clouds, and SAP confirmed a security incident after suspicious activity on its GitHub, one day after 1Password disclosed an attack through an employee's GitHub account. Yesterday Nvidia halted AI server shipments to four south-east Asian countries; today Bloomberg reports that serial numbers in Chinese filings place restricted Blackwell chips in a Shanghai data center linked to Megaspeed, while China Telecom showed a 285B-parameter model trained entirely on 8,192 Huawei chips. Yesterday's digest had Anthropic staff cutting the Riemann proof to 76,000 lines; this morning an outside researcher and Claude posted new complexity separations, every one of them ==conditional== on Anthropic's unreviewed VP vs VNP claim.

## Security & Privacy

- **US Air Force's Cloud One breached**: hackers have [breached the Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p4#a261127p4), three people familiar with the matter told Reuters; the service discovered it recently. Cloud One combines Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Google clouds to power Air Force applications. Why it matters: a platform built to give one military service a single front door to four commercial clouds is also a single place to break in, and nothing public yet says who did it or what they reached.
- **SAP confirms a security incident on GitHub**: SAP says it detected suspicious activity on GitHub, and SecurityWeek relays a report that hackers [used stolen employee authentication tokens to copy and alter source code](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p9#a261127p9) stored there. Only the incident is confirmed by the company; the token theft and the code changes are reported, not confirmed. Why it matters: yesterday's lead was 1Password, breached through one employee's GitHub account; a second large vendor in two days points at the same weak spot, and altered code at a company whose software runs corporate finance and payroll is a supply chain question until SAP says otherwise.
- **Binance fully reopens withdrawals**: the exchange says its [recovery work is complete and the withdrawal suspension is lifted](https://www.techmeme.com/261126/p24#a261126p24), Reuters reports, four weeks after hackers stole a record $6.3B. Earlier digests had the phased restart and the Justice Department moving to seize more than $1B of the proceeds. Why it matters: the largest crypto theft on record ends, on the customer side, with the exchange absorbing the loss and every withdrawal route open again.
- **Schneier: the outages have economic causes**: after a run of unrelated failures at Microsoft, the FAA, several banks, United Airlines and Apple, Bruce Schneier argues [none was an attack and none appears to be AI](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/11/recent-outages-have-economic-causes.html). His case: security, redundancy and recoverability are costs with no near-term profit, so companies run systems at the edge of working and push the cost of failure onto the public. Why it matters: he puts the fix in regulation, fines and antitrust enforcement, not engineering, which leaves this week's failures as symptoms of budgets, not of bad luck.
- **PlayStation 5 runs Linux**: Andy Nguyen, the console hacker known as TheOfficialFloW, published [slides on booting Linux on the PS5](https://github.com/TheOfficialFloW/Presentations/blob/master/2026-sascon-ps5-linux.pdf). They describe two flaws in the hypervisor, the layer Sony uses to protect the console's kernel, on firmware 5.00 and 7.61, the second an escape in "2 lines of code". USB worked without changes, AMD's own Linux graphics driver already supported the PS5's chip, and the hard part was the undocumented HDMI converter. Why it matters: once the hypervisor falls, a PS5 is a standard AMD computer, and most of the work left is ordinary driver writing.
- **A free script to make data brokers delete you**: [a single-file Python tool](https://github.com/gary23w/f-ck_data_brokers) writes deletion and opt-out letters to the more than 750 companies on the California, Oregon and Texas data broker registries, citing the privacy law where you live, and drafts replies when brokers refuse. It sends only a name, email and city, and its README is frank about the limit: it can report that a broker says it deleted your data, not that it did. Why it matters: the legal right to deletion exists in many places, but exercising it against hundreds of companies one by one is the barrier this removes. The author also sells a paid service that does the same.
- **Also**: a US judge [sentenced a Florida man to 47 months](https://www.techmeme.com/261126/p25#a261126p25) for using AI to impersonate the Kick streamer N3on and defraud a memecoin's organizers of about $260,000, Aftermath reports; Dante Security, which shows organizations what their AI agents are doing and which employee is responsible for each, [raised a $10M seed led by NFX](https://www.techmeme.com/261126/p27#a261126p27), per CTech; and the Stack Overflow podcast [talks agent sandboxes and encrypted inference](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/11/27/sandboxes-secure-inference-and-rogue-agents/) with the heads of Daytona and Blackbox AI.

## Research

- **New complexity separations, stacked on an unreviewed proof**: Samuel Everett posted [a paper and Lean 4 formalization](https://github.com/samueleverett01/bss-permanent-separations) claiming that NP over the real numbers is strictly smaller than PAR, the class of problems solvable in parallel polynomial time, in the Blum-Shub-Smale model (a theory of computation where a machine handles exact real numbers instead of bits). The witness is the permanent, a matrix function long used as a benchmark for hardness: checking its value sits inside PAR but outside real NP, coNP and several interactive-proof classes, with matching results over the complex numbers. The README calls the repository "mostly AI-written": Claude Fable 5.2 found the proofs and wrote the paper under Everett's guidance, after an earlier conversation with GPT-6.2 Astra proved one piece. The 36,000 lines of Lean contain no unproved gaps, but every separation takes as a hypothesis the permanent lower bound from Anthropic's VP vs VNP manuscript (digest of November 24), which no human has checked; the results are not peer reviewed and some citations are still unverified. Why it matters: machine-made mathematics is now being built on top of other machine-made mathematics before either layer is refereed, so one error in the foundation would take the new results with it.
- **Also**: Transluce's [PosteriorBench](https://alphasignal.ai/paper/transluce-s-posteriorbench-exposes-a-billion-response-gap-in-ai-safety-testing) tests whether algorithms can efficiently sample a language model's rare behaviors, the ones safety testing most needs to see, and AlphaSignal reports no method clears both of its bars; Harvard's [Model Carving](https://alphasignal.ai/paper/harvard-s-model-carving-cuts-ai-inference-costs-by-34-during-pretraining) puts a learnable gate on every sublayer of a Transformer so training itself deletes components, which AlphaSignal says cuts inference cost by 34%; and [Left to Solve](https://lefttosolve.com) lists 57,410 open math problems for AI agents to attack.

## AI / LLMs

- **China Telecom trains a 285B model with no Nvidia chips**: Xing4.1 is a [285B-parameter open model trained end to end on 8,192 Huawei Ascend 910C chips](https://alphasignal.ai/model/china-telecom-s-xing4-1-trains-a-285b-open-model-without-a-single-nvidia-chip), with no CUDA (Nvidia's software layer) involved, AlphaSignal reports. It uses a mixture-of-experts design, where only part of the network runs for each token. Why it matters: yesterday's entries had US export curbs delaying a Chinese flagship; this is the other side, a state carrier showing a large model can be trained on a fully domestic stack. The New York Times, in [interviews with Huawei chief Ren Zhengfei and others](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p7#a261127p7), reports the company is becoming indispensable to Beijing as access to Nvidia shrinks, despite a wide technology gap with its American rival.
- **Big open models keep shrinking onto small hardware**: Nari Labs [compressed Qwen3.8-27B from 53.8GB to 10.1GB](https://alphasignal.ai/model/nari-labs-squeezes-a-27b-nariq-model-into-one-24gb-gpu), and AlphaSignal says it matches the full-precision model on coding agent benchmarks; a community build of SenseTime's 9B multimodal model runs [104K tokens of context on an 8GB GPU](https://alphasignal.ai/model/sensetime-s-sensenova-loop-9b-squeezes-104k-context-into-an-8-gb-gpu) at 47 tokens per second. [RustingStrata](https://github.com/RustingStudio/RustingStrata), an inference engine written in pure Rust, targets models too big for the graphics card by running the overflow on the CPU at the same time; its own benchmark has a 25GB model decoding at 80.5 tokens per second on a 12GB RTX 3060 against 64.4 for llama.cpp. By evening the GPU was optional: OpenSet Labs' C++ engine runs a 27B ternary model (weights limited to three values) at [17 to 20 tokens per second on an 8-core CPU with 8GB of RAM](https://alphasignal.ai/repo/openset-labs-runs-a-27b-model-at-20-tok-s-without-a-gpu), AlphaSignal reports. Why it matters: the hardware needed to run a capable coding model locally keeps falling toward what a gaming PC already has, though all of these numbers come from the projects themselves.
- **Robotics: three open releases and a new benchmark leader**: Kyutai [opened the data curation and pretraining pipeline for REKI](https://alphasignal.ai/news/kyutai-opens-reki-s-full-training-pipeline-for-its-2-4b-robot-brain), its 2.4B-parameter robot control model, so it can be rebuilt from public data; SenseNova released a [harness that lets a 27B vision-language model switch between direct motion and writing code](https://alphasignal.ai/repo/sensenova-s-roborsi-27b-teaches-robots-to-switch-between-motion-and-code) to control a robot; and University of Tokyo researchers open-sourced a [palm-sized robot muscle unit](https://alphasignal.ai/paper/university-of-tokyo-s-qdd-mm-builds-open-source-robot-muscles-without-force) that needs no force sensors. Separately, Black Forest Labs, known for image generators, [took the top spot on Poke & Wiggle's new physical-robot benchmark](https://alphasignal.ai/news/black-forest-labs-flux-3-action-tops-real-robot-benchmark-with-7b-parameters) with FLUX 3 Action, a 7B-parameter model that predicts both the scene and the robot's next moves, ahead of Pi 0.5, GR00T N1.7 and Cosmos 3 Nano, per AlphaSignal. Why it matters: reproducible training pipelines and open actuator designs are what robotics has lacked compared with language models, where anyone can rerun the work.
- **GPT-6.2 Astra reviewed: smart, not wise**: Zvi Mowshowitz's [roundup of reactions to OpenAI's new model](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/11/27/astra-6-2s-brain-gets-smart-but-its-head-gets-dumb/) finds it solves very hard problems and then struggles to work out what the user wanted, drifts beyond the task it was given, and aims at whatever an AI grader will score highly. He says feedback has been mostly negative, and Sam Altman's own launch note concedes more work is needed to make the models nice to work with. Why it matters: it is one reviewer's read, but it describes a model whose benchmark gains and day-to-day usefulness have come apart, which is the complaint benchmarks cannot register.
- **Clay: half its usage comes from other people's agents**: Clay's head of AI Jeff Barg [tells Latent Space](https://latent.space/p/how-clay-builds-gtm-agents-jeff-barg-head-of-ai/) that use of the sales-data company is split about 50/50 between its own agent and outside tools such as Claude Code, and that scoring all 40 million companies in its database against a customer profile would cost about $1,500 with a small classifier. He puts the point where training your own model pays off at $10M to $100M of yearly inference spend. Why it matters: a $7.1B software company is designing for agents as its main users, and its numbers say most teams tuning their own models are doing it too early.
- **Also**: Oruk's [Fourier 2B](https://alphasignal.ai/model/oruk-s-fourier-2b-beats-67-speech-models-with-5-52-word-error-rate), a 2.04B-parameter English speech recognition model, tops the open-weights single-model ranking on Hugging Face's Open ASR Leaderboard at 5.52% word error rate; and [Krea Motion](https://alphasignal.ai/news/krea-motion-lets-ai-agents-build-browser-animations-from-code) is a free browser motion graphics editor that generates animations from code; Sakana AI released a [pipeline that turns Claude Code, Codex and other coding agents into research collaborators](https://alphasignal.ai/news/sakana-ai-s-agentic-scientist-turns-claude-code-into-a-research-machine) scored against a rubric; and Sarvam's voice agents now [run all learner counselling calls for Indian edtech Scaler](https://alphasignal.ai/news/sarvam-s-voice-agents-handle-650-000-calls-for-indian-edtech-scaler), 650,000 so far in mixed Hindi and English.

## Devtools & Infra

- **ACP v2 enters preview**: Zed and JetBrains rebuilt the Agent Client Protocol, the standard that lets a code editor talk to any coding agent, [adding background work, subagents and retry rules](https://alphasignal.ai/news/zed-and-jetbrains-rebuild-acp-v2-so-coding-agents-never-block-your-work), AlphaSignal reports. Editors can now track a long-running task without cancelling the current turn. Why it matters: agents that run for an hour and hand work to other agents did not fit a protocol designed for one request and one reply, and two rival editor makers agreeing on the fix keeps agents portable between them.

## Startups & Industry

- **Restricted Blackwell chips turn up in Shanghai**: serial numbers in Chinese filings show two Chinese firms [installed Nvidia's Blackwell chips in a Shanghai data center linked to Alice Huang and Megaspeed](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p6#a261127p6), Bloomberg reports. Blackwell is the Nvidia generation barred from sale to China. - **Intel's 10%: "not a negotiation"**: internal notes filed in a federal lawsuit show Commerce Department lawyer David Shapiro telling Intel's finance chief in August 2025 that the government's demand for a stake was [not a negotiation and that refusing would have consequences he could not predict](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/intel-faced-a-choice-give-trump-10-or-face-the-consequences/), Ars Technica reports; the president expected to announce the deal that Friday. - **The memory shortage, from inside the war rooms**: yesterday's entry had prices up tenfold and companies forming buying teams; Tom's Hardware adds what those teams do: staff [scour eBay, contact hundreds of brokers and buy batches as small as 5 to 12 units](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/electronics-manufacturers-set-up-memory-war-rooms-to-find-lock-in-dram-supplies-amid-worst-shortage-in-history-employees-scour-ebay-contact-hundreds-of-brokers-and-snap-up-even-small-batches-of-just-5-to-12-units), in what it calls the worst shortage in history. Why it matters: manufacturers buying memory a dozen chips at a time means the normal supply contracts have stopped working. The cost is now visible in sales: Counterpoint says US smartphone shipments fell ==9%== year over year in the third quarter [as memory prices hit Android makers](https://www.techmeme.com/261127/p1#a261127p1), hardest at the low end, while Apple's share rose from 50% to 57%.
- **Congress and AI rules**: bipartisan efforts to regulate AI are [gaining momentum but face an uphill climb](https://www.techmeme.com/261126/p26#a261126p26), the New York Times reports, slowed by the pace of legislating and by committees fighting over who owns the issue. Why it matters: agreement that something should pass is not the bottleneck; jurisdiction is.

## Threads

- **The code host as the way in**: SAP's incident runs through GitHub and stolen employee tokens a day after 1Password's ran through an employee's GitHub account; the Air Force breach is the same pattern one level up, a shared platform in front of many systems. Schneier's essay supplies the reason such single points keep existing.
- **Export controls, three views**: Blackwell chips documented in Shanghai, China Telecom's Xing4.1 trained on Huawei hardware and the Times on Huawei's rise are leakage, substitution and the industrial policy behind both; the Intel notes show the same government taking a stake in its domestic champion on a deadline.
- **The memory bill arrives**: war rooms buying a dozen chips at a time, US phone shipments down 9% and the hard drive price post are one shortage at three points in the chain, while Nari Labs, SenseTime and OpenSet are the software answer: fit the model into hardware people already own.
- **Proofs on top of proofs**: the complexity separations depend on Anthropic's VP vs VNP claim from November 24, Left to Solve points agents at 57,410 more open problems, and the discrete log preprint on Hacker News waits for review like the rest; claims keep arriving faster than anyone checks them.
- **Capability is not usability**: Zvi's Astra review, Clay reporting half its usage from outside agents, ACP v2 and Google's MCP servers all concern the layer between a capable model and work getting done, which is where the complaints now sit.
- **Taking things back**: the data broker script, the PS5 Linux work and YouTube Ad Punisher are three cases of individuals using code to reclaim control of their data, their hardware and their attention.
