**What matters today:** OpenAI [published a mass of math results](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1005004/openai-math-release-github) from an unreleased model, the biggest AI-for-science claim yet and one mathematicians will spend weeks checking. On the security side, attackers took over whole country-code domains to obtain [counterfeit certificates for Google](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/hackers-obtain-counterfeit-tls-certificates-for-google-and-other-large-services/). Yesterday's thread on agents behaving badly continues in quieter form: regulators and states are now deciding where AI acts without a human in the loop.

## AI / LLMs

- **OpenAI math dump.** An unreleased frontier model produced ==722 manuscripts== covering 372 result families, with Lean proof formalizations on GitHub, per [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1005004/openai-math-release-github) and [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics). [Scientific American's account](https://www.techmeme.com/261006/p46#a261006p46) says nearly all came from one prompt to one agent, some after several attempts. Why it matters: verification, not generation, is now the bottleneck, and an advisory group of mathematicians is already vetting claims.
- **ChatGPT text watermarking, EU only by default.** To meet the EU AI Act, which took effect in August, OpenAI [marks ChatGPT text](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/openai-will-watermark-chatgpt-outputs-by-default-but-only-in-the-eu/) with a proprietary scheme called textGrain; elsewhere it is available but off. Why it matters: no watermark is reliably tamper-proof yet, so this is compliance first, detection second.
- **Utah lets AI prescribe.** Utah will [let AI examine patients and prescribe medication](https://www.techspot.com/news/114111-utah-become-first-state-ai-examine-patients-prescribe.html) without human oversight, a first for a US state per the report. Why it matters: the autonomy question moves from chatbots to clinics, with real liability behind it.
- **OpenAI Decisions API enters public beta.** A new endpoint for [classification, routing and scoring calls](https://alphasignal.ai/news/openai-s-decisions-api-promises-routing-answers-10x-faster-for-developers), advertised at sub-200ms and powered by GPT-6 Luna. Why it matters: it targets the cheap, fast decisions inside agent pipelines rather than long answers.
- **Nano Banana 2.1.** Google's [image model update](https://alphasignal.ai/news/google-s-nano-banana-2-1-beats-pro-on-image-editing-at-flash-prices), built on Gemini 3.6 Flash, is said to beat the Pro tier at image editing for Flash-class prices, about half the cost of Nano Banana 2. Why it matters: editing quality gets cheaper to ship in products.
- **EmbeddingGemma 2.** Google released an [open, lightweight multimodal embedding model](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/embeddinggemma-2/), the kind that turns text and images into searchable vectors. Why it matters: on-device search and retrieval no longer need a paid API.
- **Open weights, fewer disasters than feared.** Nathan Lambert [argues](https://www.techmeme.com/261006/p48#a261006p48) GLM-5.3's open release has produced no major attacks despite Anthropic's warnings about Mythos-level cyber risk. Why it matters: it weakens the case for banning open models, though one quiet month proves little.

## Devtools & Infra

- **GitHub rebuilds Git for agents.** GitHub is [re-architecting its Git infrastructure](https://github.blog/engineering/architecture-optimization/building-git-infrastructure-for-agent-scale-development/) for repositories where humans and agents commit concurrently, some taking millions of commits a day. Why it matters: agent traffic, not people, is now setting the design limits of the biggest code host.
- **Cursor remote control.** Cursor's iOS app can [pair with a desktop](https://alphasignal.ai/news/cursor-ships-remote-control-so-developers-run-local-agents-from-iphone) to monitor, message and launch local coding agents. Why it matters: supervising agents becomes a phone-sized task, which raises the stakes on approval prompts.
- **OSC 7501.** Mitchell Hashimoto proposes [a terminal protocol for program status](https://mitchellh.com/writing/program-status-osc7501), an escape sequence programs can use to tell the terminal what they are doing. Why it matters: terminals could show agent and build state natively instead of scraping output.

## Security & Privacy

- **Fake certificates for Google.** Attackers hijacked the .gh, .sl and .as country-code domains, changed DNS records for chosen domains inside them, and passed automated validation to [obtain unauthorized TLS certificates](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/hackers-obtain-counterfeit-tls-certificates-for-google-and-other-large-services/) for several Google domains and other major brands. Google says Chrome now blocks the certificates and the issuers revoked them. Why it matters: a registry-level compromise defeats the checks certificate authorities rely on.
- **FortiBleed.** The FBI [warns](https://decipher.sc/2026/10/06/fbi-warns-of-fortibleed-attacks-leading-to-lockouts-ransomware) that FortiBleed attacks are leading to lockouts and ransomware. Why it matters: edge firewalls remain the favorite way in, so patch and check exposure now.
- **Atlassian pre-auth file read.** watchTowr details [CVE-2026-21589](https://labs.watchtowr.com/you-wont-hear-about-these-even-in-myths-atlassian-jira-confluence-and-more-pre-auth-arbitrary-file-read-cve-2026-21589), a path traversal that allows unauthenticated arbitrary file reads in Jira, Confluence and more. Why it matters: internet-facing Atlassian installs are exposed until patched.
- **Backdoored open coding models.** ProjectDiscovery shows [how abliterated models](https://projectdiscovery.io/research/how-abliterated-models-can-get-you-pwned), open models with safety training stripped out, can carry backdoors into your code. Why it matters: the model you download is part of your supply chain.
- **Arizona courts breach.** A September 24 cyberattack [stole personal data of 1.3M people](https://www.techmeme.com/261006/p51#a261006p51), including 30K orders of protection and 150K foster care board reports. Why it matters: the data involves people in protective situations.
- **VPN to leave Canada.** A Toronto-based VPN provider [plans to quit Canada](https://citizenlab.ca/toronto-based-vpn-provider-plans-to-quit-canada-over-lawful-access-bill/) over a lawful-access bill, per Citizen Lab. Why it matters: privacy tools are choosing jurisdiction over market access.
