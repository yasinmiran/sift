**What matters today:** this morning the WSJ's sources had Nvidia's board preparing to oust Jensen Huang; by the US open [he had resigned](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition), CFO Colette Kress was interim CEO, and the stock [fell 14%](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p34#a261112p34), taking the company under $4 trillion. The exit does not end the scandal: Huang has agreed to [testify to Congress](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p41#a261112p41) before Thanksgiving, and Semafor counts [several Justice Department probes](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p42#a261112p42) that could reach the company. Away from Nvidia, Google and Samsung opened preorders for [Gemini smartglasses from $395](https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-pushes-privacy-angle-as-gemini-powered-smartglasses-debut-starting-at-395/), the count on the GitHub secret stealer came in at 4,837 hijacked accounts, and Google's Gemini Decision 1 made decision models a three-vendor category.

## AI / LLMs

- **Google ships a decision model**: [Gemini Decision 1](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/) answers closed questions (yes/no, multiple choice, rubric scores) in a single pass and attaches a confidence score, taking text, images, audio, video and PDFs, up to 1,000 questions per request, at $0.10 per million input tokens with output free. Simon Willison [finds the API almost identical](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/11/gemini-decisions-api/) to TypeSafe AI's Jev, the model that started the category, and priced the same as OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna decision model. Google's own tests have it beating Jev on 5 of 7 shared benchmarks; Artificial Analysis, per [Latent Space's roundup](https://latent.space/p/ainews-d-day-decision-models-day/), has the two tied on its index, with Gemini slower (211 ms against 92 ms) and costlier per task. Why it matters: with a third big vendor in, cheap calibrated classifiers for routing, policy checks and ranking are now a standard model type, not one startup's product.
- **Decision models in production**: at the AI Engineer Code Summit, Glean said it [uses Jev where categories keep changing](https://latent.space/p/jev-revolution/) because it needs no retraining, and Cloudflare showed a hybrid agent in which fast decision models pick tools and extract logs while a large reasoning model handles only the diagnosis. A fourth entrant arrived today: AutoTrust's 27B-parameter [AutoTrust-JEV](https://alphasignal.ai/news/autotrust-s-autotrust-jev-turns-ai-decisions-into-a-single-token-at-97-accuracy), which returns calibrated probabilities for each option instead of text and, by AlphaSignal's account, tops tool-selection and intent-classification benchmarks. Why it matters: the pattern keeps the expensive LLM for the one hard step and hands the rest to models that return a score, which is cheaper and easier to test.
- **Chatbots send shoppers to fake stores**: a study by IP protection firm Podqi asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity where to buy from 151 brands, with questions like "Where can I find [brand] online?", and found the answers [can point to impostor and other risky sites](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1032020/gemini-chatgpt-perplexity-shopping-test-risky-fake-websites-citations). Why it matters: a chatbot's recommendation carries more trust than a search result, and counterfeiters only need to be the page it cites.

## Devtools & Infra

- **AWS Emulator, written by agents**: AWS released a free, open source [local emulator](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/test-your-applications-against-aws-locally-with-aws-emulator/) that answers the same APIs as the cloud, so tests, CLI scripts and coding agents run against a laptop instead of a deployment; GeekWire counts 200+ services. It is a single Rust binary generated from the API models the SDKs use, and AWS says agents wrote it and its team ==hasn't written or reviewed any code== ([GeekWire via Techmeme](https://www.techmeme.com/261111/p41#a261111p41)); Simon Willison [counts 29,684 commits](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/11/aws-emulator/) in the project's first three weeks. Console's review adds the first caveat: it [does not match every AWS API](https://github.com/aws/aws-emulator?ref=console.dev), and it is hard to find out which ones. Why it matters: coding agents get an official, free loop for testing against AWS, and AWS is presenting unreviewed agent output as a selling point, which makes this a public test of that claim.

## Security & Privacy

- **The GitHub secret stealer, counted**: this morning Aikido reported compromised accounts [pushing a CI secret stealer](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/compromised-github-accounts-push-ci-secret-stealer-to-tens-of-thousands-of-repositories) (code that lifts the tokens and keys a build pipeline holds) to tens of thousands of repositories. The Hacker News now puts numbers on it: [4,837 hijacked accounts](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/4837-hijacked-github-accounts-ran-ci.html), with the stealer actually running in 1,806 or more repositories. Python core developer Hugo van Kemenade's [Lock Your GitHub Actions](https://hugovk.dev/blog/2026/lock-github-actions/) is the timely how-to. Why it matters: a pipeline's secrets can publish packages and deploy to production, so each repository where the stealer ran is a possible second compromise.
- **Phone tracking through ordinary apps**: Penlink's Webloc, sold to police, [locates people using data from weather, gaming, email and religious apps](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/11/another-way-police-are-locating-people-from-their-smartphones.html); company documents reviewed by Bloomberg describe ==12 months of location history==, updated hourly, and the ability to list every phone present at a given place and time. Why it matters: the data is bought from the app ecosystem, so this kind of location search does not depend on a request to a phone carrier.
- **Autonomous AI in live attacks**: Palo Alto's Unit 42 reports attackers [using autonomous AI against semiconductor, defense and civil society targets](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/cairn-framework-ai-autonomous-attacks). Why it matters: Unit 42 is describing AI running attacks, not just assisting them, against the sectors at the center of this week's export fight.
- **Who runs Qilin**: Krebs traces the administrator of the most active ransomware affiliate program, which claims [at least 365 victims in three months](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/11/who-runs-the-ransomware-group-qilin/) and pays affiliates 80% or more of each ransom, to a 36-year-old man in St. Petersburg, through old handles he reused when applying to a forum that was later breached. Why it matters: naming an operator rarely means an arrest in Russia, but it limits his travel and makes partners warier.
- **Also**: Lego's BrickLink marketplace has been [offline for a week](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1031889/lego-bricklink-marketplace-offline-unusual-activity) after the company spotted "unusual activity", with no return date and no explanation of what happened, and Wiz published a rundown of [CVE-2026-119618](https://www.wiz.io/blog/cve-2026-119618-rce-apache-http-server-vulnerability), a critical remote code execution flaw in Apache HTTP Server. Why it matters: sellers who depend on BrickLink are waiting without information, and the Apache bug sits in one of the web's most deployed servers.

## Startups & Industry

- **Huang resigns, Kress takes over**: hours after the WSJ's sources said the board was [preparing to oust him](https://www.techmeme.com/261111/p44#a261111p44), Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of the company he co-founded in 1993, following a board investigation into the two reported relationships. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, and former Synopsys CEO Harvey Jones becomes [Nvidia's first board chair](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p29#a261112p29); the announcement carries no quote from Huang, who owns 3.6% of the company. Shares ==dropped 14% at the open==, [erasing more than $600B](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p34#a261112p34) and taking Nvidia below $4 trillion for the first time since 2025. Kress told staff this is ["an extraordinarily difficult period"](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p39#a261112p39) and set a leadership meeting for November 18; The Information's sources say she sometimes [pushed back on Huang's riskier deals](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p43#a261112p43), such as backstopping OpenAI. President Trump [proposed Tim Cook](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p38#a261112p38) as the permanent successor. Why it matters: Nvidia has never had another CEO, and its interim one is a finance chief known for caution, running the company whose spending helped finance much of the AI buildout.
- **The investigations outlast the CEO**: House Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast says Huang has [agreed to testify](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p41#a261112p41) before Thanksgiving, likely behind closed doors. Semafor's administration sources describe [multiple Justice Department probes](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p42#a261112p42) that could sweep up Nvidia: one on antitrust grounds, two focused on Megaspeed and Supermicro. The Information reports firms tied to Alice Huang [recently invested in DeepSeek](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p37#a261112p37), aiming for closer ties and to supply it with Blackwell chips, which are banned from sale to China. On Capitol Hill, the FT sees [bipartisan support building](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p3#a261112p3) for tighter export controls, Commerce is still [finalizing its rule](https://www.techmeme.com/261111/p53#a261111p53) against remote use of restricted chips, and Reuters' sources say Chinese AI companies are [rushing to rent Nvidia capacity abroad](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p4#a261112p4) before either lands. Why it matters: the resignation settles who runs Nvidia, not whether export rules were evaded, and that question now belongs to Congress and prosecutors.
- **The money that ran through Nvidia**: the FT maps how Nvidia put its profits into [AI startups, suppliers and its own chip customers](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p32#a261112p32), a set of dependencies that could come apart without Huang. The same circularity shows elsewhere: TSMC's board approved up to [$500M each of OpenAI and Anthropic stock](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/taiwans-chipmaking-giant-tsmc-to-invest-1b-in-anthropic-openai/), and AI cloud provider Nebius [fell more than 16%](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p36#a261112p36) despite revenue up 571% to $980.8M, after raising its 2026 spending plan and giving a weak outlook. Why it matters: investors are starting to price AI demand separately from the financing that suppliers extend to their own customers.
- **Funding and earnings**: LangChain, which sells tooling for companies building their own agents, raised [$300M at a $3B valuation](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p35#a261112p35) led by Alphabet's CapitalG, up from $1.25B in 2025. Munich's Agile Robots raised [$500M led by SoftBank](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p6#a261112p6) at $8.5B, and drone maker Uforce, founded in Ukraine and based in London, raised [$700M at $6B](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p12#a261112p12) with the UK National Wealth Fund co-leading. Foxconn's quarterly revenue [rose 47% to about $95.4B](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p9#a261112p9) on AI and iPhone demand. Why it matters: outside Nvidia's week, capital is still flowing to agents, robots and defense hardware at rising valuations.
- **United blocks AI booking agents**: the airline [will not let personal AI assistants buy tickets](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1031623/united-unauthorized-ai-agents), citing the risk of exposed customer data, unintended purchases and federal rules that require travelers to see and attest to certain information themselves; agents can still show United flights. Why it matters: it is an early case of a large company refusing agent traffic on policy grounds, before the traffic even exists.

## Elsewhere

- **Google and Samsung's Gemini glasses**: the first Android XR "intelligent eyewear" is [up for preorder](https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-pushes-privacy-angle-as-gemini-powered-smartglasses-debut-starting-at-395/): Samsung hardware, Google software, frames by Warby Parker (from $395, US only) and Gentle Monster (from $449, US and South Korea). The Verge's [hands-on](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p44#a261112p44) credits privacy protections such as on-device face redaction and Gemini features such as live translation. Why it matters: Google is re-entering a category Meta has had largely to itself, and leading with the privacy objection that sank Google Glass.
- **Victims take xAI to court**: Bloomberg reports a growing number of young people whose photos were sexualized with Grok are [seeking to hold xAI legally accountable](https://www.techmeme.com/261112/p7#a261112p7) for letting users generate the images. Why it matters: the cases test whether an AI company answers for what its image tool produces, not only the user who typed the prompt.
- **Tim Peters, 1952 to 2026**: the Python community is [mourning Tim Peters](https://discuss.python.org/t/in-memoriam-tim-peters-1952-2026/110028), author of the Zen of Python and of Timsort, the sorting algorithm Python and several other languages use by default. Why it matters: few people shaped both a language's code and its culture as he did.
- **EU proposes the Digital Fairness Act**: the Commission's draft [targets misleading prices, loot boxes, addictive design and undisclosed influencer marketing](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1031575/digital-fairness-act-eu-dfa-consumer-protection) across games, social media and e-commerce. Why it matters: it regulates how products are designed to hold attention and spending, a layer earlier EU platform laws left mostly alone.
- **Games and generative AI, two readings**: 19 Independent Games Festival jurors pledged not to vote for any game made with generative AI after Big Walk's developer [disclosed minimal use](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2026/11/big-walk-used-ai-to-fix-a-bug-now-the-indie-gaming-world-is-having-a-reckoning/); the GDC annual survey, out today, finds [40% of developers now use it at work](https://www.theverge.com/games/1032085/gdc-festival-of-gaming-annual-survey-ai-layoffs), up from 36%, and 70% at mobile-only studios. Why it matters: a zero-tolerance rule is forming in indie games just as use becomes routine in the rest of the industry.
