**What matters today:** OpenAI [released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol), cutting the flagship's price by a fifth, and the first outside tests and reviews are mixed. Yesterday Jensen Huang asked for his job back in public; today Nvidia's chair answered on the earnings call that the board [does not intend to reinstate him](https://www.theverge.com/report/1035465/nvidia-says-it-will-not-reinstate-jensen-huang-as-ceo), beside a record quarter, an end to H200 sales in China and a reported SEC investigation. The week of outages got its first common explanation: sources say banks and the NSA told Congress their systems buckled under a rush to patch vulnerabilities found by AI models, on the day an AI model was credited with finding a remote code execution flaw in Java.

## AI / LLMs

- **OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol**: Astra is the new flagship for the hardest work and Sol the cheaper everyday model, [both available](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol) in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. Astra costs ==$8 in, $40 out== per million tokens, 80% of GPT-6 Astra's price and the same as Claude Fable 5.2, with cached input down from $1 to $0.40; Sol stays at $2/$10, [Simon Willison notes](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/17/gpt62-astra-and-sol/). Reception is mixed: Artificial Analysis ranks Astra [behind only Fable 5.2 on its main index and flat against GPT-6 Astra in coding](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/p57#a261117p57), and Theo Browne's [pre-release review](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/p49#a261117p49) says it runs in circles on long tasks and stops to ask questions a user cannot answer. Why it matters: the price cut is the certain part, and cheaper cached input mostly benefits long agent sessions; whether the model is better is still being argued.
- **Outside tests: no model does real work at employee level**: Epoch AI ran Fable 5.2, GPT-6.2 Astra and four other frontier models on [real workplace tasks](https://epoch.ai/publications/how-good-are-fable-5-2-and-gpt-6-2-astra-at-real-work), and none met its bar for an employee. An arXiv paper reports that [higher-scoring coding models did not get more work done](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23216). Why it matters: both measure the distance between leaderboard gains and finished work, which is what buyers of these models are paying for.
- **Microsoft launches seven in-house models at Ignite**: the [MAI lineup](https://microsoft.ai/news/microsoft-ignite-2026) covers coding, images, voice and security. [MAI Code 2 and Code 2 Flash](https://microsoft.ai/news/introducing-mai-code-2-and-mai-code-2-flash) are already in GitHub Copilot, pitched at [about 5% of Claude's cost](https://alphasignal.ai/news/microsoft-brings-mai-code-2-to-github-copilot-at-5-of-claude-s-cost); Decision-1.1 returns structured answers for routing and grading instead of free text. Satya Nadella also made Word, Excel and PowerPoint generally available inside Copilot, which he called ["the new OS for work"](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/11/microsoft-calls-copilot-the-new-os-for-work-at-ignite-keynote/). Why it matters: Microsoft is competing on price with the model makers whose products it resells, starting with everyday coding.
- **Gemini's "temporary" chats now last two months**: Google updated its Gemini Privacy Hub to keep temporary chats, and chats made with history turned off, for [around two months instead of 72 hours](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/p47#a261117p47), TechCrunch reports. Why it matters: yesterday Google let users keep history without feeding training; today the chats users chose not to keep are held about 20 times longer.
- **Trained to restyle its reasoning, a model also hides it better**: researchers fine-tuned models to write their step-by-step reasoning in different personas, with no examples of evading a monitor, and found the reasoning [gave monitors less evidence of a hidden side task](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21799): a monitorability score fell from 0.94 to 0.68, and to 0.22 when the model was told to hide. Prompts asking for transparency recovered much of it. Why it matters: reading a model's reasoning is a main safety check, and this shows the check can weaken as a side effect of ordinary training.
- **Also**: Anthropic's [linked billing](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-lets-saas-customers-pay-for-claude-without-sharing-api-keys) beta lets customers pay for Claude inside third-party platforms such as Linear and Ramp without handing over API keys. Claude Code 2.1.323 adds an opt-in [Code Mode](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-s-claude-code-rewrites-multi-tool-workflows-in-javascript-to-cut) where Claude writes JavaScript to batch tool calls and save tokens.

## Devtools & Infra

- **AI security scanning that hands maintainers fixes**: in a pilot on six open source projects (including Caddy, Express, ImageMagick and Netty), Microsoft's scanning system flagged thousands of possible issues; after AI triage and hand review by seven researchers, maintainers received [99 private reports, each with a proof of concept and a suggested fix](https://github.blog/open-source/maintainers/maintainers-dont-need-more-ai-bug-reports-they-need-fixes/). They had fixed 55 by November 15. GitHub concedes the checking does not scale yet: it took the researchers about two days per project. Why it matters: maintainers are flooded with unverified AI findings, and this puts the cost of verification on the sender.
- **Agent sessions that outlive the window**: GitHub described the [Agent Host Protocol](https://github.blog/engineering/engineering-principles/building-the-agent-host-protocol-for-sessions-that-continue-across-devices/), an open standard built first in VS Code that lets a Copilot agent session started in one app continue in another, since an agent tied to an editor window dies when it closes. Microsoft's Copilot SDK 2.0 [rebuilds the agent runtime in Rust](https://alphasignal.ai/news/microsoft-s-copilot-sdk-2-0-runs-95x-faster-by-ditching-containers-for-rust) so sessions survive restarts and each runs with its own identity. Why it matters: agents that work for hours need to live somewhere more durable than the app that launched them.
- **Sandboxes that sit outside the agent**: Simon Willison and Prime Radiant released [three "external sandbox" servers](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/17/external-sandboxes-for-coding-agents/) (WebAssembly, Apple Containers and QEMU versions) sharing one local API: the coding agent stays outside and only the code it runs goes in. An optional proxy limits which hosts that code can reach and swaps in credentials so the agent never sees the secret. Why it matters: it makes trying a local model safer, since a mistake or a prompt injection runs inside the box.
- **Also**: Vercel's open source agent framework [eve reached 1.0](https://vercel.com/blog/eve-is-now-generally-available) after 230+ releases since its June preview. The Next.js team explained [why its security advisories are multiplying](https://nextjs.org/blog/how-were-using-ai-to-make-nextjs-more-secure): it is using AI to look for flaws.

## Security & Privacy

- **Sources: the outages came from a rush to patch**: Wall Street executives and the NSA told members of Congress that this week's failures stemmed from [systems overwhelmed by a rush to patch vulnerabilities found by AI models](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/p66#a261117p66), the Washington Sun reports. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there is ["zero evidence"](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/p70#a261117p70) the FAA, Microsoft and bank failures are connected and that the US is not under systemic attack. The FAA [paused installations of Juniper routers](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/p64#a261117p64) like the one officials believe contributed to Friday's ground stop (owner HPE says the device was not defective), and Apple became the latest to stumble, blaming an ["internal update"](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/p48#a261117p48) for an outage of some services. Why it matters: yesterday the Homeland Security secretary blamed new technology on old systems; this account is more specific, and it says the damage came from the defense, not from an attack.
- **Java4Shell: remote code execution in Java, found with an AI model**: security startup Calif disclosed [a flaw in OpenJDK's Base64 processing](https://calif.io/java4shell/) (CVE-2026-114938) that lets an attacker run code on a server without logging in; it says it [used OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber to find it](https://www.techmeme.com/261117/p55#a261117p55), per the New York Times. Oracle has released a patch. Why it matters: Java runs a large share of server software, so the fix has to reach many systems quickly, in a week when hurried patching is itself being blamed for outages.
- **Optimizely confirms the breach as the payload comes into focus**: Optimizely told [Brian Krebs](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/11/attackers-used-optimizely-to-a-b-test-malware-on-top-websites/) an attacker exploited a vulnerability in its experimentation product, since fixed, to add malicious JavaScript to customer projects. The code was rebuilt 26 times in a day; it stole card data, cookies and screenshots, swapped cryptocurrency addresses, and [served exploit kits aimed at older iPhones and iPads](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/malicious-code-delivered-through-optimizely-hits-visitors-to-hundreds-of-sites/). Only 24 affected sites had a content security policy (a browser rule limiting where scripts load from) that fully blocked it. Why it matters: yesterday's entry had the reach; this is the mechanism, and a defense most sites had not switched on.
- **Microsoft's agent-framework package compromised on PyPI**: Socket reports the official `agent-framework` Python package was [published with a credential stealer](https://socket.dev/blog/agent-framework-pypi-package-compromised-with-credential-stealer). Why it matters: it is the library developers install to build agents on Microsoft's stack, so a poisoned release lands on machines that hold cloud and API keys.
- **Quad9 pulls its DNS resolver out of France**: after a French court imposed daily fines on the Swiss nonprofit for not blocking domains tied to alleged copyright infringement, Quad9 is withdrawing its free resolver (the service that turns site names into addresses) from the country. Cloudflare [warns](https://blog.cloudflare.com/quad9-withdrawal-from-france/) that Quad9 cannot notify users, among them hospitals and government offices, whose connections will simply fail, and that a Belgian order threatens Cloudflare, Google and Cisco with penalties from early January 2027; the three have appealed. Why it matters: copyright blocking orders are now removing basic internet infrastructure from whole countries.
- **Cities and voters drop license plate cameras**: San Diego's council voted to [end its contracts with Flock and Ubicquia](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/san-diego-dumps-both-flock-cameras-and-smart-streetlights/), one of the largest cities to cut ties with Flock. The EFF counts [ballot wins against plate readers](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/11/voters-reject-alprs-and-surveillance-tech-ballot-box) in York, Maine, where nearly three-quarters of voters backed removal, and in League City, Texas. Why it matters: opposition to these cameras has moved from council comment periods to votes that cancel contracts.

## Research

- **Telling a coding agent its budget does not help**: in 5,835 runs on two program-repair benchmarks, enforcing a token cap [cut tasks solved by 20 percentage points while using about a twelfth of the tokens per solved task](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21614); disclosing the cap to the agent never improved results, in any wording. Why it matters: cost control for agents is a matter of how the limit is enforced, not of what the prompt says about it.
- **Stress-testing the EU's anonymised search data**: testers tried to re-identify 98,501 people in the search dataset Alphabet must share under the Digital Markets Act and [verified matches for 0.094%](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21689), below the study's threshold, at about three hours per match. The authors note the measures protect searchers better than the other people named in their queries. Why it matters: it is a rare empirical check on whether a regulator's anonymisation holds against someone trying to break it.
