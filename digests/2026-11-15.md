## AI / LLMs

- **Open models on ordinary hardware**: ChatR Labs published compressed builds of Alibaba's image model Qwen-Image-2.1 that [run on 4 to 14 GB of graphics memory](https://alphasignal.ai/news/chatr-labs-shrinks-alibaba-s-qwen-image-2-1-to-4-gb-for-consumer-gpus), the range of a consumer card. VieNeu-TTS v3 Turbo, a Vietnamese text-to-speech model built from scratch, does [instant voice cloning at 48 kHz](https://alphasignal.ai/news/vieneu-tts-v3-turbo-runs-vietnamese-voice-cloning-on-a-consumer-gpu) and runs in real time on a CPU. And Simon Willison's [datasette-llm 0.1a11](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/14/datasette-llm/) adds OpenAI-compatible endpoints, so the Datasette Agent can run on a model served locally by LM Studio, Ollama or llama.cpp. A build of OpenAI's speech-to-text model Whisper large-v3, packaged for transcribe.cpp, [runs on a laptop in under 1 GB without Python](https://alphasignal.ai/news/openai-s-whisper-large-v3-runs-locally-under-1gb-without-python) and gets 1.81% of words wrong on the LibriSpeech test set. Why it matters: image generation, voice cloning, transcription and a data agent each now work without a cloud account or a data center GPU.
- **Agents as coworkers in the team chat**: [agentes.chat](https://alphasignal.ai/news/agentes-chat-turns-team-chat-into-an-open-source-ai-agent-control-plane) is an open-source, self-hosted Slack clone where Claude, Codex, Gemini and Grok agents join channels as teammates, with approvals, spending budgets and MCP tools (the standard way agents connect to outside services). [gitseed](https://github.com/gitseed-dev/gitseed) takes the same self-hosted route for coding agents specifically. Why it matters: it puts the agent's permissions and costs in the place the whole team already watches, where a private terminal session hides both.
- **How Meta's Muse got built**: Mona Sarantakos, VP of Product at Meta Superintelligence Labs, gives [her first podcast interview](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/metas-muse-how-we-launched-the-most) to Lenny Rachitsky, who calls Muse the top app in the App Store. The assistant began as a weekend with the open-source agent OpenClaw on a Raspberry Pi, and Meta held it for seven months before launch even though it started ahead of most rivals. Why it matters: the product now leading consumer AI came from waiting, at a time when shipping first is treated as the whole strategy.
- **Community fine-tunes carry an open image model**: a community-trained add-on for Krea's open-weights image model Krea 2, focused on generating Asian characters, [passed one million downloads](https://alphasignal.ai/news/krea-s-open-krea-2-raw-spawns-1m-download-community-lora-in-weeks). Why it matters: once weights are public, adoption can be driven by what users build on top, and the most popular use may be one the original maker never targeted.
- **Also**: the South China Morning Post asks whether Chinese AI companies can [curb the cyber risks of open-weight models](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3374332/can-chinese-ai-companies-curb-cyber-risks-open-weight-models-without-losing-their-edge) without losing their edge, since released weights cannot be recalled or patched once downloaded. Sebastian Raschka explains [positional embeddings](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-positional-embeddings), the mechanism that tells a language model the order of its words, and [Quark](https://empero.org/writing/quark) is an open-source 165M-parameter language model built end to end.

## Devtools & Infra

- **Small releases**: Kiri pitches itself as a [fast Docker Desktop alternative](https://runkiri.com) for Mac, Windows and Linux. Willison also shipped [datasette-vega 0.7](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/14/datasette-vega/), the first release of the charting plugin since July 2018, updated for the latest Datasette alphas and for Datasette Lite, the in-browser version, which just gained the ability to run JavaScript plugins. [crate-ios](https://github.com/oneamendez/crate-ios) runs Linux virtual machines on iPhone and iPad. Why it matters: Docker Desktop is the default way to run containers on a laptop, so a faster replacement on all three platforms targets a tool most developers open daily.

## Security & Privacy

- **Job boards purge fake listings**: LinkedIn and Monster [took down more than 1,000 fake job postings](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/linkedin-monster-take-down-over-1-000-fake-job-postings-after-cbc-investigation-9.7415379) after a CBC investigation. Why it matters: the listings came down only once reporters found them, which says the platforms' own screening had let them through.
- **A pentest framework gets a government threat overview**: an advisory posted on the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center site gives a [threat overview of ARTEX](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2026/261113.pdf), an autonomous penetration testing framework, meaning software that probes networks for weaknesses without a human steering each step. Why it matters: a tool in the category built for authorized security testing is now being documented by the government as a threat in its own right.
- **Node on every laptop**: security vendor Morphisec argues that AI tools have [put the Node.js runtime on every endpoint](https://www.morphisec.com/blog/every-endpoint-now-runs-node-how-ai-tools-multiplied-an-unmanaged-runtime), unmanaged. Why it matters: a general-purpose runtime that IT never installed or inventoried can run code on machines whose defenses assume it is not there; the argument comes from a company that sells endpoint protection.

## Startups & Industry

- **Treasury tells Nvidia how to replace Huang**: Scott Bessent said Nvidia needs a "dramatic shakeup" in leadership after Huang's ouster, urged it to [hire an outside CEO](https://www.techmeme.com/261115/p8#a261115p8), and wants answers ==within 10 days== on whether the company pushed "seemingly illicit" chip sales to China to boost revenue, Bloomberg reports. Why it matters: a cabinet secretary is now setting conditions and a deadline for a private company's succession, which rules out a quiet internal handover.
- **Lutnick's chip rules and his old firm**: a New York Times investigation finds Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick [pushed to narrow chip curbs](https://www.techmeme.com/261115/p9#a261115p9) affecting a client of Cantor Fitzgerald, the firm he used to run, whose Malaysia data center may serve Tencent; he has been writing rules for Southeast Asian data centers, some of them Cantor clients. Semafor's sources expect him [on Air Force One to China](https://www.techmeme.com/261115/p11#a261115p11) with the president this week, after clashes with Bessent and others over Nvidia chip curbs. Why it matters: the official drafting the export rules is reported to have a business overlap with companies those rules touch, just as the rules themselves are under the most scrutiny.
- **A petition to bring Huang back**: Elon Musk and other tech executives [signed a petition calling on Nvidia to reinstate Jensen Huang](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/elon-musk-other-tech-execs-sign-petition-calling-on-nvidia-to-reinstate-jensen-huang). Forbes explains why he could lose the company in the first place: unlike Mark Zuckerberg, Michael Dell and Evan Spiegel, who hold majority voting power, Huang [had no super-voting shares](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p20#a261114p20). Why it matters: without those shares the decision rests with the board and ordinary shareholders, and a petition from fellow executives has no formal weight with either.
- **The scandal becomes a midterm problem**: the Washington Post writes that the Nvidia affair [ties together data centers, China and corruption](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p23#a261114p23), three issues voters already care about, and so is a political challenge for the White House. A Wall Street Journal piece is headlined on the [backdoor route Huang built](https://www.wsj.com/tech/jensen-huang-backdoor-ai-data-centers-a5e4499d) that keeps Beijing in the AI race (paywalled), and 23andMe's Anne Wojcicki tells Bloomberg the episode shows what happens when CEOs [think they are "untouchable"](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p22#a261114p22). For anyone arriving late, The Verge has [an overview of the whole affair](https://www.theverge.com/column/1033943/jensen-huang-nvidia-china-spying-scandal-resignation), starting from the October 29 New York Times report that Huang appeared to be the target of a Chinese spy plot. Why it matters: the story has moved from one company's leadership to an election issue, which gives officials a reason to keep pressing it.
- **Nvidia's pricing, questioned**: TechSpot reports an allegation that Nvidia [kept GPU prices high through circular financing deals](https://www.techspot.com/news/114558-nvidia-deliberately-kept-gpu-prices-high-through-circular.html), meaning money it put into customers came back as chip orders; it is a claim, not a finding. PCWorld adds the long view: Huang [built Nvidia on gamers](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3284206/jensen-huang-built-nvidia-on-gamers-ai-made-them-an-afterthought.html), and AI made them an afterthought. Why it matters: if the allegation holds, buyers outside those deals, gamers included, paid for it.
- **Data center tenants asked to backstop the power**: Bloomberg's source says developer Applied Digital has, since at least last year, [asked tenants such as Oracle and Meta for billions in guarantees](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p21#a261114p21) to secure electricity before leases are final. Why it matters: the financial risk of grid power is moving from the landlord to the tech companies, and it lands before they have a signed lease.
- **Stablecoins as Latin America's dollar account**: dollar-pegged stablecoins are [taking a growing share of the region's crypto activity](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p19#a261114p19), Bloomberg reports, used to store value and to send money across borders, including by people who know nothing about blockchains. Why it matters: this is crypto adopted for an ordinary need, holding and moving dollars, in places where local currency and bank transfers do both poorly.
- **Apple's first smart glasses take shape**: Bloomberg's Mark Gurman says Apple will [announce its first smart glasses at WWDC next June](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1033968/apples-first-smart-glasses-are-coming-together), with shipments starting before the end of 2027. The first model has no augmented reality display, and the prototypes, codenamed N50, are described as slightly thinner than Meta's second-generation Ray-Bans. The centerpiece is the revamped Siri AI controlling third-party iPhone apps, which developers are being asked to support on the phone first. Why it matters: Apple is using the iPhone's app catalog as the glasses' head start, an asset Meta does not have.
- **Also**: Bloomberg reports that a VC firm run by Madonna's manager holds a [$7 billion Anthropic stake](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-15/vc-firm-run-by-madonna-s-manager-has-7-billion-anthropic-stake) (paywalled).

## Elsewhere

- **SpaceX catches the ship**: the launch tower's "chopstick" arms caught the Starship upper stage at the end of Flight 15, the ==first time== the ship itself has been caught, and on a return from orbit; earlier catches were all boosters. Ars Technica's Eric Berger, at Starbase, describes the ship [falling horizontally out of the clouds](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/with-starship-catch-spacex-still-shows-it-can-dazzle-the-world/), lighting three Raptor engines to swing upright, and settling onto the arms by two nubs that each stick out about a foot. The caveat: SpaceX [aborted the planned catch of the booster](https://www.theverge.com/science/1033930/spacex-chopstick-starship-catch), so it did not recover the whole vehicle. Why it matters: full reuse needs both halves back, and each has now been caught, though not yet on the same flight.
- **The first AI Music Awards**: Variety's Corbin Bolies reports from the [first AI Music Awards in Los Angeles](https://www.techmeme.com/261115/p7#a261115p7), which featured AI announcers, lip-syncing and human stand-ins and was mired in technical issues. Why it matters: a ceremony meant to show AI music can stand on a stage still needed people to occupy it.
- **Fires during the lockdowns**: a study in Cell Reports Sustainability led by Jianghao Wang of the Chinese Academy of Sciences used the 2020 COVID lockdowns as a natural experiment and found that [less human activity meant less fire activity](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/11/how-the-worlds-fires-changed-while-humanity-hit-pause/) on average. Why it matters: the human share of wildfire is hard to separate from weather and climate, and the lockdowns gave researchers a rare period in which only the human side changed.
