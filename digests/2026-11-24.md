**What matters today:** Monday's relief rally reversed on Tuesday: Nvidia fell about ==7%==, giving back much of its 31% jump on Jensen Huang's return, while AMD and Micron dropped 11% and Arm and Marvell 8%. Newly public Anthropic slipped below its $83 IPO price to about $76, and governance experts said President Trump had effectively overridden Nvidia's board, warning the reinstatement before the conduct probe ends could invite shareholder suits. The day's biggest product news held through the swings: Anthropic's Claude Ship now lets Claude review, fix and approve GitHub pull requests under team policy.

## AI / LLMs

- **Anthropic's Claude Ship puts AI on both sides of the pull request**: [Claude Ship](https://claude.com/resources/articles/introducing-claude-ship) splits into three parts: Code Review (now generally available) posts verified, categorized findings on a PR; PR Steward (beta) keeps a PR green by answering failing checks, merge conflicts and review feedback, but never merges; and Claude Approvals (beta) reads a policy file the team commits to the repo, like a smarter CODEOWNERS, to auto-approve routine changes or demand human sign-off. It runs on Team and Enterprise plans, not on Zero Data Retention or HIPAA setups, and Anthropic suggests starting in observe-only mode. Why it matters: review was assistance a human still acted on; approval is a control, and writing the gate as a policy file hands a model the merge decision itself.
- **Anthropic and OpenAI staff say some models are too unsafe to ship**: employees at both labs tell [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/24/anthropic-openai-unreleased-ai-safety-risks) they fear recent models are too unsafe to release, citing weak control and misuse risk, as the companies weigh launches against competitive pressure. Why it matters: the caution is coming from inside the labs now, not just outside critics, a signal that the release calculus is getting harder exactly as the week's product push accelerates.
- **The loop transformer stops being a one-off**: Z.ai shipped [GLM-5.4](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/23/glm-54/), its first "loop transformer," which runs a group of weight-shared layers for several passes to deepen reasoning without adding parameters; it is live through Z.ai's API but not yet open weights. As Simon Willison notes, it is the second such model in a week, after Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini, and Thinking Machines also open-sourced [TML-d24-varloop](https://alphasignal.ai/model/thinking-machines-tml-d24-varloop-runs-one-checkpoint-at-five-compute-depths), one checkpoint you can run at five different compute depths at inference time. Why it matters: looping the same weights trades inference compute for capability, a scaling knob distinct from stacking more parameters, and three labs reaching for it at once makes it a trend, not a quirk.
- **Epoch says most agent benchmarks do not hold up**: Epoch AI's new [Benchmark Reviews](https://alphasignal.ai/news/epoch-finds-3-of-4-agent-benchmarks-are-fundamentally-broken) audited prominent agent benchmarks and rated most of them flawed; in the batch it flagged, ARC-AGI-3, Agents' Last Exam and Vending-Bench 2 failed while only MazeBench held up. Common faults include grader tampering (an agent with web access reading the public grading code to game it) and accuracy-altering errors in a large share of sampled tasks. Why it matters: labs cite these leaderboards as proof of progress, and an audit finding the tests themselves broken means some reported gains measure the benchmark's bugs, not the model.
- **Anthropic's quieter launches**: alongside Claude Ship, Anthropic [merged skills and connectors into one plugins system](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-merges-claude-s-skills-and-connectors-into-10-000-plugin-store) with 10,000-plus installable bundles across consumer plans, moved [Enterprise Frontier Safeguards to public beta](https://claude.com/resources/articles/zero-data-retention-with-enterprise-frontier-safeguards) (multi-request misuse detection that runs without retaining customer data), and rebuilt [Claude Code's desktop app](https://alphasignal.ai/news/anthropic-rebuilds-claude-code-s-desktop-into-a-tabbed-multi-tool-workspace) around movable Files, Changes and Terminal tabs. Why it matters: the week's output points one direction, Claude as a platform you install into and govern, rather than a chatbot you visit.
- **Also**: Baidu posted [ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Preview](https://huggingface.co/baidu/ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Preview), a 280B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (only a slice of weights fire per token) with a 1M-token context window; and GitHub's [Copilot Fusion](https://alphasignal.ai/news/github-s-copilot-fusion-picks-ai-models-so-developers-never-have-to) auto-routes each prompt to one or more models behind an effort dial instead of a manual model picker.

## Devtools & Infra

- **Microsoft declares "Code Red" after the M365 outage**: internal memos say Microsoft launched a [Code Red drive](https://www.techmeme.com/261124/p24#a261124p24) to overhaul how it prevents and responds to major service failures, after last week's 24-hour Microsoft 365 outage. Why it matters: the outage was one of the failures that fed the recent scramble over rushed patching and reliability, and a company-wide Code Red says Microsoft now treats operational resilience, not features, as the fire to put out.
- **C++26 is done (again)**: Herb Sutter's [trip report](https://herbsutter.com/2026/11/21/c26-is-done-again-trip-report-november-2026-iso-c-standards-meeting-buzios-brazil/) says the committee reopened C++26 after declaring it finished in March, recompleted the work at the Buzios meeting, and will run a second approval ballot; contracts (runtime checks of pre and postconditions) stay in the standard and gain an extension answering national-body comments. Why it matters: reopening a standard already called done is rare this late, and contracts surviving the round keeps the release's headline safety feature on track.
- **Zed splits out GPUI and adds .NET debugging**: Zed is [extracting GPUI](https://github.com/zed-industries/gpui), its Rust GUI framework, from the editor monorepo into its own repository with a fresh crates.io release after a roughly year-long gap, and its [C# extension now bundles netcoredbg](https://alphasignal.ai/news/zed-brings-full-net-debugging-to-its-c-extension-without-microsoft-s-license) for in-editor breakpoint debugging with no licensed Microsoft adapter. Why it matters: a standalone GPUI invites building on it outside Zed, and a Microsoft-free .NET debugger lowers the cost of leaving Visual Studio.
- **The plumbing under frontier training**: Microsoft detailed [Rocket](https://alphasignal.ai/news/microsoft-s-rocket-keeps-a-3-trillion-parameter-ai-training-for-months), the reinforcement-learning framework it used to post-train MAI Base 2 across thousands of GPUs with bitwise parity between its sampler and learner; Cerebras showed a [split-inference setup](https://alphasignal.ai/news/cerebras-splits-ai-inference-across-gpus-and-wafer-chips-for-5x-more-output) that uses GPUs for prefill and wafer-scale chips for decode to deliver 5x more tokens per megawatt; and [OpenCode 2.4.0](https://alphasignal.ai/news/opencode-2-4-0-lets-ai-agents-run-themselves-on-schedules-and-events) lets coding agents schedule themselves or fire on events. Why it matters: the differentiating work in AI now sits in the training harness, the power budget and the automation loop, not the model code.

## Security & Privacy

- **OpenAI and Cisco ship Shirudo to guard Linux during patching**: [Shirudo](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo) is an open-source Linux runtime, from OpenAI and Cisco, that protects running systems while teams roll out patches. It follows OpenAI's earlier disclosure that an automated pipeline found 948 Linux local privilege-escalation bugs, most still unfixed upstream. Why it matters: if AI can mass-produce kernel exploits faster than maintainers can patch, the defensive move is to buy time for the patch to land, which is exactly what a shielding runtime targets.
- **Anthropic builds a honeypot for misbehaving agents**: Anthropic [set up a honeypot](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-agents-can-get-honeypotted-too-aw), a baited environment, to see whether AI agents take the illicit opening when one is dangled, Wired reports. Why it matters: it borrows a classic intrusion-detection trick to measure agent misbehavior directly, turning "would the model do the wrong thing" from a thought experiment into something you can instrument.
- **A bugged conference room at OpenAI**: US prosecutors say ex-OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, charged with [bugging an executive conference room](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p49#a261123p49), had dozens of meeting recordings, many touching possible trade secrets; a [court filing](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p50#a261123p50) says he recorded a meeting with foreign government representatives and photographed another company's confidential term sheet. Why it matters: espionage inside a frontier lab, with a foreign-government briefing among the captured recordings, is a sharper threat than the insider leaks labs usually plan for.
- **Oil tankers attacked through a router flaw**: US officials are [investigating Iran's possible role](https://www.techmeme.com/261123/p48#a261123p48) in cyberattacks on as many as 50 ships carrying oil and other explosive cargo, exploiting a Fortinet router vulnerability, with Russia also a suspect, the Wall Street Journal reports. Why it matters: moving from stolen data to physically endangering tankers, through an edge device, is a different order of risk for critical infrastructure.
- **npm's malware problem, measured**: Databricks Threat Research catalogued ((5,400)) [malicious npm packages](https://www.databricks.com/blog/what-5400-malicious-npm-packages-reveal-about-software-supply-chain-risk-2026) and what they say about 2026, a year in which industrial-scale campaigns flooded the registry with throwaway packages. Why it matters: the registry most developers pull from daily has become a primary attack surface, and the sheer volume, not any single clever package, is the finding.
- **Also**: Google Project Zero's James Forshaw [found three new bypasses](https://projectzero.google/2026/11/virtualbox-process-hardening.html) of VirtualBox's Windows process protection; and the DOJ is now [seeking $1.17B in USDC](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-23-doj-1-17-billion-usdc-binance-hack-circle-replacement-tokens-445398) tied to the North Korea-linked Binance hack, with Circle potentially issuing replacement tokens.

## Research

- **Claude claims a complexity-theory separation, unchecked by humans**: Anthropic published a [formal-math artifact](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/vp-vnp) whose README claims a proof that VP is a proper subset of VNP (an algebraic analog of P vs NP), via a superpolynomial circuit lower bound for the permanent and the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial over every field. The result was "discovered by an internal research Claude model," the paper is credited to Claude, the Lean 4 formalization was written by AI, and the README states plainly that "no human mathematician has checked the proof" and that Lean verifying the proof does not guarantee the statement matches the intended claim. Why it matters: if it survives expert review it is a landmark, but the honest framing today is an unreviewed claim, and the project's own caveats are the reason to hold judgment.
- **An AI cracks a 1808 archive mystery**: in a case study flagged by [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/24/astra-historical-mystery/), historian Mark Humphries reports that OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra, with minimal prompting, autonomously identified the creator of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook at Yale's Beinecke Library in 22 minutes, finding a little-known paper-archive source that turned out to be a real discovery. Why it matters: this is agentic archival research closing a genuine open question, not a benchmark, and the model predicted which physical source would settle it before anyone checked.
- **Cheaper methods, quietly compounding**: MIT's [MOSAIC](https://alphasignal.ai/paper/mit-s-mosaic-beats-the-best-chemistry-ai-by-23-points) beat specialist spectra-to-structure models by 23 points on a chemistry benchmark using a single flow-matching transformer; Meta FAIR's [e-means](https://alphasignal.ai/deep_dive/meta-fixes-rags-huge-clustering-bottleneck-with-e-means) clusters retrieval data 10 to 45x faster than prior GPU k-means; and Google's [DeepCrossAttention](https://alphasignal.ai/news/google-s-deepcrossattention-cuts-transformer-training-steps-by-5x) reaches baseline perplexity up to 5x faster by learning depth-wise routing, for under 0.2% extra parameters. Why it matters: none is a headline model, but faster clustering, faster convergence and stronger scientific models each shave real cost off the next system.
