**What matters today:** The evening's lead is a rift between Anthropic and the US military: an official told the BBC the [Defense Department no longer uses Anthropic's tools](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c5j9x9pr0240o), though sources say Claude was in use as recently as last week. Earlier, attack-side AI led: SecurityWeek reports [AI agents aimed SQL injection at US and Canadian government sites](https://www.securityweek.com/ai-agents-aimed-sql-injection-at-us-and-canadian-government-sites). Around both, rules and money keep moving: Altman says OpenAI and Anthropic still [differ on AI regulation](https://www.techmeme.com/261004/p19#a261004p19), OpenAI is [putting visual ads in ChatGPT](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1004655/openai-chatgpt-visual-ads), and Norway wants to [ban AI glasses in public places](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/ai-glasses-face-their-first-major-government-crackdown/). Yesterday's Super Intelligence Force and California's OpenAI subpoena were the government side of agent accountability; today that pressure reaches vendors' contracts and privacy rules.

## AI / LLMs

- The BBC reports an official says the [DOD has stopped using Anthropic's tools](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c5j9x9pr0240o), with sources saying Claude was used as recently as last week, including in operations against Iran. It shows how quickly a lab's government standing can flip while the White House task force from yesterday starts its 120-day clock.
- OpenAI will [test visual ads in ChatGPT](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1004655/openai-chatgpt-visual-ads) in the US later this month, first alongside image generation: sponsored images sit apart from the output and, OpenAI says, will not influence answers. Ads started in February as text links; this is the first format that shows pictures, and Plus and Pro subscribers are exempt.
- To meet the EU AI Act, OpenAI [plans text watermarking](https://openai.com/index/eu-text-provenance) for ChatGPT and Codex users in the EU, with an opt-in for API customers worldwide starting today. Detection access starts with researchers, so the public cannot check text yet.
- Altman [tells Politico](https://www.techmeme.com/261004/p19#a261004p19) that OpenAI and Anthropic hold fundamentally different worldviews on AI regulation, arguing AI's benefits justify accepting some risks. It sets the two labs up as opposing voices.
- Google's James Manyika, in a [Bloomberg Q&A](https://www.techmeme.com/261004/p21#a261004p21), says AI risk responsibility must be shared across industry, government and society. He points to an accord with no legal weight that commits Anthropic, OpenAI, Nvidia, Meta, SpacexAI and Google to "robust internal processes": voluntary pledges, not rules.
- Axios sources say [several Western open-weight models launch this month](https://www.techmeme.com/261004/p24#a261004p24), including Reflection AI's first, meant to rival the top Chinese open-weight models. Aleph Alpha also [released Kolibri](https://the-decoder.com/aleph-alpha-releases-kolibri-an-open-weight-model-that-makes-the-case-for-european-ai-sovereignty), an open-weight model pitched on European AI sovereignty.
- Wired's [look at Meta's Muse](https://www.techmeme.com/261004/p16#a261004p16): extracted system prompts show the agent compiles "a page for every person in the user's life", with facts, history and tips for improving relationships. Millions have downloaded it, and the privacy cost is the profile kept on people who never installed it.
- Cohere's [North 2](https://www.techmeme.com/261005/p18#a261005p18) adds cross-session memory and a redesigned harness (the scaffolding around the model) to its enterprise agent platform, in cloud or on-premises. Memory that persists between sessions is the feature enterprises keep asking agents for.
- The Information describes a [gray market of token resellers in China](https://www.techmeme.com/261005/p5#a261005p5) that use accumulated identities and USDT-funded cards to obtain Claude accounts and route them through proxies, a sign of how much demand regional access limits leave unmet.

## Devtools & Infra

- Cloudflare is [building a free public certificate authority](https://linuxiac.com/cloudflare-is-building-a-free-public-ca-to-rival-lets-encrypt) (the body that issues HTTPS certificates) to rival Let's Encrypt, which would put a second large free issuer in a role one nonprofit has dominated.
- AWS opened a public preview of [Bedrock Managed Agents powered by OpenAI](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-weekly-roundup-amazon-bedrock-managed-agents-powered-by-openai-q3-service-availability-updates-kiro-workflows-and-more-october-5-2026/), agents that run inside a customer's AWS account under existing identities and permissions. It lets security teams govern OpenAI-based agents with tools they already use.
- GitHub published [ReviewBench](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/reviewbench-an-open-benchmark-for-ai-code-review/), an open benchmark for comparing AI code reviewers on what they catch, miss and flag as noise.

## Security & Privacy

- SecurityWeek: [AI agents aimed SQL injection](https://www.securityweek.com/ai-agents-aimed-sql-injection-at-us-and-canadian-government-sites) (malicious database queries typed into web forms) at US and Canadian government sites, a concrete case of automated agents probing public-sector targets. Microsoft says [AI is giving attackers a head start](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/02/ai-cybersecurity-threats-microsoft-report), the same shift from the defender's side.
- Denmark's civil registry, CPR, [reported extensive unauthorized access](https://www.cpr.dk/cpr-nyt/nyhedsarkiv/2026/okt/omfattende-uautoriseret-adgang-til-borgeres-cpr-oplysninger) to citizens' data; the Hacker News headline puts the exposure at 8.8M people. Registry numbers are used across public and private services, so leaked records are hard to make useless.
- Ben Thompson wrote that his own Mac was hacked through a [macOS flaw under active attack](https://stratechery.com/2026/apple-and-a-hackers-future/): the Dutch NCSC saw root access gained on machines with port 5900 (screen sharing) open to the internet and a Monero miner installed. Apple patched it last week, so the lesson is to patch and close that port.
- Norway will [propose a temporary ban on AI glasses](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/ai-glasses-face-their-first-major-government-crackdown/) in places such as parks, beaches, schools and kindergartens, with an expert group to draft permanent rules. It is the first major country to move, and it targets filming without people's knowledge.
- Politico: Apple and Google [pitch state bills](https://www.techmeme.com/261004/p15#a261004p15) with self-attested age checks and no private lawsuits, while Meta backs bills that make app stores, not platforms, verify ages. Who has to verify, and who can be sued, is the real fight. Meta, TikTok and X are separately [challenging Ofcom](https://www.techmeme.com/261005/p22#a261005p22) over the volume of information it demands under the UK Online Safety Act.
- Google [paused its open source bug bounty](https://www.itpro.com/software/open-source/this-pause-is-due-to-a-significant-rise-in-automated-submissions-the-vast-majority-of-which-are-not-valid-google-pauses-open-source-bug-bounty-scheme-over-ai-slop-submissions) over a surge of automated submissions, most of them invalid, and arXiv [rate-limited paper submissions](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/02/arxiv-imposes-rate-limit-on-paper-submissions-to-stem-the-ai-slop-tide/5300899) to stem AI slop. Open review systems now pay for machine-generated volume.
