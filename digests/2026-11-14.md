**What matters today:** the FAA [grounded every departing US flight](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/faa-orders-third-nationwide-ground-stop-in-its-history/) for several hours Friday evening, only the third nationwide ground stop in its history; CBS's sources trace it to an update on the private network that links the air traffic control system, and the FBI does not suspect an attack. Yesterday the story was Microsoft 365's worldwide outage; today a second piece of critical infrastructure went down, apparently on a routine change. In the Nvidia affair, Reuters reports Jensen Huang misled his own general counsel, and a leaked document shows Amazon planning to catalog nearly every product sold online so AI shopping agents keep coming to it.

## AI / LLMs

- **Amazon wants to own the product catalog**: a document seen by Reuters describes the "World Product Graph", a plan to [hold data on nearly every product sold anywhere online](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1033697/amazon-world-product-graph-ai-search), including items Amazon does not sell, partly by [crawling rivals' sites](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p37#a261113p37). The aim is to stop shoppers leaving for Reddit or Google to research, and to make Amazon's index the one AI agents rely on. Why it matters: Amazon expects agents to favor whichever site has the best data, so it is competing on the catalog itself as well as on what it stocks.
- **Claude's agent platform, fully self-hosted**: Anthropic opened a private beta of [self-hosted Claude Managed Agents](https://claude.com/resources/articles/self-hosted-claude-managed-agents), its service for running long-lived autonomous agents. Customers already could host the sandbox where an agent's code runs; now the harness, credentials and session history also run in their own cloud or on-premise, with model requests routed to Anthropic, Bedrock, Google, Microsoft Foundry or their own gateway. Why it matters: agents that fix builds and triage alerts unattended need deep access to internal systems, and some companies will only grant it to software they operate themselves.
- **Also**: Perplexity published [training, evaluation and use cases for Decider v1.3](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/perplexity-decider), DeepSeek says it now [exposes its app's features through an API](https://x.com/deepseek_ai/status/2121247896918253875), and Dwarkesh Patel argues that AI's effect on mathematics [previews what it will do to AI research](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p38#a261113p38), with models matching top human researchers possibly a couple of years away.

## Devtools & Infra

- **A nationwide ground stop, traced to an update**: the FAA stopped all US departures Friday evening and cleared domestic flights to resume at [8:35 pm EST](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/faa-orders-third-nationwide-ground-stop-in-its-history/); more than a million travelers were affected and Air Canada cancelled its remaining US flights for the night. CBS's sources say an update to the private communications network connecting the air traffic control system [made it reboot and crash](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p35#a261113p35), and Bloomberg's source says the FBI is examining a software update and [does not believe it was a cyberattack](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p33#a261113p33). ASI, maker of SMART, the AI advisory tool whose nationwide expansion the FAA announced on November 10, says the outage was [not a result of its system](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p34#a261113p34). Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed decades-old analog equipment and warned of more to come. Why it matters: one change to one network stopped a whole country's departures, and the official in charge says nothing prevents a repeat.
- **Vercel nearly doubles Token Pass credit**: the $30 per team monthly subscription now includes [$150 of tokens, up from $80](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-token-pass-now-includes-one-hundred-fifty-dollars-of-monthly-tokens), for coding agents using open-weight models through its AI Gateway, and the lineup [adds Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro](https://vercel.com/changelog/kimi-k3-1-pro-is-now-available-in-token-pass) with a 1M-token context window. Requests on Token Pass keys stop when the credit runs out, with no overage charges. Why it matters: a flat fee for five times its value in tokens is a subsidy, aimed at pulling coding agents onto cheaper open models.

## Security & Privacy

- **US drops its push against ICE-tracking tools**: free speech group FIRE says the Justice Department and Homeland Security [agreed to stop pressing Apple and Facebook](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p41#a261113p41) over the "ICE Sightings - Chicagoland" Facebook group and the Eyes Up iOS app, both of which report immigration enforcement activity. Why it matters: the government had leaned on two platforms to curb tools that report on its own agents, and it has now agreed to stop.

## Startups & Industry

- **Nvidia: what Huang told his own executives**: Reuters, citing sources and messages, reports Huang [misled several Nvidia leaders](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p36#a261113p36), including general counsel Tim Teter, about the nature of his relationship with Alice Huang. The WSJ says the board has [started its search for the next CEO](https://www.wsj.com/business/nvidia-starts-search-for-next-ceo-4fe0a873) (paywalled). Abroad, US officials told CNBC that Trump is [not expected to raise](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p40#a261113p40) the alleged Chinese intelligence operation when he meets Xi. Background reading: the Guardian traces [Megaspeed's trail from Australia to Singapore](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/13/a-baffling-trail-from-australia-to-singapore-what-is-megaspeed-the-company-implicated-in-downfall-of-nvidias-jensen-huang), and Semafor looks at the [pressure on Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/13/2026/us-commerce-secretary-in-the-spotlight-over-nvidia-scandal). Why it matters: if the company's own lawyer was misled, the question moves from one relationship to what Nvidia's disclosures and compliance reviews rested on.
- **Nvidia's money, after Huang**: VCs and founders tell TechCrunch his exit [leaves a large hole in AI startup funding](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/jensen-huangs-departure-blasts-big-hole-in-ai-startup-funding-vcs-and-founders-say). Yet Axios reports Nvidia has [agreed to buy $10B of Anthropic shares](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/13/anthropic-ipo-nvidia-jensen-huang). Why it matters: Nvidia was among the largest backers of AI startups, so founders are watching whether the checks continue without the person who wrote them.
- **The Anthropic stake that got away**: the FT calculates that Sam Bankman-Fried's $500M investment in 2021 would be worth ==about $37B== if Anthropic lists near $1.5 trillion; the stake was [given up in 2024 for about $1.3B](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p42#a261113p42). Why it matters: it is a measure of how far the valuation moved in two years, and of what selling in 2024 left on the table.
- **Cybercab orders open, with a subscription**: Tesla invited "select customers" to [order the driverless car for personal use](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p39#a261113p39) at about $30K plus a required $299 per month Autonomy Subscription to operate it; Electrek reports it works [only inside Tesla's Robotaxi zones](https://electrek.co/2026/11/13/tesla-cybercab-personal-use-orders-299-subscription-geofence). Why it matters: the car does not drive without the monthly fee or outside the mapped areas, so buyers own the hardware while Tesla keeps control of when and where it runs.
- **Deals and listings**: Moonshot AI, the Chinese lab behind Kimi, is [shutting its Cayman holding company](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p31#a261113p31) and moving investors into its mainland entity ahead of an IPO, the WSJ's sources say. Cerebras reported [record third-quarter revenue](https://investors.cerebras.ai/news-releases/news-release-details/cerebras-systems-delivers-record-revenue-third-quarter-2026) and raised its full-year outlook, and JD.com [completed its takeover of Ceconomy](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p1#a261114p1), the German electronics retailer, after EU approval, having bought about 60% for roughly $1.5B. Why it matters: a Chinese AI lab unwinding its offshore structure points to a listing at home, not in New York.

## Research

- **Smaller numbers, simulated robots**: Thinking Machines Lab wrote up [end-to-end 4-bit training](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/end-to-end-4-bit-training), and MIT's Han Lab described [training reasoning models in simulation](https://mit-han-lab.github.io/pear-blog) to operate real robots natively. On Hugging Face's daily list, [EgoX](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.16728) tackles transferring manipulation skills across robot bodies from first-person video, and another paper asks [who should remember what](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10439) when several users and several agents share a conversation. Why it matters: both lead items aim at cost, one by shrinking the numbers a model trains with, the other by moving robot training off physical hardware.

## Elsewhere

- **Starship reaches orbit, catch attempt today**: flight 15 [lifted off on schedule Friday](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/starship-flight-15-powers-to-orbit-setting-up-a-possible-catch-on-saturday/) from South Texas, the booster performed cleanly and the upper stage reached a nominal orbit, only the second time Starship has flown to low Earth orbit. That sets up a possible catch of the upper stage on Saturday. Why it matters: bringing the upper stage back intact is the missing half of full reuse.
- **GLP-1 drugs, used off script**: a CDC survey of more than 8,000 US adults estimates that [9.8% used a GLP-1 medication in 2025](https://arstechnica.com/health/2026/11/millions-in-us-use-unapproved-glp-1s-and-use-them-in-unapproved-ways/), about 27 million people, and 44% of users took it differently than prescribed by skipping doses, delaying refills or cutting the dose. More than half of those cited cost or availability, and 58% of younger adults who changed their use named cost, against 20% of those 65 and over. Why it matters: trial results assume the drug is taken as prescribed, and for almost half of real users it is not.
