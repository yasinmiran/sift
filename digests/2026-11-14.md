**What matters today:** Friday's nationwide FAA ground stop now has an official cause: Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy [blames copper-to-fiber wiring work](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p14#a261114p14) at a single remote facility, where contractor L3Harris may have installed a "faulty router", and airlines were still cancelling flights Saturday as they reset. Yesterday the story was Microsoft 365's worldwide outage, which Microsoft now says is fully over; today's is how small the fault behind a whole country's departures was. Italy's privacy regulator [fined Meta €805M](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p11#a261114p11) for allegedly accessing Android users' photos and videos without consent, and the Washington Post reports that Marco Rubio's rewrite of a CIA report [helped bring down Jensen Huang](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p10#a261114p10).

## AI / LLMs

- **Amazon wants to own the product catalog**: a document seen by Reuters describes the "World Product Graph", a plan to [hold data on nearly every product sold anywhere online](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1033697/amazon-world-product-graph-ai-search), including items Amazon does not sell, partly by [crawling rivals' sites](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p37#a261113p37). The aim is to stop shoppers leaving for Reddit or Google to research, and to make Amazon's index the one AI agents rely on. Why it matters: Amazon expects agents to favor whichever site has the best data, so it is competing on the catalog itself as well as on what it stocks.
- **Claude's agent platform, fully self-hosted**: Anthropic opened a private beta of [self-hosted Claude Managed Agents](https://claude.com/resources/articles/self-hosted-claude-managed-agents), its service for running long-lived autonomous agents. Customers already could host the sandbox where an agent's code runs; now the harness, credentials and session history also run in their own cloud or on-premise, with model requests routed to Anthropic, Bedrock, Google, Microsoft Foundry or their own gateway. Why it matters: agents that fix builds and triage alerts unattended need deep access to internal systems, and some companies will only grant it to software they operate themselves.
- **Claude joins the meeting**: [Claude Meetings](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-meetings), announced Thursday and rolling out in beta on Team and Enterprise plans, puts Claude in Google Meet calls as a visible participant that answers questions in chat, builds docs and whiteboards with the team, and leaves a page of notes and transcript in claude.ai. It saves no audio, and by default stays out of calls with outside guests. Why it matters: a note-taker that can also act through connected tools during the call competes with the meeting-notes startups, several of which Anthropic says it is building transcript handoffs for.
- **Open models lose their patron**: Nathan Lambert argues Huang's exit is a [major setback for US open models](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-source-ai-without-jensen): the funding Huang poured into the ecosystem will very likely dry up, and so will his willingness to spend political capital defending open weights against rushed regulation. His fix is a frontier open-model lab based in the US. Why it matters: open-weight labs leaned on one executive's money and protection while their business model was still unproven, and both are now in doubt.
- **Also**: Perplexity published [training, evaluation and use cases for Decider v1.3](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/perplexity-decider). Speakers at the AI Engineer Code Summit cosigned a [No AI Slop manifesto](https://latent.space/p/ainews-the-no-ai-slop-manifesto/) meant as a discussion tool for teams, n8n [open-sourced Nod](https://alphasignal.ai/news/n8n-open-sources-nod-a-local-ai-agent-that-blocks-unauthorized-actions), a local personal agent with its own sandboxed computer and browser, and Helen Toner, who voted to fire Sam Altman, tells The Verge that safety [cannot rest on any one CEO being trustworthy](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1032907/helen-toner-openai-cset-ai-safety-policy-interview).

## Devtools & Infra

- **A nationwide ground stop, traced to one facility**: Duffy now says [copper-to-fiber wiring work at a single remote location](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p14#a261114p14) caused the instability behind the order, and that contractor L3Harris may have installed a "faulty router" there; airlines kept cancelling flights Saturday while they repositioned. The FAA stopped all US departures Friday evening and cleared domestic flights to resume at [8:35 pm EST](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/faa-orders-third-nationwide-ground-stop-in-its-history/); more than a million travelers were affected and Air Canada cancelled its remaining US flights for the night. CBS's sources had said an update to the private communications network connecting the air traffic control system made it reboot and crash, and Bloomberg's source says the FBI [does not believe it was a cyberattack](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p33#a261113p33). ASI, maker of SMART, the AI advisory tool whose nationwide expansion the FAA announced on November 10, says the outage was [not a result of its system](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p34#a261113p34). On Friday Duffy had blamed decades-old analog equipment and warned of more to come. Why it matters: the work meant to replace that old equipment is what stopped a whole country's departures, and it happened at one site.
- **Microsoft 365 is back**: Microsoft says [all Microsoft 365 products are operational](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p8#a261114p8), closing the outage that began Thursday and that yesterday was only gradually recovering; Bloomberg counts governments, traders, hospitals and businesses among those disrupted. Why it matters: full recovery took roughly two days, which is the figure customers will now plan around.
- **Vercel nearly doubles Token Pass credit**: the $30 per team monthly subscription now includes [$150 of tokens, up from $80](https://vercel.com/changelog/vercel-token-pass-now-includes-one-hundred-fifty-dollars-of-monthly-tokens), for coding agents using open-weight models through its AI Gateway, and the lineup [adds Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro](https://vercel.com/changelog/kimi-k3-1-pro-is-now-available-in-token-pass) with a 1M-token context window. Requests on Token Pass keys stop when the credit runs out, with no overage charges. Why it matters: a flat fee for five times its value in tokens is a subsidy, aimed at pulling coding agents onto cheaper open models.

## Security & Privacy

- **Italy fines Meta over Android photos**: Italy's data protection agency fined Meta ==€805M== (about $887M) for allegedly [accessing Android users' photos and videos](https://www.reuters.com/world/italy-fines-meta-887-million-over-android-users-data-access-2026-11-14/) without their consent and without informing them. Why it matters: the penalty targets what an app reads off the phone once installed, a practice that is hard for users to see and that regulators rarely price this high.
- **Agent guardrails that fail open**: a researcher reports that when a pre-tool hook (the check that runs before an agent may use a tool) crashes, [CrewAI and the Claude Agent SDK run the tool anyway](https://aminrj.com/posts/agent-hooks-fail-open). A separate test fired the same destructive calls at four possible placements of an approval gate against a real Postgres database, and [only two placements stopped everything](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2121536021053718738.html). Why it matters: a safety check that lets the action through when it breaks protects nothing in exactly the cases where something has gone wrong.
- **US drops its push against ICE-tracking tools**: free speech group FIRE says the Justice Department and Homeland Security [agreed to stop pressing Apple and Facebook](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p41#a261113p41) over the "ICE Sightings - Chicagoland" Facebook group and the Eyes Up iOS app, both of which report immigration enforcement activity. Why it matters: the government had leaned on two platforms to curb tools that report on its own agents, and it has now agreed to stop.

## Startups & Industry

- **Nvidia: what Huang told his own executives**: Reuters, citing sources and messages, reports Huang [misled several Nvidia leaders](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p36#a261113p36), including general counsel Tim Teter, about the nature of his relationship with Alice Huang. The WSJ says the board has [started its search for the next CEO](https://www.wsj.com/business/nvidia-starts-search-for-next-ceo-4fe0a873) (paywalled). Abroad, US officials told CNBC that Trump is [not expected to raise](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p40#a261113p40) the alleged Chinese intelligence operation when he meets Xi. Why it matters: if the company's own lawyer was misled, the question moves from one relationship to what Nvidia's disclosures and compliance reviews rested on.
- **How Washington turned on Huang**: the Washington Post's source says Rubio took a CIA report about Alice Huang and [wrote his own version in more assertive terms](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p10#a261114p10), an intervention that gave momentum to officials pushing tougher China restrictions. Axios reports [MAGA figures turning on Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/14/lutnick-commerce-china), and the FT's sources say that despite 20 years of business in China, Huang [struggled to reach the policymakers](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p7#a261114p7) driving Beijing's chip self-sufficiency push. Why it matters: the account shifts from what Huang did to who assembled the case against him, and the next target appears to be the Commerce Secretary.
- **Nvidia's money, after Huang**: VCs and founders tell TechCrunch his exit [leaves a large hole in AI startup funding](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/jensen-huangs-departure-blasts-big-hole-in-ai-startup-funding-vcs-and-founders-say). Yet Axios reports Nvidia has [agreed to buy $10B of Anthropic shares](https://www.axios.com/2026/11/13/anthropic-ipo-nvidia-jensen-huang). Why it matters: Nvidia was among the largest backers of AI startups, so founders are watching whether the checks continue without the person who wrote them.
- **The Anthropic stake that got away**: the FT calculates that Sam Bankman-Fried's $500M investment in 2021 would be worth ==about $37B== if Anthropic lists near $1.5 trillion; the stake was [given up in 2024 for about $1.3B](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p42#a261113p42). Why it matters: it is a measure of how far the valuation moved in two years, and of what selling in 2024 left on the table.
- **Cybercab orders open, with a subscription**: Tesla invited "select customers" to [order the driverless car for personal use](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p39#a261113p39) at about $30K plus a required $299 per month Autonomy Subscription to operate it; Electrek reports it works [only inside Tesla's Robotaxi zones](https://electrek.co/2026/11/13/tesla-cybercab-personal-use-orders-299-subscription-geofence). Tesla has since [posted the subscription agreement](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p12#a261114p12), which Electrek reads as harsher than the order page suggested, and The Verge notes the steering-wheel-free two-seater [can only be bought in supported service areas](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1033844/tesla-personal-cybercab-orders-299-subscription-fine-print). Why it matters: the car does not drive without the monthly fee or outside the mapped areas, so buyers own the hardware while Tesla keeps control of when and where it runs.
- **Deals and listings**: Moonshot AI, the Chinese lab behind Kimi, is [shutting its Cayman holding company](https://www.techmeme.com/261113/p31#a261113p31) and moving investors into its mainland entity ahead of an IPO, the WSJ's sources say. Cerebras reported [record third-quarter revenue](https://investors.cerebras.ai/news-releases/news-release-details/cerebras-systems-delivers-record-revenue-third-quarter-2026) and raised its full-year outlook. Why it matters: a Chinese AI lab unwinding its offshore structure points to a listing at home, not in New York.
- **Trump Media's bitcoin bill comes due**: the Truth Social owner faces a [November 30 deadline to repay up to $1B](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p13#a261114p13) in convertible bonds it sold last May to fund a bitcoin treasury, the FT reports, with its shares down more than 60% this year. Why it matters: with both the stock and crypto prices down, the company may have to sell assets or borrow at a high cost to pay for a bet made at the top.
- **Funding**: Cambridge's Tendrils raised [$30M led by Benchmark](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p2#a261114p2) for a CPU architecture built around thousands of parallel cores; Luminal, an inference cloud and compiler for running models across different chips, raised a [$25M Series A](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p3#a261114p3); and WhiteMatter raised [$20M](https://www.techmeme.com/261114/p5#a261114p5) for "guardian" agents that supervise other agents' file access and actions. Why it matters: two of the three are bets on chips and compilers that are not Nvidia's, in the week its founder left.

## Elsewhere

- **Starship reaches orbit, catch attempt next**: flight 15 [lifted off on schedule Friday](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/starship-flight-15-powers-to-orbit-setting-up-a-possible-catch-on-saturday/) from South Texas, the booster performed cleanly and the upper stage reached a nominal orbit, only the second time Starship has flown to low Earth orbit. That sets up a possible catch of the upper stage on Saturday. Why it matters: bringing the upper stage back intact is the missing half of full reuse.
- **Ice older than its star**: a Science study using the James Webb Space Telescope found [water and carbon dioxide ice in the disk around Beta Pictoris](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/11/james-webb-telescope-finds-ice-older-than-the-star-it-orbits/), a star about 20 million years old and 64 light-years away. The water ice is glassy, not crystalline, and mixed with carbon dioxide in roughly the proportions seen in our own comets. Why it matters: it suggests the ingredients for water-rich planets are common around young stars.
- **GLP-1 drugs, used off script**: a CDC survey of more than 8,000 US adults estimates that [9.8% used a GLP-1 medication in 2025](https://arstechnica.com/health/2026/11/millions-in-us-use-unapproved-glp-1s-and-use-them-in-unapproved-ways/), about 27 million people, and 44% of users took it differently than prescribed by skipping doses, delaying refills or cutting the dose. More than half of those cited cost or availability, and 58% of younger adults who changed their use named cost, against 20% of those 65 and over. Why it matters: trial results assume the drug is taken as prescribed, and for almost half of real users it is not.
